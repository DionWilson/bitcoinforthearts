=== EPISODE NOTES (paste into Episode Notes, under 4000 characters) ===

London designer, maker, and engineer Thomas Forsyth works across more materials than most makers touch in a career: wood, metal, ceramic, plastic, paper, electronics, 3D printing, laser cutting, and CNC. His work has been made for the Saatchi Gallery, Glastonbury Festival, Nissan, Honda, Greenpeace, Cadbury, Lush, Ogilvy, and the Wall Street Journal, plus a life-size paper K-2SO with a moving head for Silicon Valley Comic Con.

In this episode, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson sits down with Tom to talk about why he stopped trying to put a label on what he does, and let the work people needed decide it for him. He calls himself a general handychap, and he means it: the gallery commission and the shelf on someone's wall both count.

Tom tells the story of a Glastonbury build where everything that could go wrong did, and what sixteen-hour days in the mud taught him about working smart instead of long. He explains the secret he and paper artist Owen Gildersleeve kept from the Comic Con organizers until the reveal, and why an art event brief is a different kind of creative problem than a brand job.

Then the rabbit hole. Bitcoin showed up three times before it clicked: a bar in Finsbury Park, a quiet January spent teaching himself about money, and years of watching it do what people said it would do. He describes his first Lightning payment in a family restaurant in South London, and why Nostr is the first social media he has ever enjoyed. "Your thoughts became zaps, and those zaps became food in your belly."

He also gives a maker's view on AI, from someone who watched 3D printing go from hype to useful tool, and on the link between sound money and sound craft: reality tests your work, and it humbles you.
