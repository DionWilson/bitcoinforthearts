## Why the message is structured this way

- **"This is brilliant" up front** — Beth volunteered for substantial work. Lead with appreciation, not logistics.
- **"What BFTA covers" before "what I'm asking from you"** — establishes that this isn't an ask of free labor; it's a partnership. Compensation framing comes first, contribution second.
- **Specific dollar caps** — $300 booth + $500 travel — gives Beth real numbers to plan around. Vague promises ("we'll cover what we can") would put her in an uncomfortable position of having to ask.
- **"Two things you do NOT need to do"** — protects Beth from over-functioning at the event, which would burn her out and reduce conversion quality. Same principle as the Avi/Kyle Vegas runner.
- **"A genuine thank you" at the end, not the start** — closing on appreciation lands harder than opening with it. Doesn't read as throat-clearing.
- **Concrete next steps + offer to advance prepay** — makes it easy for Beth to say yes to traveling without putting her own card on the line if money's tight
