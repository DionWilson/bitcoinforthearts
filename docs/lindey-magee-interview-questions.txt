Share Your Bitcoin Journey: Lindey Magee, Episode 10 — Interview Questions


Format note (Dion only): Two beats per question. One short premise plus
one short question. Roughly 30 to 40 words total. Anything that doesn't
fit the premise becomes a follow-up after she answers. NO em-dashes
throughout, per the recent newsletter feedback.

This conversation has three distinct emotional beats: the loss of
Chuck, the children's book mission, and the Winnebago year. Give each
its own space; do not rush past any of them. Let her tell as much or
as little as she wants on the Chuck section.


Background research (for your prep, not the show):

- Lindey Magee, Mississippi. Widowed mother of two: Bennett (24-25) and
  Palin (16-17). Her late husband Chuck is the pivot of her Bitcoin
  journey, which she has discussed openly on other podcasts.
- Bennett, age 12, advised her to buy Bitcoin in 2012. She did not.
  She calls this her biggest financial regret. Bennett is now in his
  mid-twenties; the family runs across two generations of Bitcoin.
- Currently displaced to a Winnebago with her daughter Palin and three
  dogs due to mold toxicity in the family home. Pursuing legal
  consequences against the HVAC company that caused it.
- "Lives on a BTC standard." Strike customer, loyal Jack Mallers Show
  viewer, on Nostr at @lindeymagee.
- Christian faith is explicit and central. Quoted: "Jesus is king, so
  I won't say Bitcoin saved me, but the Lord has certainly allowed it
  to bless me hugely."
- Self-described "accidental author." The book emerged while she was
  trying to validate her own Bitcoin conviction.

The book itself: Bitcoin: A Treasure to HODL
- Rhyming children's picture book with "Seuss-meets-Sats" illustrations.
- $21 price point. Includes a Did You Know section and a glossary.
- Debuted at Bitcoin 2025 Las Vegas.
- Endorsed by Vijay Boyapati (The Bullish Case for Bitcoin), Del
  Bigtree (HighWire), Matthew Lysiak (Fiat Food), Joel Bomgar, and
  Efrat Fenigson.
- Multiple SKUs: single, 3-pack ("Orange Pill"), 12-pack ("Donate a
  Dozen"), and a Satoshi School Pack.
- "Spent generational wealth" bringing it to life.
- Goals: distribution into Barnes & Noble, into schools next year.

Website: lindeymagee.com. Nostr/X handle: @lindeymagee.


HOST INTRO (spoken on camera before Q1)

Same canonical structure as Episode 6 (Joe Martin) and Episode 8
(James Kintore). Constant opener, then guest framing, then a
one-paragraph "in this conversation" tease, then the handoff.

  Welcome to another episode of Share Your Bitcoin Journey presented
  by Bitcoin for the Arts. I am Dion Wilson, the founder and executive
  director for Bitcoin for the Arts, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit empowering
  artists with Bitcoin grants, education, and sovereign funding tools.

  Today we have the pleasure of speaking with a Mississippi-based
  author, mother, and homeschooling advocate whose first book has
  reached families across the country. Known for pairing Seuss-style
  rhyming storytelling with one of the hardest topics in the world to
  explain, and for living the Bitcoin standard through some of the
  most difficult years of her life with grace and faith.

  Her book, Bitcoin: A Treasure to HODL, debuted at Bitcoin 2025 Las
  Vegas and has been endorsed by Vijay Boyapati, Del Bigtree, Matthew
  Lysiak, Joel Bomgar, and Efrat Fenigson. After losing her husband
  Chuck, she rebuilt her family's life on a Bitcoin standard. Today
  she is working on getting her book into Barnes & Noble and into
  schools, while raising her daughter and recovering her family's
  health from mold toxicity, currently from a Winnebago with three
  dogs.

  In this conversation, she shares her personal journey from grieving
  widow to author to Bitcoin advocate, how Bitcoin gave her time to
  heal, and why she believes the most important Bitcoin work
  happening right now is in classrooms, libraries, and family
  conversations. Her story is heartfelt, honest, and full of
  practical wisdom for any parent or family who has ever wondered
  how to start the Bitcoin conversation with the people they love
  most.

  Without further ado, welcome to the show, Lindey Magee.


BACKGROUND & JOURNEY

1. Lindey, welcome to Share Your Bitcoin Journey. Before the book,
before the Winnebago, before any of the Bitcoin work, there was a
Mississippi mom raising two kids. Where does the story actually
start for you?

2. You homeschool your kids. That's a particular kind of choice that
says something about how you see education and family. What put you
on that path, and how has it shaped how you think about teaching
anything to children, including Bitcoin?

3. Your son Bennett, when he was twelve years old, told you to buy
Bitcoin in 2012. Take us into that moment. What did he say, and
what did you do with it at the time?

4. We have to talk about Chuck. Losing him is the moment your story
pivots. Take us in only as far as you want to take us, but the
audience needs to understand the weight of what that loss meant
for the family and what it asked of you.

5. After Chuck, you found Bitcoin in a different way. Not as
Bennett's twelve-year-old advice, but as something you needed to
validate for yourself. When did Bitcoin go from idea to lifeline?


THE BITCOIN TURN

6. You've described Bitcoin as something that gave you "time." Time
to heal, time to raise your daughter, time to be home. Walk us
through what that actually looks like, day to day, in the life of
a Mississippi widow living on a Bitcoin standard.

7. You're a Strike customer, a Jack Mallers Show viewer, and on
Nostr. You did the work to actually live this, not just hold it.
What was the hardest part of going from "I own Bitcoin" to "my
family runs on Bitcoin"?

8. Your faith is part of this story. You've said Jesus is king, and
that Bitcoin is what the Lord allowed to bless you. Talk to us about
how those two convictions sit together for you.


BITCOIN: A TREASURE TO HODL — THE BOOK

9. You call yourself an "accidental author." That's a beautiful
framing. The book started not as a publishing project but as
something you were writing to understand Bitcoin yourself. Walk us
through how it actually came together.

10. The illustrations have been described as Seuss meets sats. That's
a real creative choice. Why rhyming, why bright, why Seuss energy
for one of the most serious topics of our time?

11. There's a Did You Know section and a glossary at the back. Adults
who read the book to their kids end up understanding Bitcoin
themselves, sometimes for the first time. Was that intentional from
the start, or did you discover it along the way?

12. You've said you spent generational wealth bringing this book to
life. That's a serious sentence. What did that investment look like,
and at what point did you know you had to keep going?

13. You've shared that adult readers hit "eureka moments" reading
the book to their kids. Walk us through one. A specific parent, a
specific child, the moment Bitcoin clicked. What did they say?


THE WINNEBAGO YEAR

14. You and your daughter are currently living in a Winnebago with
three dogs because of mold toxicity in your house. That's not a
sentence most authors say in interviews. How are you actually doing,
and what does daily life look like right now?

15. Pursuing the people who caused this is part of your current work
too. You haven't let it crush you. Where does that resilience come
from, and what would you tell another family in the middle of
something similar?

16. Bitcoin has been the financial backbone that lets you do all of
this without panicking about the next paycheck. If you had not been
on a Bitcoin standard before all of this happened, what does this
season of your life look like differently?


THE WORLDVIEW

17. Bennett, your son, is in his mid-twenties now. The kid who told
his mom to buy Bitcoin in 2012 has grown up. What does the family
Bitcoin standard look like across two generations, and what has he
taught you that you wouldn't have learned otherwise?

18. Parents on a Bitcoin standard end up teaching their kids things
most kids don't grow up with. Low time preference, sound money,
valuing time over things, skin in the game. What have your kids
learned from living this that you didn't grow up with?

19. Your mission is clear. You want this book in Barnes & Noble. You
want it in classrooms. You want children to understand sound money
before they have to live with broken money. What's the hardest part
of getting from "I wrote a book" to "this book is in every American
family's hands"?


LOOKING FORWARD

20. We're going to read your book to a room of children at the
Midwest Bitcoin Summit in September. That's a real moment for any
author. What do you most want the kids in that room to walk away
with?


THE CLOSING QUESTION

21. Last question. We ask every guest this on Share Your Bitcoin
Journey. If a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from Bitcoin for
the Arts landed in your wallet tomorrow, what would it unlock for
you as an author and as a mother?

NOTES ON ORDER

  - Q1-5 are the biographical opening and pivot. Q4 is the heaviest.
    Hold space. If she takes ten minutes on the Chuck question, let
    her. The listener will follow.

  - Q6-8 are the Bitcoin turn and the faith conversation. Q8 is
    where her faith comes onto the table directly. Don't rush past
    it; some of your audience needs to hear that Bitcoin and faith
    can coexist for an articulate person. Other listeners will not
    share the faith but will respect that you let her name it.

  - Q9-13 are the heart of the episode. The book is the tangible
    artifact she has built and the thing that travels best in the
    audience after the episode airs. Q12 (generational wealth
    invested) is the line most likely to produce a quotable
    moment. Q13 (a specific reader's eureka moment) gives Lindey
    a story to tell about real impact on real families.

  - Q14-16 are the Winnebago year. Visceral, visual, currently
    happening. Q16 (the counterfactual: what does this season look
    like without Bitcoin) is the line most likely to convert a
    Bitcoin-curious listener into a Bitcoin-conviction listener.
    Don't rush.

  - Q17-19 are the worldview pivot. Q17 (Bennett, intergenerational
    Bitcoin) is uniquely her story. Q18 (what kids learn from
    living on a Bitcoin standard) bridges her parenting to the
    book's mission and gets at the values layer beyond the
    mechanics. Q19 (the distribution question) is the practical
    mission ask.

  - Q20 sets up the Midwest Summit collaboration in real time on
    air. The audience hears that the book is going to be read to
    children at the conference and feels invited into that moment.

  - Q21 is sacred. Every episode ends with it.
