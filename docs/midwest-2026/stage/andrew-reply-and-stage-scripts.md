## 4. Day 1 script — Dion open (~5 minutes)

Speak at conversation pace. Cut lines in brackets if short on time.

---

Hello Columbus. My name is Dion Wilson. I am the founder and executive director of Bitcoin for the Arts.

Before Bitcoin for the Arts, I spent sixteen years as a professional dancer. Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater. Dance Theatre of Harlem. Philadanco. Disney’s *The Lion King* on Broadway and on the road. Twenty-five years in this industry taught me something simple and hard: artists are trained to make the work, and then left alone to figure out how the work gets paid for.

I started Bitcoin for the Arts because the funding rails for culture are broken. Grants that move slowly. Platforms that take the upside. Gatekeepers who decide which stories are safe. Bitcoin gives artists a different path: peer to peer value, Lightning tips in the room, micro-grants that clear without a six-month committee, and an audience that can support the work the moment it moves them.

That is why it matters that we are here. Midwest Bitcoin Summit is not only charts and keynotes. For the first time at this event, culture has a home on the Expo floor. We call it **Bitcoin Arts Park**.

Here is what we brought you.

On this stage: live performance, starting today with a voice that already proved value-for-value can work for a working musician.

In our footprint: a **film cinema** with Bitcoin documentaries and trailers, including Dirty Coin, Finding Home, Hummingbird, Bigger Than Bitcoin, Till My Last Breath, and a teaser conversation around One Poop Scoop Away. Filmmakers and creators can meet us at the booth, including IndeeHub as a value-for-value resource.

A **gallery wall** with a peer-to-peer Lightning silent auction and work you can take home.

A **Living Room** where we help people get a wallet in their hand and practice sending sats the way artists actually get paid.

Merchandise, donations, and a team that will talk to you like humans, not like a pitch deck.

We are not here to decorate Bitcoin. We are here to prove that sound money can fund a living culture. If Bitcoin is going to last, it needs more than price. It needs songs, films, paintings, theater, and the people who make them. That is how you get a renaissance: artists paid fairly, audiences close to the work, and rails nobody can freeze because the art got too honest.

While I have been talking, our crew has been finishing this stage. Thank you Steven, Julie, and James.

## 7. Panel prep — *Does Bitcoin Need Art to Last?* (Thu 1:50–2:20)

**Role:** Panelist. Moderator: Kenneth Burris. Co-panelists: Dr. Michael J. Kelly, Kyle Knight, Ainsley Costello.

**Audience takeaway (from Andrew):** Examine whether art and performance can sustain Bitcoin culture beyond price cycles and technical communities.

**Before session:** Bring one work/performance example and audience response. Distinguish personal interpretation from claims about all Bitcoin users. Email Kenneth any topic tweaks.

### Your lead: CORE 1 (02:00–07:00)

**Q:** What can art communicate about Bitcoin that a technical explanation or financial argument cannot?

**Talking points (≤90 sec):**
- Charts explain scarcity. Art explains *why a human would care enough to change their life.*
- Example: Ainsley’s room tipping in sats, or a film like *Dirty Coin* making mining feel like community and energy rather than a white paper abstract.
- Art carries memory across cycles. Price forgets. Culture remembers.

### Your second voice: CORE 4 (17:00–22:00)

**Q:** What would make Bitcoin-inspired art meaningful to someone with no financial stake?

**Talking points (≤90 sec):**
- If the work only flatters holders, it is marketing. If it tells a human truth (sovereignty, labor, exile, joy, craft), non-holders can still feel it.
- Example: a dancer’s body, a song about work, a painting of light. Bitcoin can be the rail underneath without being the only subject on the surface.
- Invite people into the feeling first; the wallet can come second.

### Closing round (≤30 sec)

Hope they leave knowing: Bitcoin without culture is a tool. Bitcoin with artists paid fairly is a civilization project. That is what Bitcoin Arts Park is testing in Columbus.

### Concrete examples to have ready
1. Ainsley / value-for-value live (audience tips, career on rails not platform rent).  
2. Film cinema slate at the Park (especially *Dirty Coin* or *Finding Home*).  
3. Silent auction / Lightning as patronage in public.  
4. Your own path: dancer → funding gap → BFTA as 501(c)(3).

### Reserve if asked
- Support beyond “donate exposure”: pay stipends, tip in the room, buy work, grant in Bitcoin, hire artists for conferences.  
- Creative tension: belonging vs honesty; BFTA prefers independence over a single approved “Bitcoin message.”
