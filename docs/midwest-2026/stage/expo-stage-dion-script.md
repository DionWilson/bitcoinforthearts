# Main opening speech

## 0:00-0:45 | The human problem

Good afternoon, Columbus.

Before I tell you what Bitcoin for the Arts is, I want to tell you what we are trying to prevent.

We are trying to prevent the moment when an artist who has trained for half a lifetime sits at a kitchen table, looks at the rent, the medical bill, the travel cost, and the next unpaid rehearsal, and decides that the work must wait.

Not because the talent disappeared.

Not because the discipline failed.

Because the money ran out before the idea did.

[Pause.]

I know that arithmetic.

## 0:45-1:45 | Who I am

My name is Dion Wilson. I am the founder and executive director of Bitcoin for the Arts.

I spent twenty-five years in the performing arts, including sixteen years as a professional dancer with Alvin Ailey American Dance Theater, Dance Theatre of Harlem, Philadanco, and Disney's *The Lion King* on Broadway.

I have known extraordinary stages. I have also known the calculation behind the curtain: How long is this contract? What happens if the body gets hurt? How much work can an artist give away before there is no artist left to give it?

The starving-artist story is not romantic. Hunger does not make the work more honest. Instability does not make the artist more gifted. Too often, it simply removes gifted people from the field.

## 1:45-3:10 | The contradiction in the numbers

Art is not a tiny economy.

The United States Bureau of Economic Analysis found that arts and cultural production contributed $1.17 trillion to the American economy in 2023 and supported 5.4 million jobs.

That is not decoration. That is an industry.

But the amount culture creates is not the same as the amount creators keep.

In a 2024 national survey of Americans with a dedicated artistic practice, 37 percent had worked two or more jobs. Forty-six percent had done artistic labor without financial or material return, even though they wanted to be paid.

Think about that contradiction.

A trillion-dollar cultural economy rests partly on people routinely asked to donate the labor that makes it possible.

And this is not only an American problem. UNESCO's 2025 global report found that just 9 percent of surveyed artists and cultural professionals believed their economic and social rights were adequately or strongly protected.

The art is global. The insecurity is global. Our response must be global too.

## 3:10-4:35 | What Bitcoin for the Arts does

That is why we built Bitcoin for the Arts.

We are a 501(c)(3) nonprofit supporting working artists through Bitcoin micro-grants, education, and tools for direct patronage.

Bitcoin does not replace fair contracts, healthcare, unions, public arts funding, or responsible institutions. One technology cannot solve every failure around an artist.

What Bitcoin can do is specific and powerful.

It lets a patron support an artist across a border without waiting for banking hours. Lightning makes small acts of patronage practical. Self-custody gives an artist direct control. Bitcoin's fixed supply offers a long-term savings tool that does not require a committee's permission.

Our job is to build the bridge between that technology and the people who create culture.

Our published allocation target is clear: 55 percent for artist grants, 30 percent for programs and education, 10 percent for operations, and 5 percent for a long-term Bitcoin reserve called the HODL Vault.

This is not charity that asks artists to become smaller. It is patronage that gives them room to become fully themselves.

## 4:35-6:30 | Why Columbus

Columbus is a fitting city in which to make this argument.

This region did not build its stability around one industry. It has 18 Fortune 1000 headquarters, including five Fortune 500 companies, across healthcare, insurance, finance, energy, technology, retail, manufacturing, and construction.

But the number of headquarters is not the most important part of the story. The beginnings are.

One hundred years ago, Ohio farmers pooled $10,000 because rural drivers needed fairer insurance. Their cooperative became Nationwide.

In 1955, John McConnell borrowed $600 against his Oldsmobile to buy his first load of steel. That became Worthington Industries. Its steel successor reported more than $3 billion in fiscal 2025 sales and manufacturing across six countries.

Dave Thomas was adopted as an infant. He lost his adoptive mother at five, moved repeatedly, and left high school to work full time. In 1969, he opened the first Wendy's in Columbus. By the end of 2025, the system had more than 7,300 restaurants.

These stories do not prove that every founder began with nothing. They prove something more useful.

Columbus knows how modest beginnings, pooled capital, and patient work become durable institutions.

## 6:30-7:30 | Place BFTA inside that tradition

Bitcoin for the Arts is at a modest beginning.

We do not pretend that a 30-foot expo footprint is already a world institution.

We are saying that every durable institution had a first room.

A first policy.

A first load of steel.

A first restaurant.

A first person who believed before the scale made belief easy.

Bitcoin Arts Park is our first room in public.

Inside are live music, Columbus theater, independent film, visual art, a peer-to-peer silent auction, wallet education, and working creators you can meet.

This is what a new patronage system looks like before it becomes obvious.

## 7:30-8:40 | The ask

The artists here have already made a gift: rehearsal hours, equipment, travel, and the part of themselves that cannot be replaced by an algorithm.

Many showed up with little or no funding from us because they believed this room should exist.

Please do not let their belief be the last contribution.

Applause is gratitude. Patronage is gratitude with resources attached.

Visit Bitcoin Arts Park. Meet the artists. Bid. Zap a musician. Make a one-time or recurring donation. Sponsor a program. Make an introduction before you leave Columbus.

Give at the level that is honest for you. Then tell one other person why you gave.

We are not asking this room to rescue artists.

We are asking this room to recognize them as builders and fund them accordingly.

## 8:40-9:05 | Close

A civilization is not remembered only for what it financed.

It is remembered for what it sang, painted, filmed, wrote, danced, and dared to imagine.

Culture is not a side stage. Culture is how a civilization remembers what it values.

Today in Columbus, we are putting culture on a Bitcoin standard in public.

Help us build the institution that makes that standard last.

[Pause. Let the final sentence land. Then use the correct handoff below.]

# Fact check and source notes

These notes are for preparation, press follow-up, and slide footnotes. Do not read the citations aloud.

## Artist economy

1. **$1.17 trillion, 4.2 percent of U.S. GDP, and 5.4 million jobs in 2023.** U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis, "Arts and Cultural Production Satellite Account, U.S. and States, 2023," released April 2, 2025.
   https://www.bea.gov/news/2025/arts-and-cultural-production-satellite-account-us-and-states-2023

2. **37 percent worked two or more jobs; 46 percent performed artistic labor without financial or material return but wanted compensation.** NORC at the University of Chicago, *National Survey of Artists: Findings Report*, survey fielded September-November 2024 and published in 2025. The survey covers people with a dedicated, professionalized artistic practice, including people whose primary occupation is outside the arts. The primary-job median and multiple-job findings should not be confused with NEA occupational statistics.
   https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2025/National%20Survey%20of%20Artists%20-%20Findings%20Report.pdf

3. **Only 9 percent reported adequate or strong protection of economic and social rights.** UNESCO, *Culture: The Missing SDG*, 2025 Global Report on Cultural Policies. This is a survey-based global indicator about artists and cultural professionals, not a measured global poverty rate.
   https://www.unesco.org/en/culture/global-report

4. **Useful backup statistic, not included in the main speech:** In 2024, 23 percent of Americans whose primary occupation was an artist occupation worked part time, compared with 16 percent of the total labor force. Nine percent held a second job, compared with 5 percent overall. National Endowment for the Arts using Current Population Survey data.
   https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/a5-report-202509.pdf

## Columbus and Central Ohio

5. **18 Fortune 1000 headquarters, including five Fortune 500 headquarters.** The Columbus Region, June 2026. The 18 headquarters employ more than 36,000 people in the metro and span healthcare, financial services, insurance, energy, technology, retail, advanced manufacturing, construction, and life sciences. The Columbus Region is the area's economic development organization, so attribute the count when using it in formal materials.
   https://columbusregion.com/press-release/18-fortune-1000-headquarters/

6. **Nationwide origin and scale.** Ohio farmers pooled $10,000 to form Farm Bureau Mutual Automobile Insurance Company. The first policy was sold April 14, 1926. Nationwide's 2025 annual report lists $73.2 billion in sales and premiums and $359.8 billion in assets.
   https://news.nationwide.com/centennial/
   https://www.nationwide.com/cps/annual-report/index.html

7. **John McConnell and Worthington.** In 1955, McConnell borrowed $600 against his 1952 Oldsmobile to buy his first load of steel and establish Worthington Industries in Columbus. Worthington Steel's fiscal 2025 Form 10-K reports $3.093 billion in net sales and manufacturing operations in the United States, Canada, China, India, Germany, and Mexico.
   https://www.worthingtonsteel.com/company/who-we-are/history
   https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1968487/000095017025099742/ws-20250531.htm

8. **Dave Thomas and Wendy's.** Wendy's first-party history documents Thomas's adoption, the death of his adoptive mother when he was five, repeated childhood moves, leaving high school for full-time work, and the first Wendy's opening in Columbus on November 15, 1969. Wendy's fiscal 2025 Form 10-K reports 7,397 restaurants in the United States and 38 foreign countries and U.S. territories as of December 28, 2025. Do not use the disputed claim that Thomas completed only eighth grade.
   https://www.wendys.com/daves-legacy-early-years
   https://www.wendys.com/who-we-are-daves-legacy-founding-wendys/
   https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/30697/000003069726000009/wen-20251228.htm

## Claims intentionally excluded

- "World's first 501(c)(3) paying artists in Bitcoin." The repository repeats this claim but contains no comparative proof.
- "Most-screened Bitcoin documentary in the world." Not needed for this speech and not independently substantiated here.
- Any promised attendance total. Repository proposals conflict between 3,000 and 7,000.
- "Every artist is paid in Bitcoin." August newsletters state that many Midwest artists appeared with little or no BFTA funding.
- Guaranteed appreciation or preservation of purchasing power. Bitcoin is volatile and no future financial return should be promised.
- A claim that Columbus is uniquely the only major U.S. city with a diverse economy. The city's economic diversity is supportable; unique national superiority is not.
