# Ohio Chapter 1716 note — NY org, no Ohio “chapter”

**Important clarification:** “Chapter 1716” is **Ohio’s Charitable Organizations Act** (a statute). It is **not** a local chapter of Bitcoin for the Arts. BFTA being a **New York** 501(c)(3) does **not** by itself exempt us from Ohio solicitation rules when we ask for money in Ohio.

**Current fact (Dion, Aug 2026):** BFTA does **not** currently have an Ohio charitable solicitation registration on file.

---

## Does a New York nonprofit need it?

Under **ORC 1716.02**, a charitable organization that intends to **solicit contributions in Ohio by any means** (or have contributions solicited in Ohio on its behalf) generally must **register with the Ohio Attorney General before soliciting**, then renew annually, unless an exemption in **ORC 1716.03** applies.

Out-of-state / foreign charities are covered the same way when they solicit in Ohio. Physical HQ in New York is not a free pass.

For Midwest 2026, activities that likely count as soliciting in Ohio include:

- Selling **raffle tickets** at the Summit in Columbus
- Asking for **donations / sponsorships** on site in Ohio
- Possibly broader online asks that Ohio residents can respond to (separate analysis)

The AG’s raffle reply addressed **gaming / Chapter 2915**. It did **not** replace **Chapter 1716 registration**.

---

## Possible exemptions (do not assume)

ORC 1716.03 lists exemptions (religious, certain schools, member-only asks, etc.). One commonly discussed path is **1716.03(G)**: organizations under a **$25,000** gross-revenue threshold (with conditions, excluding certain government/501(c)(3) grants).  

**Do not self-declare an exemption without checking the statute and, ideally, confirming with Charitable Registration.** If BFTA’s gross revenue exceeds the threshold, registration is the default path before Ohio solicitation.
