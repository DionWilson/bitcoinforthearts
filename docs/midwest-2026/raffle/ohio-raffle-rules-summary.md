# Ohio nonprofit raffle rules — research digest (BFTA)

**Purpose:** Working summary for Midwest Bitcoin Summit planning.  
**Not legal advice.** Verify with Ohio AG Charitable Law Section and counsel before selling tickets.

## Bottom line

As a 501(c)(3), Bitcoin for the Arts **can legally conduct a raffle** in Ohio.  
**No state-level raffle license is required** under current law (ORC 2915.092).

You still must follow the rules in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 2915, keep proper records, and check local (Columbus / Franklin County) requirements. Fully online raffles remain restricted.

## Key rules (Ohio AG Charitable Law Section + ORC 2915.092)

| Topic | Current rule |
|-------|--------------|
| **Who can run a raffle** | 501(c)(3) organizations (and certain other tax-exempt categories + schools) |
| **State license needed?** | **No** — eligible orgs do **not** need a bingo license to run a raffle |
| **Proceeds** | As a 501(c)(3), BFTA may keep **100% of net profit** for charitable purposes |
| **How the winner must be chosen** | Physical draw: ticket stubs (or detachable sections) corresponding to **all tickets sold** must be placed in a receptacle and drawn from it. Pure random-number-generator / fully digital selection is **not** authorized under current law |
| **Online ticket sales** | Currently restricted / high risk. The statute is silent or interpreted restrictively by the AG. Fully online raffles (ticket sales + remote digital draw) are treated as problematic. House Bill 476 (to authorize online raffles with registration/licensing) passed the House in late 2025 but was still pending in sources through mid-2026 |
| **Recordkeeping (ORC 2915.10)** | Must keep for **at least 3 years**: itemized gross receipts, itemized expenses (with receipts), list of all prizes, name/address/SSN of anyone winning a prize of $600+, and how net profit was used/distributed |
| **Local rules** | Some cities/counties add their own permits or reporting. Columbus has a Charitable Solicitations registration process. Confirm with City of Columbus License Section and/or Franklin County before selling tickets |

## Practical guidance for the Midwest Bitcoin Summit

**Safest structure right now**

- Sell tickets **in person** at the event (or in limited pre-sale that still results in physical stubs).
- Conduct a **physical drawing** from a receptacle containing stubs for every ticket sold.
- Keep the charity (BFTA) as the clear operator of the raffle — not a third-party for-profit running it “on behalf of” BFTA.
- Maintain the three-year record set required by ORC 2915.10.
- Handle federal tax reporting on large prizes (W-2G / withholding rules when prizes meet IRS thresholds).

**Before selling any tickets, the board should:**

1. Confirm current guidance directly with the **Ohio Attorney General Charitable Law Section**
   - Email: CharitableLaw@OhioAGO.gov
   - Phone: 800-282-0515
   - Page: https://charitable.ohioago.gov/Charitable-Gaming
2. Check **City of Columbus** charitable solicitations / local gaming or event permitting requirements.
3. Confirm BFTA’s existing Ohio charitable solicitation registration is current (most nonprofits soliciting in Ohio must register with the AG under Chapter 1716).

## Board-packet recommendation language

> “Ohio note: Raffles by 501(c)(3) nonprofits are legal and do **not** require a state raffle/bingo license under ORC 2915.092. However, the raffle must comply with Chapter 2915 (especially physical-draw rules and 3-year recordkeeping under 2915.10). Fully online raffles remain restricted under current AG interpretation. Local Columbus/Franklin County rules may also apply. Board should confirm current Ohio AG Charitable Law Section guidance and local requirements in writing before any tickets are sold.”
