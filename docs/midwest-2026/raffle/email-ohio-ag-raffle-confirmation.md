# Email draft — Ohio AG Charitable Law Section (raffle confirmation)

Copy-paste below. Adjust prize description / dates if the artwork raffle details change before sending.

---

**To:** CharitableLaw@OhioAGO.gov  
**Subject:** Confirmation request — in-person 501(c)(3) raffle at Midwest Bitcoin Summit (Columbus, Sept 2026)

Dear Charitable Law Section,

Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. is a New York 501(c)(3) nonprofit (EIN 41-2642260). We are planning a short, **in-person charitable raffle** during the Midwest Bitcoin Summit at the Greater Columbus Convention Center in Columbus, Ohio, on **September 23–24, 2026**.

We understand from ORC 2915.092 and publicly available Charitable Gaming guidance that a 501(c)(3) organization may conduct a raffle in Ohio **without** obtaining a bingo/raffle license, provided the raffle complies with Chapter 2915 (including a **physical drawing** from stubs corresponding to all tickets sold, and recordkeeping under ORC 2915.10).

**Proposed structure (please confirm whether this is consistent with current guidance):**

1. **Operator:** Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. will operate the raffle directly (not a for-profit third party running it on our behalf).
2. **Ticket sales:** In person at the event only (physical tickets with stubs / detachable sections). We do **not** plan a fully online raffle or remote digital draw.
3. **Drawing:** On-site physical draw from a receptacle containing stubs for every ticket sold.
4. **Prize:** Donated artwork (charitable raffle prize). Approximate fair-market value will be documented for our records and for any required federal tax reporting if thresholds are met.
5. **Proceeds:** Net proceeds retained by Bitcoin for the Arts for charitable purposes consistent with our 501(c)(3) mission.
6. **Records:** We will maintain the itemized records required by ORC 2915.10 for at least three years.

**Questions we would appreciate confirmed in writing:**

1. Does this in-person ticket sale + physical-draw structure remain compliant for a 501(c)(3) under current law and AG interpretation?
2. Is any **state-level** registration, notice, or license required for this raffle beyond Chapter 1716 charitable solicitation registration (if applicable)?
3. Are there any additional Charitable Law Section requirements we should follow for a one-time event raffle at a trade-show / conference venue in Columbus?
4. Do you recommend we also clear any **local** Columbus or Franklin County permit/registration before selling tickets, and if so, which office?

Thank you for your guidance. We will not sell raffle tickets until we have confirmation (or clear written direction) from your office and any required local authorities.
