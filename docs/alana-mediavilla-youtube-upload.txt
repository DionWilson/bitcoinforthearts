=== DESCRIPTION ===

In this two-hour conversation, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson sits down with Alana Mediavilla, the Puerto Rican filmmaker behind the award-winning documentary Dirty Coin and founder of Campo Libre in San Juan.

Alana spent more than a decade inside Silicon Valley producing for Google, Apple, PayPal, Volkswagen, and Volvo, including four years as a video producer for Google Cloud. In 2020 she walked away, moved her family home to Puerto Rico, and built the most important documentary on Bitcoin mining ever made. Dirty Coin has since been screened in over 200 cities through a community-hosted distribution model and won Best National Documentary in Puerto Rico, the first English-language film ever to do so.

This is the full story: a photographer father at Kodak, a Cuban mother whose family left Havana under Fidel, the personal sovereignty question that runs through every choice she has made since, and why the next decade of cinema belongs to artists who control their own rails.

IN THIS EPISODE:
- Growing up in Puerto Rico with a Kodak photographer father, and how free film rolls shaped a filmmaker
- Half Cuban, half Puerto Rican: sovereignty as a verb, not a noun
- The 2018 phone call from her Cuban grandfather that reframed Bitcoin as a family tool
- Buying 3 BTC at $200 each, and finding out her husband had sold them at $600
- Leaving Google Cloud during the 2020 lockdown and moving three kids home to Puerto Rico
- Becoming a Bitcoin-only maxi after watching neighbors get rug-pulled on the island
- Why Dirty Coin had to exist: policymakers regulating math they did not understand
- What Malawi taught her that Texas could not, and how mining unlocks stranded renewable energy
- Grid balancing: why a Bitcoin miner can turn on and off 20 times a day and an AI data center cannot
- Financing a documentary in Bitcoin, and the day a 40k BTC donation became 70k of production spend
- Building trust with Cynthia Loomis, flare-gas operators, and communities burned by mainstream media
- Community-hosted distribution: 500 screenings, 200 cities, aimed at meetup leaders
- The most unreported story about Bitcoin and energy
- Where techno-optimism ends and surveillance begins
- The vision for decentralized film distribution paid in sats
- Node Runners: the documentary she is filming through 2027 for 2028 release
- Advice for women and mothers looking at Bitcoin from outside the movement
- What a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from BFTA would unlock for Node Runners
