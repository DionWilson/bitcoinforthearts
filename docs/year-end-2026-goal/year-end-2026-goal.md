## Headline Goal

**Fund 50 working artists with Bitcoin grants of $500–$2,000 each by December 31, 2026.**

---

## Why This Number

Federal arts funding is in structural retreat — the NEA has been targeted for elimination in two consecutive federal budgets, state arts appropriations are projected to drop 7.7% nationally for FY2026, and the ARPA stimulus dollars that propped up the field through 2024 are now gone. Working artists are exposed.

Funding 50 artists this year is not a vanity metric. It is the smallest scale at which BFTA proves the model: that a Bitcoin-funded 501(c)(3) can deliver grants reliably, transparently, and at a cadence that matters — without depending on the institutional rails that are failing the field.

## The Plan, By the Numbers

| Line item | Floor scenario | Stretch scenario |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Average grant size *(range $500–$2,000)* | $1,000 | $2,000 |
| Direct artist grants **(55%)** | $50,000 | $100,000 |
| Programs **(30%)** — workshops, events, research, this fundraiser | $27,300 | $54,600 |
| Operations **(10%)** — software, banking, admin | $9,100 | $18,200 |
| HODL Vault **(5%)** — long-term Bitcoin endowment | $4,550 | $9,100 |
| **Total raise required** | **$91,000** | **$182,000** |

Hitting **$91K** funds the headline goal at the floor scenario — 50 artists at $1,000 each, with the rest of the allocation budgeted for programs, operations, and the long-term endowment. Hitting **$182K** doubles every artist's grant. Either way, every dollar maps to a transparent allocation published quarterly at [bitcoinforthearts.org/transparency](https://www.bitcoinforthearts.org/transparency).
