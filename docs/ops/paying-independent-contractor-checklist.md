# Paying an independent contractor (501(c)(3) checklist)

**Use for:** Steven (sound / production engineer) for Midwest Bitcoin Summit work  
**Org:** Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. · EIN 41-2642260  
**Not legal or tax advice.** Confirm with your CPA if anything is unclear.

---

## Correct sequence

1. **Confirm he is a contractor, not an employee**  
   He invoices for event services, uses his own tools/methods, is not on payroll. If that is the relationship, treat as independent contractor.

2. **Collect Form W-9 before you pay**  
   IRS Form W-9 (Request for Taxpayer Identification Number and Certification).  
   You need this to issue Form **1099-NEC** if you pay him **$600 or more** in a calendar year (US).

3. **Get a written invoice**  
   On his letterhead or email is fine. Must include:
   - His legal name / business name  
   - Address  
   - Date  
   - Description of services (e.g. Midwest Bitcoin Summit live sound, Sept 22–24, 2026)  
   - Amount due (USD and/or BTC if paying in Bitcoin)  
   - Payment instructions (bank / Zelle / Strike / Bitcoin address)  
   - Invoice number (optional but helpful)

4. **Optional but smart: short independent contractor agreement**  
   Scope, dates, fee, who owns recordings, kill fee, insurance if any. Especially useful for event production.

5. **Pay against the invoice**  
   Keep PDF of invoice + payment proof (transfer receipt, txid if BTC) in your books.

6. **Year-end**  
   If total paid in 2026 is ≥ $600, issue **1099-NEC** by the IRS deadline (typically Jan 31 following the year). Your bookkeeper/CPA can e-file.

---

## If you pay Steven in Bitcoin

- Still get the **W-9** and **invoice** (invoice can state USD equivalent or BTC amount).  
- For your books, record the **USD fair market value on the payment date**.  
- Keep the **txid** / payment confirmation with the invoice.  
- 1099-NEC (if required) is generally reported in **USD**.
