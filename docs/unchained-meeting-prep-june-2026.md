## 1. Unchained — what's on the table

Their core differentiator: **collaborative custody** (multi-sig, you hold 2 of 3 keys) layered onto traditional financial products. They're not a custodian taking your keys — they're a coordinator that lets you hold real Bitcoin in tax-advantaged structures while keeping self-custody.

### The macro story (this is the rhetorical hammer)

The **American Rescue Plan Act of 2021** created the **Special Financial Assistance (SFA) Program** under PBGC, also known as the **Butch Lewis Act**. Headline numbers:

- **~$94 billion** allocated to bail out severely underfunded multiemployer pension plans
- **$54.6 billion** approved as of mid-2024, covering **818,000 workers, retirees, and beneficiaries**
- **250+ plans** projected to ultimately receive funds
- Without the bailout, PBGC's multiemployer insurance program itself was projected insolvent by FY 2026
- Bailouts are **non-repayable grants** — taxpayer money, not insurance premiums
- Designed to keep recipient plans afloat for ~30 years (through 2051)

**Plans receiving SFA include several entertainment-industry funds:**
- AFM-EPF (Musicians) — $1.527 billion (Aug 2024)
- Radio, Television and Recording Arts Pension Plan — $63 million (2024)

### Translation for the meeting

The fiat pension system for performing artists has materially failed. **Two performing-arts pension plans alone needed nearly $1.6 billion in printed-money bailouts** in the past two years. The "fix" is a federal mandate that says these plans must be classified as "critical status" through 2051 *regardless of their actual financial health*. That's not a fix — that's a stay of execution funded by the same monetary debasement that broke them in the first place.

There will not be another $94 billion Butch Lewis Act in 2051.

## 3. Why performing artists have the worst-shaped retirement need

This is the part most pension-industry people don't internalize, and it's where Dion's lived experience is the trump card.

A performing artist's career economics are *structurally hostile* to fiat-based defined-benefit plans:

| Lifecycle factor | Office worker | Performing artist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Active career length | ~40 years (25 → 65) | **~10–25 years** (dancers retire 35–45; even non-dancers face severe income instability) |
| Income shape | Steady salary | Project-based, irregular, often below median |
| Vesting eligibility | Usually achieved | **Many artists never accumulate enough credits in a single plan** to qualify for full benefits |
| Years drawing retirement | ~20 (65 → 85) | **~40–50** (retire at 40, live to 85) |
| Savings window | ~40 years | **~15 years** |
| "Compounding window" inside fiat | OK — long enough to outpace inflation in equities | **Brutal** — 15 years isn't enough for fiat assets to outpace debasement, and benefits are paid in increasingly worthless dollars over the 40-year drawdown |

**The artist's retirement curve is the inverse of the worker's curve. The pension structure was designed for the worker's curve. The artist gets the worst of both ends.**

Bitcoin's properties uniquely match this lifecycle:

- **Hard cap (21M)** — drawdown won't be eroded by debasement
- **Long-term appreciation** — short savings window can compound enough to fund a long drawdown window
- **Self-custody** — no plan administrator, no fund insolvency risk, no merger-bailout dependency
- **Portability** — an artist who works in eight states under five contracts in their career doesn't lose anything to vesting fragmentation
