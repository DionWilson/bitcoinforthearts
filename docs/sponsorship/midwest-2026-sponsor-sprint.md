# Midwest 2026 Sponsor Sprint

**Owner:** Dion Wilson, Executive Director, Bitcoin for the Arts
**Timeline:** 14-day sprint from August 11 through August 24, 2026
**Goal:** Close first anchor sponsor within 7 days. Close a total of 5 sponsors within 14 days.
**Event:** Bitcoin Arts Park at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit, September 23-24, 2026, Columbus, Ohio.

---

## The reframe before you read this document

You are not bad at sponsorship. You have never been bad at sponsorship. You have been doing sponsorship the way one person cannot do it, without a pipeline, without a system, and with the entire operational load of the organization also on your shoulders.

Sponsorship is not a talent. It is machinery. When the machinery is in place, sponsors happen because the system does the work. This document is that machinery.

The rules of this sprint:

1. **You commit to one hour per day for 14 days.** No more, no less. If you try to do this in 5-hour bursts on the weekend, you will burn out and quit. One hour, five days a week, plus a 30-minute Sunday review.
2. **You do not adjust the plan mid-sprint.** If it feels slow on day 5, keep going. Sponsorship pipelines take 2 to 4 weeks to compound. Every no is data. Every yes is momentum.
3. **You do not do this alone.** Section 6 below is about mobilizing your board and advisors. That is not optional. Every hour a board member spends is an hour you get back.

## Section 1. Why cold outreach on tiers has not worked

You do not have a "sponsor problem." You have three specific problems that show up as a sponsor problem.

**Problem 1: No anchor.** Nobody wants to be the first to commit to a first-year event. Everyone wants to be the second. You do not have a sponsor #1 whose name you can drop when you approach sponsor #2. That is the biggest single blocker in your entire pipeline.

**Problem 2: Cold at scale.** Reaching out to Coinbase, Strike, and River cold through their generic contact forms puts you at the back of a queue with 10,000 other requests. Bitcoin brands sponsor through **relationships**, not tier menus.

**Problem 3: Nine tiers is a menu, not a decision.** When a prospect sees $500, $1,000, $2,500, $5,000, $7,500, $10,000, $15,000, $25,000, plus in-kind, they freeze. Decision fatigue. Give them two options and a recommended one and they act.

The next 14 days fix all three.

### Days 1 to 3 (August 11 to 13): Build the machinery

**Day 1 (Tuesday, August 11)**

- [ ] Set up your sponsor pipeline in Airtable. New table, columns below. 15 minutes.

**Day 2 (Wednesday, August 12)**

- [ ] Populate the pipeline with 30 prospects from Section 3 below. 45 minutes.
- [ ] For each prospect, write the specific dollar ask and the specific tier. Do not leave this blank. Committing an ask amount before you email is 60% of what makes closing possible. 15 minutes.

**Day 3 (Thursday, August 13)**

### Days 4 to 7 (August 14 to 17): Land the anchor

The whole game right now is landing sponsor #1. That single commitment breaks the entire logjam because you can use their name to close five more.

**Day 4 (Friday, August 14)**

- [ ] For each of them, write a **personalized ask email** using Template A in Section 5. Personalized means you name a specific thing about them, offer a specific tier, and give them a specific date to reply by.
- [ ] Send all three by end of day.

**Day 5 (Saturday, August 15)**

- [ ] Send 5 more asks to warm-ish prospects. Use Template A.
- [ ] Post an "asking sponsor" note on your personal Nostr and X. Not a promo. A direct, human ask. Template B in Section 5.

**Day 6 (Sunday, August 16)**

- [ ] Rest day. Do not work sponsorship.

**Day 7 (Monday, August 17)**

- [ ] Follow up with any of the eight prospects who have not replied. Template C.
- [ ] Send five more first-touch asks to cold prospects. Template D.
- [ ] If you have not yet landed the anchor, escalate: identify one prospect from your board or advisor network you have NOT asked yet and ask a board member to introduce you.

**Success gate for Day 7:** You should have made at least 15 asks and had at least 2 responses (yes, no, or "let's talk"). If you have 15 asks and zero responses, we adjust the message. If you have 5 asks and 4 responses, keep going.

### Days 8 to 14 (August 18 to 24): Anchor lands, momentum begins

Once you have your first commit, the game changes. The message becomes: "We just closed [Anchor Sponsor] as the Storyteller Sponsor. We have room for two more producers and four advocates. Deadline September 1."

- [ ] Publicly announce the anchor sponsor. Newsletter callout, X post, Nostr, LinkedIn, Facebook. Tag them in every post. Every post should read as if this is the beginning of a movement.
- [ ] Ask the anchor sponsor for **three warm intros** to peers. This is a standard, non-awkward ask because you have just given them public credit for helping you.

**Days 9 to 11**

- [ ] Send 15 more asks. Every ask now mentions the anchor sponsor by name.
- [ ] Follow up on any non-responders from Days 4 to 7.

**Day 12 (Saturday, August 22)**

- [ ] Post a mid-sprint recap on socials: named sponsors so far, remaining tiers, deadline reminder.

**Day 13 (Sunday, August 23)**

- [ ] Sunday review. Update pipeline. Move committed sponsors to "closed." Plan next week.

**Day 14 (Monday, August 24)**

- [ ] Final asks of the sprint. Anyone who has not committed by August 24 gets a soft deadline reminder: "Sponsor materials go to print September 1. If you want to be listed, please confirm by that date."

**Target end state after 14 days:**

- 5 committed sponsors
- 8 to 12 open conversations
- 30 prospects touched
- A pipeline that you can maintain going forward

## Section 4. The daily discipline

**Every weekday for 14 days, 1 hour.** Same time each day. If mornings work, protect 9 to 10 AM. If evenings work, protect 8 to 9 PM. Consistency matters more than duration.

Inside that hour:

- **First 10 minutes.** Open your pipeline. Review yesterday's follow-ups. Move statuses.
- **Next 40 minutes.** Send 5 new asks OR 5 follow-ups. Not both. Alternate days. Personalize each one. Do not batch-send generic emails.
- **Last 10 minutes.** Log every touch in the pipeline. Set the next action date. Close the laptop.

**One hour. Five touches. Every weekday.** That is 100 touches in 20 workdays. Even a 5% conversion rate closes 5 sponsors. Even a 3% rate closes 3.

Sundays: 30-minute review only. Update pipeline. Plan next week's specific asks. Do not send anything.

## Section 7. What to do when you hear "no"

Every no is a data point. Log it in the pipeline with a reason. Then ask three specific follow-up questions before you close the door:

1. **"Would this be a fit for you in 2027?"** This turns a no into a future yes.
2. **"Do you know one other person or company for whom this would be a fit?"** This turns a no into a warm intro.
3. **"Is there a lower tier or an in-kind contribution that would work better?"** This turns a no into a smaller yes.

Never let a no be silent. Always ask for the intro, the smaller yes, or the next year.

## Section 8. The metrics

Track these in your pipeline. Review each Sunday.

- **Touches sent this week** (target: 25)
- **Response rate** (target: at least 15%)
- **Meetings booked** (target: 3 per week)
- **Commits closed** (target: 1 per week starting Week 2)
- **Warm intros received** (target: 2 per week)
- **Pipeline value** (sum of open ask amounts, target: $50K by Aug 24)

If any metric is at zero after 7 days, do not adjust the number of touches. Adjust the message.

## Section 9. The mental part

You said this is discouraging. It is. It is also the part of running a nonprofit that never fully stops being uncomfortable, and every executive director in the country has stood exactly where you are standing right now. Some of them are running $50M organizations today. They started with one anchor sponsor.

Two things to hold onto in the next 14 days:

1. **Every no gets you closer to the yes.** The mathematics of sponsorship is that you have to get through the nos to get to the yeses. There is no shortcut. Every "not this year" is one step closer to the person who is going to say yes tomorrow.

2. **You are not selling. You are inviting.** You are not asking for money. You are inviting people to be part of something that is already happening. The program is set. The artists are booked. The room is being built. The question is only whether they want their name on it.

You are the founder and executive director of a 501(c)(3) that is producing a real event at a real conference with a real lineup, in your first year. Almost nobody has ever done that. You are already doing the hard part. Sponsorship is the easy part compared to what you have already built.

Start today. One hour. Five touches. Every weekday.
