=== DESCRIPTION ===

Abel James grew up in the back woods of New Hampshire. His mother was a nurse practitioner. He finished high school and college in six years, studied brain science and music at Dartmouth, and then followed his doctor's advice. He got sicker. He did the opposite and got well. That habit of questioning the expert is what led him to sound money, and then to getting paid for his music with no label in the middle.

He is a New York Times bestselling author and the host of The Fat-Burning Man, one of the longest-running health podcasts. He is also a singer, guitarist, and sax player. Swamp Thing, produced by Denny Hemingson of the Tim McGraw band, with David Santos and Wendy Moten, hit number one on Wavlake and Fountain. The record was about ten years old and earning pennies from the old systems. Uploaded to those apps, it brought in hundreds of dollars in a few weeks, plus messages from the people actually listening. He sells the new album, Headline, directly at abeljames.com.

In this episode, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson talks with Abel about music as a language the body hears before the mind does, the food industry insiders who would not feed their own product to their kids, a starter house in Austin that later listed for $1.2 million, and a tax bill bigger than what he paid for the house. Abel bought his first bitcoin in 2016 and 2017 and left it alone. Fiat, he says, is a race you are always losing. Sound money gave him permission to spend a day writing a song.

They also get into the contracts. What a deal actually buys can be the songs, the name, the likeness, and the right to decide what the artist is allowed to say. Abel has turned those deals down, in music, in television, and in books. In a room of Bitcoin musicians in Nashville, and again at Nostr Vegas, he found the other version. People zapping songs in real time, and asking how they can help.

This conversation is education. It is not medical, financial, legal, or tax advice.
