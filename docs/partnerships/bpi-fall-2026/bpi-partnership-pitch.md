## The Pitch (forward this to BPI)

**Subject:** Bitcoin For The Arts × BPI — a cultural lead-in to your DC convening (Sept 20 or 21)

---

To the BPI team,

Bitcoin Policy Institute is bringing the most consequential Bitcoin policy gathering of 2026 to DC on Sept 22–23. The people in that room are the ones who will shape Bitcoin's regulatory future for the next decade.

**Bitcoin For The Arts wants to give that gathering its cultural mooring** — and we're already booked in the same building, on the right side of your calendar.

### Who we are, briefly

[Bitcoin For The Arts](https://bitcoinforthearts.org) is the first 501(c)(3) in the United States funding artists exclusively in Bitcoin. EIN 41-2642260. Founded 2025 by Dion Wilson, an AEA-credentialed performing artist whose career includes Dance Theatre of Harlem, the *Lion King* national tour, and 15+ years inside the working-artist economy. Our governance, board, allocation formula (55/30/10/5 — direct grants / programs / operations / HODL Vault), and quarterly transparency reports are all public. We funded our first artist grants this year.

We're not a casual nonprofit asking for a favor. We're a young organization building durably — including [a research portal](https://www.bitcoinforthearts.org/research) modeled on Brookings and the American Cancer Society, anchored by an annual *State of Arts Funding* report and a recent deep-dive on the ARPA cliff (the structural funding gap left by the expiration of American Rescue Plan arts dollars). That research is intentionally policy-adjacent and we'd value BPI's eyes on it.

### The opportunity

Our flagship event, **Art + Zap Weekend**, lands at Pubkey DC in September 2026. It runs in two halves:

1. **A one-day, 4.5-hour Bitcoin podcast livestream** featuring 21 artists across music, dance, theater, visual art, film, writing, and storytelling — hosted by Bitcoin podcasters, broadcast on YouTube, X, and Nostr.
2. **One in-person night at Pubkey DC** with live performance, a silent art auction, and a Broadway-caliber headliner. (Broadway is dark Mondays, so top NYC talent is uniquely available.)

We had been holding **Monday, September 14** as our internal target date. After our most recent conversation with Trey, we're proposing to shift to **Sunday, September 20 or Monday, September 21** — to position our event as the cultural opening to BPI's policy days the following Tuesday–Wednesday.

We'd flex to either Sunday or Monday depending on which works better for BPI's own venue setup needs.

### Why this matters for BPI specifically

A serious policy convening becomes a more powerful one when its audience arrives already culturally activated. The night before is when relationships form, and BFTA's event is structured to produce exactly that — a benefit, not a hotel cocktail hour; an artist showcase, not a sales pitch.

Specifically:

- **Cultural credibility.** Bitcoin policy is too often framed by its critics as a finance-and-tech story. BPI's work is the antidote to that. BFTA brings the third leg of the stool — Bitcoin as a cultural and patronage system — to the same building, the same week. Photographs, video, and press from that night carry forward the message that Bitcoin policy and Bitcoin culture are part of one civilization-scale conversation.

- **Press hook.** A policy convening is harder to pitch to non-trade press in isolation than a policy convening *plus* a cultural benefit night with 21 working artists, a silent auction, and a Broadway-caliber headliner. The combined story is the one that earns features in *NYT*, *Washington Post Style*, and arts-policy outlets that wouldn't typically cover a policy day.

- **Donor cultivation.** BFTA's audience is high-net-worth Bitcoin donors, arts patrons, and the NYC/DC cultural overlap. Many of them are people BPI would want in the room for its own work but wouldn't otherwise come to a Tuesday-morning policy session. Arriving Sunday or Monday for the BFTA night and staying through your event is a structurally easier ask than a single-day fly-in.

- **Networking architecture.** Our finale is intentionally social — silent auction format, table seating, opportunities for sustained one-on-one conversations between Bitcoiners, artists, donors, and policy figures. That's the warmup BPI's panel-and-keynote format isn't designed to produce, and it's where the strongest BPI-attendee relationships of the week will likely form.

- **Tax-deductible association.** Both organizations are 501(c)(3)s. A formal partnership is clean from a compliance standpoint and creates a tax-deductible giving vehicle for any BPI donors who'd value supporting cultural Bitcoin work in addition to policy.

### Four partnership shapes — pick what fits

We're presenting this as a menu so BPI can engage at whatever depth makes sense. Any one of these works for us; combinations are welcome.

**Option 1 — BPI as Cultural Pre-Event Sponsor of BFTA**

BPI sponsors BFTA's event at a tier:

| Tier | Amount | BPI receives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title Sponsor | $25,000 (1 slot) | Co-branding throughout; persistent on-screen logo through the livestream; title billing at Pubkey; 6 reserved seats; a 5-minute branded moment from the stage; permanent listing on BFTA's Sponsors page and 2026 Transparency Report |
| Supporting Sponsor | $10,000 (3 slots) | Rotating on-screen logo; logo on donation overlay; brief on-stage thank-you; 3 reserved seats; signage at Pubkey |
| Friend of the Arts | $5,000 (5 slots) | Rotating sponsor card; on-stage thank-you scroll; 1 reserved seat; signage |

In any tier, BPI receives a speaking moment from the Pubkey stage (Executive Director or designee, ~5 minutes) framing the policy days ahead. BFTA promotes BPI 2026 across all our channels — newsletter, social, livestream — through the lead-up.

**Option 2 — Co-Hosted Cultural Opening (zero dollars from BPI)**

BFTA and BPI jointly present the event as *"Art + Zap Weekend, Cultural Opening to BPI 2026"* (or similar mutually agreeable framing).

- **From BFTA:** marketing throughout our lead-up positioning the event as the official cultural opening to BPI's convening. BPI logo on event materials and livestream. Speaking slot at Pubkey.
- **From BPI:** mention of BFTA's event in your attendee comms, your event app, and your pre-event marketing. Permission to use BPI's logo on our materials.
- **Revenue:** stays fully separate. Each org runs its own ticket / sponsorship economics.
- **Cost to BPI:** zero dollars. Cost is one round of comms coordination and logo-use approval.

This is the lowest-friction shape and the one we'd recommend if budget is constrained on your side. The reputational and audience-sharing benefit is real even without dollars changing hands.

**Option 3 — Venue Continuity Partnership**

If BPI needs the Pubkey DC building from Monday Sept 21 for your own setup, BFTA shifts to **Sunday night Sept 20**. The building stays continuously activated:

- Sunday night: BFTA event
- Monday: BPI setup day (audience already in town from Sunday)
- Tuesday–Wednesday: BPI main event days

Joint signage at the venue — *"Welcome to BPI Week at Pubkey DC, beginning with Bitcoin For The Arts Art + Zap Weekend"* — frames the four nights as one civilization-of-Bitcoin moment. We can co-design the signage and lobby experience.

**Option 4 — BFTA as Official Cultural Track within BPI 2026**

This is the inverse of Option 1. **BFTA pays BPI** a track-sponsor fee to be included as the official cultural component of BPI 2026 — listed in your program, your app, and your attendee materials. BPI attendees get complimentary access (or a steep discount) to BFTA's event. BFTA gets event-level promotion through BPI's full attendee pipeline.

Track-sponsor fee is something we'd discuss based on what slot BPI typically reserves for this kind of inclusion. We'd want a clear deliverable list to justify the investment.

### Concerns we anticipate

A few things that might give BPI pause, addressed upfront:

- **"This isn't on-brand for a policy convening."** Counter: this is precisely the kind of cultural soft power that durable policy needs to associate with. The Bitcoin policy story strengthens when it's grounded in a parallel cultural and artistic story. BFTA's brand is serious — we publish research, our board includes a multi-decade public-sector financial professional, our governance is transparent.
- **"Will BFTA's event compete with our event for donors or attention?"** Counter: we're not competing for the same dollar. BPI donors give to policy work; BFTA donors give to artist grants. They're complementary motivation paths. Many donors give to both — and a single trip to DC where both organizations are featured produces more giving across both than two separate trips would.
- **"How much coordination overhead does this add?"** Counter: Option 2 (co-hosted cultural opening) requires roughly one comms coordination call, one logo-approval round, and a few cross-promotional posts. That's it. Heavier partnership shapes are entirely scoped to whatever BPI is willing to invest.
- **"What if your event under-delivers?"** Counter: we ship every reasonable promise. Our existing landing page lists exact sponsor deliverables; our existing donor receipts include EIN, the 501(c)(3) compliance language, and the "no goods or services" tax statement. If we miss a deliverable, we make it right — that's documented in our public accountability framework.
