### The content framing (this is the board question)

You are right that “general financial literacy” and “Bitcoin” are two different conversations for a board. Here is how I would walk in:

**Phase 1 — what I’d like to start with for your students**  
Money literacy for young people who want careers in the performing arts:

- What money *is* (not just “how do I budget”)
- How money works in a performing career (irregular income, contracts, savings between gigs, the difference between surviving a season and building longevity)
- How the monetary system they live in quietly shapes rent, tuition, audition costs, and the price of everything around them
- The idea of **sound money** — what makes money trustworthy over time — as a principle, not a product pitch

This meets students where they are. It respects that most of them, and many of the adults around them, have never been taught monetary basics in any arts program. It also creates a clean bridge: once someone understands what sound money *means*, they can later decide whether Bitcoin belongs in that conversation. Phase 1 does not require that decision.

**Phase 2 — optional, only if you and the board want it later**  
A separate, clearly labeled conversation introducing Bitcoin as one modern attempt to implement sound money principles, and why some working artists are paying attention. That session would be opt-in, age-appropriate, and board-approved before it ever touches students. I will not smuggle it into Phase 1.

So the honest framing for your board:  
**We are offering a money-and-career module for On Broadway students, co-created with Rema, taught with On Broadway’s oversight. Bitcoin for the Arts is the partner organization. Bitcoin itself is not the required subject of the first pilot.**

### A concrete Phase 1 outline (for us to revise together)

Working title: **The Artist and the Money**  
Audience: older On Broadway students preparing for college auditions / professional tracks  
Format: **two sessions**, ~60–75 minutes each, discussion-heavy, no jargon  
Pilot window: whenever you have room in the Fall program

**Session 1 — What is money, and why does it matter for artists?**
- Money is not “just cash.” It is a tool: a way to store work, plan time, and make trade-offs.
- Short history in plain language: barter → commodity money → paper → the money we use now.
- Artist-specific reality: irregular income, long training years, high upfront costs (classes, headshots, travel, audition fees).
- Activity: map a year in the life of a working performer — where money comes in, where it goes, what breaks people.
- Homework / takeaway: one personal money question they want answered before Session 2.

**Session 2 — The system you live in, and what “sound money” means**
- Why prices for rent, food, transportation, and training keep rising — purchasing power in plain English.
- Why longevity is rare: not only talent and grit, but the ability to survive gaps without eating the future.
- Case study (with your permission): what On Broadway’s 12 years and scholarship model teach about building something that lasts — and what gig-to-gig careers look like by comparison.
- Sound money principles: scarcity, durability, divisibility, portability, verifiability — without naming a product first.
- Close: “You don’t have to become economists. You do need enough money literacy that the industry can’t quietly run the table on you.”
- Optional parent/board one-pager afterward summarizing what was taught.

**What we would not do in Phase 1**
- No wallet setup
- No asking students or families to buy Bitcoin
- No political rant
- No replacing your existing training — only adding a layer
