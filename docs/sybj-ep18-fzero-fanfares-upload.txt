DESCRIPTION (paste into Fanfares Description)

Fzero is a designer, illustrator, node runner, and co-founder of Ninefold, the studio he runs with Ivy Lumi. Their line is "stories that make you build." He started by copying manga as a kid. He studied economics, finance, and marketing, then opened stores and ran the floor for big fashion brands. The work looked like a career. It did not feel like one. On December 25, still under 30 and home with his family, he had a stroke. He already knew Bitcoin. He had been stacking sats for years. The scare settled the question. Time is the currency you cannot count, and he wanted to leave something that helps people after he is gone.

In this episode, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson talks with Fzero about the moment Bitcoin stopped being an idea and became a node in the house, about getting banned from X and staying on Nostr, and about pricing work once the payment is peer to peer. His GENESIS 113 pins are 21,000 sats. The shop is Take My Sats. He describes the first drawing that sent people to the Bitcoin white paper, the day a friend wore his alien as a tattoo at Nostriga, and GENESIS 113, the painting that treats Bitcoin as frequency: a tower of light rising out of noise. He made the painting. Timechain Art Magazine is featuring it in Edition One. It hung at The Space in Denver. The pins, made with Modeotec, went to BTC Prague and then to Plebeian Market.

He also tells the story of $BLEEP, the retro sci-fi series about an extraterrestrial trying to understand humanity through Bitcoin. The character is him, with the awkwardness left in. Season 1 is wrapped. Season 2 arrives in 2027. And he is plain about rooms and galleries. The artist creates first. Everyone else is there to serve the artist. The profit comes from serving them well.

This episode is on Fanfares this week. 21 sats unlocks it. 11 sats go to Bitcoin For The Arts and 10 sats go to Fzero. Next week the full video is free on YouTube.

IN THIS EPISODE:
Drawing manga as a kid, then leaving it for economics and retail. Opening shops, running the floor, and feeling the art go missing. Tattooing as the way back. A skull, a dollar sign, and a QR code that opens the white paper. Why the details matter, and what gets lost when two people use the same word for different things. A European bank account that would not move the money. Running a node. The stroke on December 25. Time as the currency. COVID, cold storage, and who a stack is actually for. How a fiat job reaches the body, not just the bills. Joining Nostr and X in the same stretch, then losing the X account. Why Nostr stayed. Pricing pins at 21,000 sats. Take My Sats. Low time preference and the Mona Lisa on a donkey. Building in public. Why a middleman slows the work down. The alien tattoo at Nostriga, and the trust of drawing on skin. GENESIS 113: his painting, then Timechain Art Magazine Edition One, The Space in Denver, and a thank you to Asanoha. The Modeotec PCB pins, a run of 42, at BTC Prague and Plebeian Market. Who $BLEEP is for. Why a story teaches proof of work faster than a lecture. Kids' rooms in El Salvador and Las Vegas. Ninefold with Ivy Lumi, announced at Origin Seoul. Eight worlds, including $BLEEP, Life with Bitcoin, Zaza, and Tynka. Stories around the fire. What a gallery owes an artist. "What can I do for Bitcoin?" Season 2 of $BLEEP in 2027. What a $500 to $2,000 micro-grant would unlock.
