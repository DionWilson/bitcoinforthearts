Bitcoin for the Arts (BFTA) is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit that pays working artists directly in Bitcoin and maintains a long-term reserve so what we raise today supports artists for generations. We are co-curating Generations, the gallery and live programming inside the Midwest Bitcoin Summit — two days of working artists in a curated cultural room, with sponsorship opportunities from $500 to $25,000.

The Opportunity

Bitcoin conferences have grown into serious institutions. What has been quietly missing alongside them is a curatorial counterpart — a serious cultural room, with serious working artists, treated as central to the programming rather than peripheral to it. Generations is that room, and Columbus is where Bitcoin for the Arts is going to help build it.

The audience is the reason. The room at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit is a crowd of Bitcoiners who already understand sound money — and who are, by every definition, the future patron base for the next generation of working artists. Bringing real artists into that room, paid in Bitcoin, supported by infrastructure that lets the audience zap them in real time, is exactly the kind of moment our 501(c)(3) was built to create.

Total fundraising target: $80,000+. The level of commitment that lets us bring working artists into Columbus and pay them like the working professionals they are. Tiers below from $500 to $25,000.

Why This Matters

Bitcoin for the Arts pays working artists directly in Bitcoin through quarterly micro-grants and longer-term residencies. Every donation flows through a public allocation rule we call 55/30/10/5: 55% to artists, 30% to programs, 10% to operations, and 5% to a long-term Bitcoin reserve we call the HODL Vault. What we raise today supports artists for generations.

Sponsorships at Generations directly fund artist payments at the event and grow BFTA's reserve for future grant cycles. Every sponsor is publicly credited and counted in our annual transparency report.
