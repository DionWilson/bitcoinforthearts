## How to generate the PDF

Anyone can produce a clean PDF from the HTML in under a minute. No special software needed.

### Mac / Windows / Linux — any browser

1. **Open the file.** Double-click `midwest-2026-sponsor-sheet.html` (or right-click → Open With → Chrome / Safari / Firefox / Edge).
2. **Open the print dialog.** `Cmd + P` (Mac) or `Ctrl + P` (Windows / Linux).
3. **Set print options:**
   - **Destination:** Save as PDF (or "Microsoft Print to PDF" on Windows).
   - **Paper size:** Letter (8.5 × 11 in).
   - **Margins:** Default.
   - **Background graphics:** **ON** (very important — without this, the colors and tier-card backgrounds disappear).
   - **Headers and footers:** OFF.
4. **Click Save.** Name it `BFTA-Generations-Sponsor-Sheet-2026.pdf` or similar.

The PDF should be **6–8 pages** on Letter paper. Cover and hero on page 1, programming overview on page 2, sponsorship tiers on pages 3–5, in-kind contributions and contact info on the final pages.

### If the colors don't print correctly

Make sure "Background graphics" or "Print backgrounds" is enabled in your browser's print options. Without it, the orange / lime / cream / black brand colors won't render in the PDF.

## How to use it in outreach

The sponsor sheet is a **leave-behind**, not a pitch in itself. The flow that works:

1. **Personalized outreach email** *(the actual pitch)* — drafted using the BFTA outreach script, sent to a specific decision-maker. References the sheet at the bottom: *"Attached is our full sponsor sheet for context."*
2. **PDF attachment** *(the leave-behind)* — gives the recipient something to forward internally to their team or share with finance / marketing leads.
3. **Follow-up call** *(the close)* — once they've seen both the email and the sheet, the call is for them to ask specific questions and lock the tier.

**Do not send the sheet alone.** Without the personalized email, the PDF arrives cold and reads like a generic sponsorship deck. The email + PDF together is the unit.

## Editorial principles for sponsor conversations

These are the things volunteers should keep in mind when discussing the sheet:

1. **Tax-deductibility is real and matters.** BFTA is a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt nonprofit, EIN **41-2642260**. Sponsorship dollars are tax-deductible to the full extent allowed by law. For corporate finance teams reviewing the spend, this matters substantively.
2. **The sheet is honest about what we can and can't deliver.** We don't promise specific artist names because the lineup is still being finalized. We don't promise specific livestream viewer counts because the broadcast scale depends on factors outside our control. We commit only to what we own — programming categories, naming rights, branding placement, recap content, transparency reporting.
3. **Sponsors are buying outcomes, not promises.** Every sponsor at this event is publicly credited and counted in BFTA's annual transparency report. That permanent association is part of what they're buying.
4. **The 55/30/10/5 rule is the differentiator.** Most arts nonprofits don't publish their allocation rule. BFTA's transparency about how every dollar moves is worth mentioning in any extended conversation — it builds trust and signals seriousness.
5. **No artist names are public yet.** Per Julie Dziedzic's recent guidance, BFTA is holding off on announcing specific artists until the final plan is approved with the conference. The sheet describes programming categories, not individuals. Volunteers should follow the same discipline in conversation.

## When to update this file

The sponsor sheet should be re-versioned and re-distributed when any of the following change:

- A specific tier sells out (mark slots as "filled" or remove from the sheet).
- The fundraising target shifts (e.g., we exceed $80K and want to raise the public stretch number).
- New programming threads get added (e.g., a confirmed second band, a confirmed mural by Marcus Connor).
- A confirmed marquee sponsor becomes public and we want to credit them in the sheet.

After the September event, this sheet is replaced with a **post-event impact report** (planned for October) that documents what was delivered, who sponsored, and the full transparency accounting.
