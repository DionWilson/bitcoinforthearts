=== DESCRIPTION ===

Jered Tanner spent seasons at the Utah Shakespeare Festival, covered Timon, Zazu, and Pumbaa on the North American tour of Disney's The Lion King, and played Merlin at Tournament of Kings in Las Vegas. From the outside, that looks like making it. From the inside, the work was four-month contracts, unemployment he did not know how to file, years on other people's couches, and a good paycheck that still cost him the chance to make anything new. He records as Eon Jred. With his wife Andrea, he is one half of The Mysteries Gone and the lead singer of Dust Mummies.

He did not come to Bitcoin first. He came through GameStop and AMC, a Dogecoin buy at the top, and a coin he launched himself so he could see how meme coins actually work. He is making a student film about that world, Buy the Dip, while finishing a graduate degree at Maharishi International University. Along the way he talked with Bernard von NotHaus, who issued the Liberty Dollar and had it seized. That is the contrast he lands on. Satoshi did not back Bitcoin with gold, and he did not stay to run it.

In this episode, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson sits down with a friend from the same company. They talk about what a theater career actually pays, why houses can be full and shows can still close, and the choice between a steady check and the work an artist came to do. Dion's answer to "I could have bought it at $1,000" is the line from the clip. Less than 5 percent of the world owns any Bitcoin, and the price is already near $100,000. Both "we are early" and "we missed the boat" cannot be true. You did not miss the boat. We are early. Artists are going to feel the old system fail first, so artists have to understand this before everybody else.

Dion also walks through one practical rail: rolling a retirement account into bitcoin with Unchained. He says it on camera. This is education, not financial, legal, or tax advice.
