◆ New on the BFTA Substack

Hodlers are not enough.

BIP-110 stalled. Adam Back explained why. Both raise concerns worth taking seriously. The deeper truth is that the plebs already own the majority of Bitcoin. They just do not use it.

Over the weekend, a coalition of sound-money Bitcoiners tried to activate BIP-110. The chain mined two blocks and stalled. The next morning, Adam Back, one of the small handful of people whose original work directly informed the Bitcoin whitepaper, posted a single sentence on X: “the economic users control the protocol, via the market. 1000s of nodes with no economic use have no influence.”

This piece takes both sides seriously. BIP-110's concerns about spam, block-space bloat, and rising node cost are real and BFTA shares them. Adam's observation about how governance actually resolves is empirically true. But underneath both sits something more uncomfortable. Individual holders already own roughly eighty percent of the effectively circulating supply of Bitcoin. The institutions that dominate its governance own less than twenty. The problem is not weight. The problem is that the plebs, holding the majority of the world's hardest money, refuse to conduct their economic lives in it.

The fix does not require anyone to buy more Bitcoin. It requires the people who already hold Bitcoin to begin transacting with it, in the ordinary rhythms of their economic lives. Every artist selling a print for sats is an economic user. Every family paying tuition, every merchant taking Lightning, every writer accepting a zap. Change the composition of the economic majority, and everything downstream, including protocol politics, changes with it.

◆ Also on the BFTA Substack

Bitcoin was never supposed to be an investment.

The word “investment” is doing all the damage. Here is how to think about Bitcoin as money, why we still cannot, and why every artist should learn to.

The companion piece to this week's article. Bitcoin was not designed as a share certificate or a stock ticker or a hedge fund product. It was designed as money. The whitepaper says so. Every metaphor that treats Bitcoin as an investment quietly imports the language of the very system Bitcoin was built to make optional, and the more the language wins, the more the technology loses.

Together, these two pieces form a two-part frame BFTA will keep returning to. The first argues Bitcoin's purpose. The second argues Bitcoin's citizenship. Read them in either order.

◆ Share Your Bitcoin Journey  ·  Episode 13

Anik Malcolm.

Depicting Bitcoin  ·  The Whole Entire Universe, 900 hours, 21 million beads  ·  The mountains of central Italy

Classically trained in London, disillusioned in Reykjavik, and now living with his wife and children in the mountains of central Italy, Anik Malcolm is producing some of the most rigorous fine art anywhere in the Bitcoin space today. His practice carries a two-word tagline: Depicting Bitcoin. His most consequential work is a monumental oil painting titled The Whole Entire Universe, which debuted at Bitcoin 2026 in Las Vegas and took over 900 hours to complete.

Twenty-one million beads. A cube of 276 by 276 by 276. Each face gives up exactly 4,096 squares, which turn out to be 64 by 64. The halving pattern of 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2 appears in the image itself. And this only works cleanly at 21 million. Anik walks through the whole discovery on camera, and the episode closes with his own five-minute animation video of the painting, included with permission. Do not skip the outro.
