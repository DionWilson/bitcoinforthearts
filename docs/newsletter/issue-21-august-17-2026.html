◆ Bitcoin Arts Park at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit

Meet the openers.

Stack culture on sound money. Two days. Two noon shows on the Expo Stage. Two artists we are putting on the poster this week.

◆ New on the BFTA Substack

The Missing Word.

A theater veteran wrote the sharpest Broadway postmortem of the year on Cats: The Jellicle Ball. It never once said inflation. That is not his oversight. That is what our entire culture does.

Brian D. Frey did the math in public. Three Tony Awards. 94% average capacity. A million-dollar weekly nut. An eighteen-million-dollar capitalization that needed three and a half years of perfect houses just to get investors back to zero. He is right about every number he names. What the piece never names is the monetary force pulling those numbers apart.

This is not a rebuttal. It is a completion. Artists are never taught to connect rising production costs, vanishing middle-class audiences, and shrinking nonprofit budgets to the currency those budgets are denominated in. Bitcoin for the Arts exists so that the next generation of working artists does not have to spend a career riding that escalator without understanding what it is doing to them.

◆ Q3 Grants

Two weeks to September 1.

The third-quarter grant window is still open. Working artists across every discipline can apply for a Bitcoin micro-grant from $500 to $2,000, paid entirely in sats, to fund a specific project, a new body of work, a piece of equipment, or the time it takes to make something serious. There is no application fee. Grants are reviewed quarterly by the BFTA board.

Submissions close on September 1, 2026. If you are working on something and the money is what stands between you and finishing it, apply. If you know a working artist for whom that sentence is true, forward this email.
