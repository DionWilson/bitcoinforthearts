GALLERY AND FILM.

Dear sovereign renaissance,

Four weeks out. Last week we put faces on the lunch-hour openers: Ainsley Costello on Wednesday and Rock 'n' Roll Andy Breakheart on Thursday. This week we add the next layer of Bitcoin Arts Park: Nadia Vaeh on film, and Shipwreck Shawn on the gallery wall. Plus a new Substack essay that completes another arts story the press told one layer short.

   

◆ Bitcoin Arts Park at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit

What we are building.

Columbus. September 23-24. A park inside the summit where conference energy turns into something you can stand in front of.

Paintings on the wall. A film teaser on a screen. Artists in the room. Noon openers on the Expo Stage. Space for conversation that does not fit a trade-show pitch.

The openers from Issue #21 still stand. Ainsley opens day one. Breakheart opens day two. Across both days, Short North Stage, Proof of Print, and Lindey Magee's children's reading remain on the board. This week's update is the gallery and the film.

Get your pass at midwestbtc.com. Help underwrite the park at bitcoinforthearts.org/midwest.

   

◆ Film at the booth

Nadia Vaeh.

Teaser. Talk. Q&A. Pitch. An independent feature that belongs in the park.

Nadia Vaeh joins us at the Bitcoin Arts Park booth for a conference-length experience around One Poop Scoop Away, the indie feature she stars in (directed by Josh Bedsole). This is not a full feature screening. It is a teaser, a conversation, Q&A, and a pitch-deck walkthrough for anyone who wants to understand how an independent film gets finished and found.

Nadia will also help hold the booth and capture Summit footage while she is there. The live show we talked about is being held for Nashville so Midwest stays focused on film, gallery, and the park itself.

We had her booked for Supporting Your Local Bitcoin Journalist this past week. She got sick, so we are rescheduling the podcast interview. The Midwest booth plan is still on.

Watch the teaser: vimeo.com/jbedsole/opsa

   

◆ Gallery

Shipwreck Shawn.

Paintings you walk up to, not thumbnails you scroll past.

Shipwreck Shawn · Volatility Blues

We mentioned Shawn briefly in the last few issues. This week he gets the space he was owed. His paintings hang in Bitcoin Arts Park for the duration of the summit: Bitcoin-adjacent work that sits on the line between market chaos and human stubbornness.

If you have followed him online, Midwest is the chance to see the paintings as objects. More detail on which pieces hang where as we lock the booth layout.

◆ New on the BFTA Substack

The Gaps in the Dam.

WBEZ told a clear story about healthcare gaps for Chicago dancers. It stopped one layer short of the money question.

Dance for Life is on track to raise half a million dollars for dancers who are uninsured, underinsured, and aging off their parents' plans. Every word of that reporting is correct. The essay is not a rebuttal. It is a completion: healthcare fills gaps in the dam, and money is the water putting pressure on the wall.

Written for dancers holding a lot of things in, and for the people who fund them. Start here if you missed it.

The Throughline This Week

Openers. Gallery. Film. Fund it.

The Gaps in the Dam argues that artists cannot fix what they cannot name about money. Bitcoin Arts Park puts working artists in Columbus on belief more than budget: openers on the stage, Shawn on the wall, Nadia on the screen. The next step is funding, so those same artists leave with a larger following and a stronger career, not only a thank-you.

Name it. Stage it. Hang it. Screen it. Fund the artists. That is how a renaissance compounds.

Until next time.
