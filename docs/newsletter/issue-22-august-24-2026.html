Four weeks out. Last week we put faces on the lunch-hour openers: Ainsley Costello on Wednesday and Rock 'n' Roll Andy Breakheart on Thursday. ◆ Bitcoin Arts Park at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit

What we are building.

Columbus. September 23-24. A park inside the summit where conference energy turns into something you can stand in front of.

Paintings on the wall. A blank canvas filling in public. A film teaser on a screen. Artists in the room. Noon openers on the Expo Stage. Space for conversation that does not fit a trade-show pitch.

The openers from Issue #21 still stand. Ainsley opens day one. Breakheart opens day two. Across both days, Short North Stage, Proof of Print, and Lindey Magee's children's reading remain on the board. This week's update is the gallery, the live painting, and the film.

Get your pass at midwestbtc.com. Help underwrite the park at bitcoinforthearts.org/midwest.

   

◆ Film at the booth

Nadia Vaeh.

Teaser. Talk. Q&A. Pitch. An independent feature that belongs in the park.

Nadia Vaeh

Nadia Vaeh joins us at the Bitcoin Arts Park booth for a conference-length experience around One Poop Scoop Away, the indie feature she stars in (directed by Josh Bedsole). This is not a full feature screening. It is a teaser, a conversation, Q&A, and a pitch-deck walkthrough for anyone who wants to understand how an independent film gets finished and found.

Nadia will also help hold the booth and capture Summit footage while she is there. The live show we talked about is being held for Nashville so Midwest stays focused on film, gallery, and the park itself.

We had her booked for Supporting Your Local Bitcoin Journalist this past week. She got sick, so we are rescheduling the podcast interview. The Midwest booth plan is still on.

Watch the teaser: vimeo.com/jbedsole/opsa

   

◆ Gallery & live painting

Shipwreck Sean.

Finished work on the wall. A blank canvas in the park. Your ideas on the brush.

Shipwreck Sean · Volatility Blues

This is Sean's first proper feature in the newsletter. A draft edit for last week's issue named him briefly, but that edit never shipped. So we are doing it right here.

His finished paintings hang in Bitcoin Arts Park for the duration of the summit: Bitcoin-adjacent work that sits on the line between market chaos and human stubbornness. If you have followed him online, Midwest is the chance to see the paintings as objects.

Shipwreck Sean · gallery detail

The live canvas

Shipwreck Sean · blank canvas, starting point for the live painting

Sean will also paint live at the booth. This blank canvas is the starting point. One thing is already locked: Bitsby, Sean's signature character, will appear somehow, some way. The rest of the canvas is open.

Bitsby · Shipwreck Sean's signature character

He is open to suggestions from the audience for everything else: Bitcoin shapes, objects, depictions, symbols, scenes, jokes, mythologies. Whatever you want to see land on the canvas alongside Bitsby.

Reply to this email with your idea. We will also ask on social. Bring more suggestions in person at the park. Audience input is how we keep people coming back to the booth between sessions.

When the painting is finished, it becomes the raffle prize. Winner announced by 3PM on Thursday, September 24, the second day of the conference. Details on how to enter land closer to the summit. For now: help Sean decide what joins Bitsby on the canvas.

   

◆ New on the BFTA Substack

The Gaps in the Dam.

WBEZ told a clear story about healthcare gaps for Chicago dancers. It stopped one layer short of the money question.

Dance for Life is on track to raise half a million dollars for dancers who are uninsured, underinsured, and aging off their parents' plans. Every word of that reporting is correct. The essay is not a rebuttal. It is a completion: healthcare fills gaps in the dam, and money is the water putting pressure on the wall.

Written for dancers holding a lot of things in, and for the people who fund them. Start here if you missed it.

The Throughline This Week

Suggest it. Watch it land. Fund it.

The Gaps in the Dam argues that artists cannot fix what they cannot name about money. Bitcoin Arts Park puts working artists in Columbus on belief more than budget: openers on the stage, Sean on the wall and the live canvas, Nadia on the screen. The next step is funding, so those same artists leave with a larger following and a stronger career, not only a thank-you.

Name it. Stage it. Hang it. Paint it live. Screen it. Fund the artists. That is how a renaissance compounds.
