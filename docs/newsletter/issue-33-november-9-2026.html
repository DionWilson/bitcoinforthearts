◆ The Work We Stand Behind  ·  Bitcoin Mandala

Art for the next hundred years.

This weekend we sat down with Marcus Woo, founder of the Bitcoin Mandala Foundation. We will go deep into his story when he joins us on the podcast. Today we want you to see the project, because it is exactly the kind of work Bitcoin for the Arts exists for.

The Bitcoin Mandala connects seven sacred places on Earth, from Peru to Kyoto to Antarctica. At each one, a hardware wallet has been ritually buried in the land. Together, the altars form one planetary mandala. Artists are invited to make work inspired by those places: painting, music, poetry, video, and forms no one has imagined yet. Digital works, and records of physical ones, can be stored on-chain, where they become part of Bitcoin's permanent memory.

The horizon is 2140, the year the last new bitcoin is expected to be issued. Marcus is giving twenty or thirty years of his life to a work that people will still be enjoying a hundred years from now.

Why this is our work

Low time preference means choosing what lasts over what is fast. When money cannot be printed away, it makes sense to build for your grandchildren's grandchildren. Before Bitcoin, almost no one could plan culture on that scale. Sound money makes it possible, and artists are the ones who give it form.

These are the projects we want to stand beside, help fund, and teach the world about. This is why we are building the bridge between artists and sound money: so that ideas this big can bring people together and create value for generations.

Want to help? There are two ways. Reach Marcus directly through the Bitcoin Mandala site. Or write to us at hello@bitcoinforthearts.org and tell us you want to help through Bitcoin for the Arts. We will be in touch.

◆ Share Your Bitcoin Journey  ·  Episode 19

Bitbrand: Bitcoin Saved My Life

Episode 19 is out. Our guest is Bitbrand, the maker behind the Bitcoin Branding Iron. He works the old way: steel, fire, and leather. No ink, no printer, and no two pieces alike. We met at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit in Columbus.

We taped it in the back of his van in New Jersey, at a table he built himself. He tells us how a branding iron became a business, why he open-sourced his own invention, and how he runs a maker shop on a Bitcoin standard.

It is also our Halloween special. Stay for the middle, when two very scary central bankers show up.
