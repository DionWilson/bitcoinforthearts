◆ Share Your Bitcoin Journey, Episode 11

Alana Mediavilla.

Puerto Rican filmmaker  ·  Director of Dirty Coin  ·  Founder of Campo Libre  ·  First English-language film to win Best National Documentary in Puerto Rico

Episode 11 of Share Your Bitcoin Journey is live. Alana Mediavilla, the Puerto Rican filmmaker behind Dirty Coin, joins us for our longest and most substantive conversation to date. Two hours on Silicon Valley, sovereignty, mining, and independent cinema on a Bitcoin standard.

The headlines:

  • From Google Cloud to independent cinema. Ten years inside Big Tech producing for Google, Apple, PayPal, Volkswagen, and Volvo. Walked away in 2020, moved her family home to Puerto Rico, and built her own production company, Campo Libre, in San Juan.
  • Three Bitcoin at $200, and a lesson she never forgot. Bought BTC in 2015. Found out at a Starbucks, on the day Bitcoin hit $10,000, that her husband had already sold all three at $600. Her first lesson in not your keys, not your coins.
  • A documentary financed in Bitcoin. One BTC donation received at roughly $40,000 became $70,000 of production spend before the film was even finished. That is the math fiat never gives you.
  • What Malawi taught her that Texas could not. Bitcoin mining as the tool that unlocks stranded renewable energy in landlocked countries, and lets rural grids get built out without predatory debt.
  • 500 screenings in 200 cities. Community-hosted distribution through Kinema, aimed at meetup leaders instead of the general public. Won Best National Documentary in Puerto Rico as the first English-language film ever to do so.

◆ The Week in Arts Funding

Two beats worth watching.

The NEA July 2026 grant cycle is open. The National Endowment for the Arts opened its second major funding round of the year. Full applications are due Tuesday, July 21. That is eight days from today. For any artist in our audience working through a 501(c)(3) fiscal sponsor, or any small arts organization ready to apply directly, this is the most immediate public dollar available in the country right now. Read the guidelines at arts.gov/grants.

Christie's hosts its Art+Tech Summit this Thursday. On July 16 at Rockefeller Center, Christie's convenes Wall Street, technology, and the traditional art market for its 2026 Art+Tech Summit. The program looks at how emerging technology, including blockchain and digital assets, is reshaping collecting and value in the art world. The auction house world is beginning to notice at scale what Bitcoin For The Arts has been saying since day one: technology is not a threat to art, it is the new patronage rail. That the conversation is happening at Christie's and not at BFTA is fine by us. We would rather build the thing than debate it.

Two beats, one lesson. The old rails are still writing the checks, and the new rails are starting to make it into the same rooms.
