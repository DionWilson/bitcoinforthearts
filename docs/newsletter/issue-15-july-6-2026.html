◆ The New York Record

A record public budget, still eaten by the currency.

On June 30, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and City Council Speaker Julie Menin reached a handshake agreement on New York City's 2027 budget. The headline number is real and worth naming: $323.8 million for culture, the largest allocation the Department of Cultural Affairs has ever received, up from last year's record of $299.6 million. The city also created a new Cultural Stability Fund: $10 million per year for the next three years to help arts organizations experiencing unexpected or emergency circumstances. Mamdani called artists the beating heart of the city.

Now sit with the math. The total New York City budget for 2027 is $125.8 billion. The record culture allocation is 0.26 percent of the city budget. The council's preliminary plan had culture at only $216 million; the handshake pushed it 50 percent higher because the previous number was not enough. The new Cultural Stability Fund exists because even at $323 million, arts organizations are still going to need emergency money. And it was all announced in the same week as a rent freeze for stabilized apartments, because the same mayor knows perfectly well that his artists cannot afford to stay in the city he governs.

This is the third fiat arts funding story we have brought to you in as many issues. In Issue #14 we ran the Royal Ballet cuts in London alongside the Manhattan Borough President's $50 million discretionary bandage. Different cities, different politicians, same pattern. Every headline is a record. Every record is followed by a new emergency fund. Every emergency fund is denominated in a currency that has lost purchasing power every year of Mayor Mamdani's life, and every year of his predecessors, and will continue to lose purchasing power throughout the three-year Cultural Stability Fund window.

Bitcoin for the Arts is not against the record. Mamdani is doing what a mayor can do. Menin is doing what a council can do. The Cultural Stability Fund is a serious act of public will. And it will still not be enough. Because there is one thing a city cannot do, and it is the thing that matters most: a city cannot fix the money. That is Bitcoin's job. And that is exactly the parallel rail we are building.

◆ Midwest Bitcoin Summit

Bitcoin Arts Park is locked in.

BFTA's dedicated cultural footprint at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit on September 23 and 24 in Columbus is now programming end-to-end.
