◆ Arts funding

Digitize the archive. Then fund what lasts.

NYPL Library for the Performing Arts · $60M · largest gift in its history.

This month the New York Public Library for the Performing Arts at Lincoln Center received an anonymous $60 million gift, the largest single donation in the research library's history. The money goes to preserve, catalog, and digitize a roughly 15 million-item archive of theater, dance, and music: rare manuscripts, still photography, fragile lacquer discs and wax cylinders, prompt books, and set models that have been too delicate to circulate for decades.

We find this interesting for a reason that goes past the headline number. As art and life move deeper into the information age, institutions are finally treating digital access as infrastructure, not a side project. Digitization is how a Sondheim set model in a vault becomes something the world can study. We appreciate what the Library for the Performing Arts is doing for the arts, and we are glad we are not alone in thinking forward about what culture can look like from here.

The invitation, from our corner: once the work is digitized and preserved, invite the next layer. Digital sound money beside digital preservation. Bitcoin as the settlement and patronage rail that can travel with the archive, pay the living artist, and keep the cultural record funded without waiting on the next anonymous miracle alone.
