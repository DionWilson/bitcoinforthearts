◆ The September Pivot

Why we're going to Columbus instead of DC.

For three months, we've been planning Bitcoin Art Night at PubKey DC for September 21. Then a door opened we couldn't ignore.

Our advisor Kyle Knight — a producer and curator with deep roots in the Bitcoin culture world — has been named Art Director of the gallery floor at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit (Columbus, OH, Sept 23–24). He's curating a 60×60 installation called Generations, with anchor partnerships from the Human Rights Foundation, Bitcoin Trading Cards, and BSN. His curatorial brief is the most serious cultural document any U.S. Bitcoin conference has assembled to date.

Kyle invited Bitcoin for the Arts in not as a sponsor, but as a co-curatorial partner. Stage rights, podcast booth space, a panel slot, and a path to guaranteed artist payments are on the table. PubKey DC will move to October or November as a smaller, fully BFTA-owned night without the calendar pressure.

For three years, working artists have shown up to Bitcoin conferences for "exposure" and walked away financially upside-down. We are not joining that pattern. We are breaking it.

That commitment is the spine of how we're approaching Generations. The full operational plan is being finalized this month with Kyle, the conference, and our advisory board, but the conditions we will not bend on are already locked:

  • Artists are paid first, in writing, before any production dollar is spent.
  • A staffed Lightning onboarding station on the gallery floor — a real person whose job is to walk first-time users through real on-the-spot transactions.
  • Dedicated music programming windows with the gallery acoustically separated from the speaker hall.
  • A BFTA mission panel, plus stage time for participating artists to share their Bitcoin journeys.
  • Visible BFTA curatorial branding — named on physical signage, digital, and press, not subordinated to anyone else's logo.

If those conditions are met, this is BFTA's coming-out moment. If they're not, we walk and PubKey DC stays on. Either way, our subscribers are the first to know — that's why this email exists.

◆ The Long Read  ·  New on Substack

"The Money Is There."

Our newest Substack piece went live this weekend. It's the long version of why we're approaching the Midwest Summit the way we are — and the structural argument for why the gap in arts funding is a bridge problem, not a capital problem.

Christie's sold $1.1 billion of art in one night last week. Bank of America's new report says working artists are the most fragile part of the market. Both things are true. Together they describe the gap a different kind of arts funding has to close.

The piece walks through the math: BFTA's annual budget to fund 50 working artists is a rounding error inside a single Christie's lot. The capital exists. The connective tissue between historical-collector wealth and working artists is what's missing — and that's the bridge BFTA was built to be.

It's also the long version of why every sponsor dollar this September is structural, not symbolic. If you read one piece from us this month, read this one.

◆ Share Your Bitcoin Journey — Episode 6

Joe Martin.

21st century troubadour  ·  Isle of Man  ·  Americana / country  ·  Value-for-value

Episode 6 of Share Your Bitcoin Journey is live. UK-based singer-songwriter Joe Martin — Isle of Man-based, Nashville-recorded, Bitcoin-paid — joined us for one of the most honest conversations we've had on the show about what it actually costs to make real art in the fiat era, and what Bitcoin and Nostr change for an independent musician trying to do it the right way.

The headlines from the conversation:

  • $100 in the first week. Joe uploaded his first song on RSS and earned more in seven days than years of Spotify streaming had given him. The moment Bitcoin clicked.
  • "I made my album without any AI." Joe's new record Alone in Valentine was made deliberately as a final pre-AI human document — a two-year process, a halfway-around-the-world Nashville session with Cal Campbell and Cornelius Webb, and most of his savings.
  • AI slop and inflation are parallel debasements. One eats your money. The other eats your art. Joe makes the parallel as clearly as anyone we've heard.
  • Sats over Spotify. Joe sells merch and music for sats on the Nostr marketplace — including limited vinyl that Grimey's Records in Nashville now stocks for U.S. fans.
  • Advice for younger songwriters: Get on Nostr first. Then RSS. Then save the sats.

◆ From Albany

New York's 2026–27 budget passes. Arts & culture is in it.

The New York State Senate passed the SFY 2026–27 budget, with a named "Arts, Culture & Recreation" section in the appropriations alongside Education, Healthcare, Housing, Environmental Stewardship, and the rest of the state's structural priorities.

That arts and culture has its own budget category — at all, named, in the same paragraph as the priorities the state cannot legally avoid funding — is meaningful in 2026. Half a dozen U.S. cities and states are quietly cutting their arts agencies as ARPA stimulus money expires; New York keeping arts visible at the appropriations level is a position, not an accident.

We're going to read the actual line items carefully and publish a clean breakdown on our research portal at bitcoinforthearts.org/research — what New York is funding, what it isn't, and how it compares against last year's allocation and against the cities now walking off the ARPA cliff.
