## HOST INTRO (spoken on camera before Q1)

Same structure as prior episodes: constant opener, guest framing, one-line
"in this conversation" tease, handoff.

  Welcome to another episode of Share Your Bitcoin Journey presented by
  Bitcoin for the Arts. I am Dion Wilson, the founder and executive director
  for Bitcoin for the Arts, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit empowering artists with
  Bitcoin grants, education, and sovereign funding tools.

  Today we are joined by Nadia Vaeh, an award-winning recording artist and
  filmmaker whose music has always been about more than entertainment. An
  Atlanta native building a career across songwriting, touring, and her
  label Galactic Cat Records, Nadia has spent more than a decade living the
  independent-artist grind: writing, performing, building businesses, and
  stacking the side hustles most creatives never put on the poster.

  That lived experience became the spark for her first feature documentary,
  a film that asks the question thousands of artists carry quietly: what
  does it actually mean to "make it" in today's music industry? Rather than
  chasing celebrity, she is documenting the resilience, sacrifice, and
  unconventional paths of artists, producers, managers, and industry voices
  who keep choosing the work. The feature is still completing interviews,
  with editing ahead, and a teaser already in hand.

  This September she joins Bitcoin for the Arts at the Midwest Bitcoin
  Summit in Columbus, presenting that teaser and a live talk inside our
  Bitcoin Arts Park booth, capturing new footage on site for the film, and
  standing with us as a working artist in the room.

  In this conversation we talk about her path from music as a lifeline to
  filmmaker documenting the industry from the inside, what independent
  artists are really navigating right now, how Bitcoin enters a creative
  life built on honesty and survival, and what it means to bring this story
  into a Bitcoin conference audience that needs to meet real artists, not
  abstracts.

  Without further ado, welcome to the show, Nadia Vaeh.

## BACKGROUND & JOURNEY

1. Nadia, welcome to Share Your Bitcoin Journey. Before the documentary,
before Galactic Cat Records, before the Midwest Summit booth, there was a
kid who needed music. Where does that story actually start for you?

2. A lot of artists can name the moment music stopped being a hobby and
became necessary. For you, what was the turning point when songwriting
became a lifeline, not just a craft?

3. You have spoken about losing your mother at seventeen, and about music
giving language to grief when words fell short. As much as you want to
share here, how did that shape the artist and the filmmaker you became?

4. More than a decade of independent work: touring, writing, performing,
building businesses, side hustles. For listeners who only see the stage
version, what does that decade actually look like day to day?

5. You ask a question a lot of artists carry quietly: what does it mean to
"make it" today? When did that question get loud enough that it had to
become a film?

---

## THE FILM

6. Walk us into the documentary. What story are you telling, and why does
it need to be told from inside the industry rather than from a critic's
seat?

7. You are both filmmaker and participant, living the story while you
document it. How do you hold that double role without losing honesty or
empathy in the interviews?

8. Who have you been sitting with: artists, producers, managers,
entrepreneurs, industry leaders? What pattern keeps showing up that the
public never sees?

9. The feature is still finishing interviews, with editing ahead and a
teaser ready now. Where is the film in its life cycle, and what are you
hoping the finished cut does in the world?

10. At the heart of it you suggest "making it" might not be a destination
at all. After everything you have filmed so far, what does "making it"
mean to you right now?

---

## MONEY, INDUSTRY & THE ARTIST ECONOMY

11. Independent artists live inside streaming math, touring costs, and
side-hustle stacks. From your seat, what is broken in how the industry
pays the people who actually make the work?

12. You have advocated around songwriter rights and organizations like
SONA. What should a working songwriter understand about compensation that
nobody teaches in the beginning?

13. Your songs have been paired with real causes, Alliance of Hope, Peace
Over Violence, Human Rights Campaign and others. How do you think about
art as both livelihood and service without burning out the artist?

14. Mental health is not a side topic in your work. In an industry that
runs on hustle and public performance, what does sustainable creativity
actually require?

---

## THE BITCOIN TURN

15. How did Bitcoin first enter your world: a person, a payment, a
conference, a broken system you were already documenting?

16. Every guest has a moment where Bitcoin stopped being a headline and
became personal. As someone who has lived the indie-artist economy, what
was yours?

17. Bitcoin for the Arts exists because artists were never taught how the
money system works underneath their careers. What part of that education
do you wish you had gotten ten years ago?

18. Your film is about resilience and unconventional paths. Where do you
see Bitcoin fitting inside that story: tool, thesis, subplot, or something
you are still testing?

---

## MIDWEST BITCOIN SUMMIT & BITCOIN ARTS PARK

19. This September you are with us at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit inside
Bitcoin Arts Park. Why bring a music-industry documentary into a Bitcoin
conference booth?

20. The plan is a timed summary, the teaser or portions of the film, Q&A,
and your pitch deck on site. What do you want someone who stops at the
booth for ten minutes to walk away understanding?

21. You are also capturing Summit footage for the documentary. What do you
hope a Bitcoin conference room reveals that a music-industry room might
miss?

22. You will spend time helping at the Bitcoin for the Arts booth, not
only presenting your own work. What does it mean to you to stand with
other artists in that space?
