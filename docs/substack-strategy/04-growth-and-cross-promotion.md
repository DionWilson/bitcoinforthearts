# Growth + Cross-Promotion Tactics

Specific, ranked tactics to grow BFTA Substack subscriber count and engagement. Ordered by ROI on Dion's time, highest first.

## Tactic 1 — Substack Notes (highest ROI for time invested)

**The mechanism:** Notes is Substack's native social feed, similar to Twitter/X. Substack's algorithm aggressively recommends Notes to users browsing the For You feed — and Notes drive the highest discovery-to-subscribe conversion of any Substack growth channel.

**The plan:**
- **3 Notes per week minimum** (Mon / Thu / Sat per the calendar)
- **Engage with 5–10 other Notes per day** in the Bitcoin + nonprofit + arts niches — like, restack, thoughtful 1-line replies. ~10 minutes daily.
- **Restack interesting Notes from other publications** with a 1-line BFTA angle added — Substack's algorithm rewards this, and other publications will reciprocate.
- **Convert each longform article into 2–3 Notes** in the days after publishing — pull quotes, key statistics, "the part of the article you should screenshot" highlights.

**Time investment:** 15 minutes per Note + 10 minutes/day general engagement = 1.5 hours/week
**Expected impact:** 60–80% of new subscribers in the first 6 months will come from Notes.

## Tactic 2 — Reciprocal cross-posts with adjacent publications

**The mechanism:** Substack's "recommend" feature lets publications cross-promote. A recommendation from a publication with 5,000 subscribers can drive 50–200 new BFTA subscribers depending on overlap.

**Target publications to recommend BFTA (and reciprocate):**

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Bitcoin nonprofit / education Substacks** | Bitcoin Park, Bitcoin Beach, BTC++ Education, Plebs Magazine |
| **Arts criticism / culture Substacks** | Anyone writing about arts funding (Hyperallergic adjacent voices, indie arts critics on Substack) |
| **Bitcoin philosophy + culture writers** | Lyn Alden adjacent, Allen Farrington adjacent, sound money + culture writers |
| **Featured artist Substacks** | Any of BFTA's featured artists who run their own Substack — easiest yes |
| **Nonprofit founder peer Substacks** | Other small 501(c)(3) founders writing about building |

**Process:**
1. Identify 10 candidate publications
2. Read 3 articles from each before reaching out (you'll find natural angles)
3. Reach out individually with: "Hey, I run BFTA's Substack. Loved your piece on [X]. Would you be open to a mutual recommendation?"
4. **Lead with a thoughtful comment on their work**, not the ask
5. After they accept, write a small "publications I read" mention in your next article featuring 2–3 of these reciprocal recommenders

**Realistic target:** 5 reciprocal recommendations within 2 months. Each one is 50–200 new subscribers over the year.

**Time investment:** ~1 hour per outreach (read, write, follow up) × 10 outreaches = 10 hours over 4 weeks. Then ongoing maintenance is minimal.

## Tactic 3 — Convert Beehiiv newsletter readers into Substack subscribers

**The mechanism:** Beehiiv readers already trust BFTA. They just don't know about the Substack. Each Beehiiv newsletter should drive a portion of readers to subscribe to the Substack.

**The plan:**
- **Add a permanent Substack CTA** in every Beehiiv newsletter footer: "Read longer-form analysis at bitcoinforthearts.substack.com — new piece every Wednesday + Friday"
- **In each Beehiiv newsletter, feature 1–2 recent Substack articles** with strong pull quotes ("This week on the Substack: [headline]. Read more →")
- **Periodically, send a Beehiiv-only piece** that's a teaser for an upcoming Substack longform — drives anticipation and clicks

**Reverse direction also matters:** every Substack article should mention the Beehiiv newsletter at the end — "Want operational updates and event news? Subscribe to our Beehiiv newsletter at bitcoinforthearts.org/subscribe."

**Realistic target:** 10–15% of active Beehiiv readers convert to Substack subscribers within 6 months if this is consistent.

## Tactic 5 — Strategic Notes engagement with sponsors + potential sponsors

**The mechanism:** Most Bitcoin-native company CMOs and founders are on Substack. Engaging with their work positions BFTA in their feed.

**The plan:**
- Identify 20 Bitcoin-native companies BFTA wants as sponsors
- Find which of their leadership/marketing are on Substack
- Subscribe to those publications + leave thoughtful Notes engagement on their content periodically
- After 2–4 weeks of genuine engagement (not flattery), reach out about Art + Zap Weekend sponsorship

**This is slow-burn relationship building, not direct selling.** It compounds over months. The goal is BFTA being a name they recognize when the cold sponsor email lands.

## Tactic 6 — Comment on every major arts-funding crisis story in the news

**The mechanism:** When an NEA cut or a museum layoff hits the press, journalists, advocates, and other nonprofits flood the comment sections of those articles + the social posts about them. Showing up there with substantive comments + a link to relevant BFTA writing puts BFTA in front of an audience already on the topic.

- Bookmark NYT Arts, Hyperallergic, Inside Philanthropy comment sections
- Within 24 hours of a major story, write 1 thoughtful comment that links to the relevant BFTA piece
- Repeat on the social posts (Twitter, LinkedIn) of those articles

**Time:** 30 minutes per major story. Maybe 4–6 major stories per month.

## Tactic 7 — Paid promotion (only if the budget supports it)

**Only after Tactics 1–6 are running consistently for 3 months.** Paid promotion compounds organic, doesn't substitute for it.

| Channel | Budget recommendation | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| **Substack Boost (paid recommendations from larger pubs)** | $200–$500 one-time | A short campaign of paid recommendations from larger publications — useful for breaking out of the cold-start phase |
| **Twitter/X promoted Notes** | $100–$300/month | Targeted at Bitcoin + arts overlap audiences |
| **Nostr zap-promoted notes** | Variable, low cost | Effective in the v4v ecosystem |

**Total recommended paid spend:** $500–$1,000/quarter, only after organic is working. **Not a priority for the first 3 months.**

## What NOT to do

- ❌ **Don't buy email lists.** Quality of the audience matters more than size. Bought lists hurt the algorithm and damage sender reputation.
- ❌ **Don't auto-DM every new follower** with a "thanks for following!" message. Spammy, low-conversion, hurts brand.
- ❌ **Don't trade subscribes for subscribes** with random other small publications you haven't read. The "I'll subscribe to yours, you subscribe to mine" model gives you a vanity number and zero engaged readers.
- ❌ **Don't post the same article verbatim to LinkedIn / Medium / Mirror / etc** — Substack penalizes duplicate content from the SEO side and you cannibalize your own discovery
- ❌ **Don't change publishing cadence to chase a viral moment.** A consistent Wednesday + Friday rhythm is more valuable than 3 articles in one week and silence the next.

## Success metrics — what to track

Set up a simple weekly tracking sheet (Google Sheets, Notion, whatever):

| Metric | Where to find it | Weekly target |
|---|---|---|
| Total subscribers | Substack admin | +5–15/week consistently |
| Open rate | Substack admin | >40% (industry average is 20–25%) |
| Click rate | Substack admin | >5% |
| Notes engagement (likes + restacks) | Substack notes | 10+ per Note posted |
| New cross-post recommendations landed | Manual count | 1–2 per month |
| Substack-attributed event signups (Art + Zap inquiries from Substack readers) | Sponsor inquiry tracking | Trend up over time |
| Sovereign Circle signups attributed to Substack | UTM tracking on links | Trend up over time |

**Review this sheet every Sunday** when planning next week's content. Over 12 weeks the trends tell you what's working.

## The 3-month decision point

After 3 months of consistent execution, evaluate:

| If after 3 months... | Then... |
|---|---|
| Subscriber growth >50% AND open rate >40% | Continue current cadence, start exploring paid promotion (Tactic 7) |
| Subscriber growth flat AND Notes engagement low | Re-evaluate content angles — probably too generic. Get sharper on newsjacking. |
| Subscriber growth healthy BUT no Sovereign Circle / sponsor conversions | Audit the CTAs at the end of each article. Make them more specific and visible. |
| Cadence is breaking down (missed Wednesdays or Fridays) | Cut to 1 article/week from contributor only, run Dion's longform every other week. Better to publish less and keep the rhythm than promise 2/week and miss weeks. |
