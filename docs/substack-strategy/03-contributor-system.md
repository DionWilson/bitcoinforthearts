# Contributor System — How to Hit 2 Articles/Week Without Burning Out

The cadence in `01` is unsustainable solo. The Friday article must come from a contributor. This doc covers: who to recruit, how to invite them, what to give them, what BFTA gets back, and how to maintain editorial quality.

**Realistic target: 4 of these 10 commit to 1 article/month.** That's enough to fill the Friday slot consistently with rotation.

### Tier 2 — Mission-aligned outsiders worth pitching (lower yes rate, higher growth value)

These are people with audiences who would benefit from co-publishing on BFTA's Substack. The "what's in it for them" is exposure to BFTA's growing arts audience.

| Type | Examples to approach |
|---|---|
| **Bitcoin podcasters in your outreach pipeline** | Guest op-ed → cross-promotion to their show audience |
| **Bitcoin Magazine writers** | A guest piece on arts funding → bylined in BFTA Substack with their bio |
| **Hyperallergic-style arts writers who get Bitcoin** | Rare but they exist — find via Twitter/Nostr search |
| **Nonprofit founders in adjacent spaces** | Bitcoin Park, Bitcoin Beach, BTC++ Education, similar — guest pieces about parallel work |
| **Academic/research voices** | Anyone publishing on art + economics or art + crypto research |

**Realistic target: 1 outsider piece per month.** Higher effort to land but each one is a growth multiplier.

### Tier 3 — Featured artists themselves

Every artist BFTA funds becomes a potential contributor. Build it into the grant program — when an artist receives a micro-grant, the offer is "we'd love to publish a 600–800 word reflection on your work in our Substack within 30 days, in exchange for [fee or in-kind, see below]."

## What contributors get in exchange

This is the key to making the contributor system work. Voluntary contributors flake; compensated contributors deliver.

### Compensation options (tiered, your call per contributor)

| Tier | What they get | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **Volunteer** | Public byline + bio + link to their own work + permanent BFTA Substack archive credit + FMV acknowledgment letter for their records | Default for board members + advisors + featured artists already in the org |
| **In-kind** | Above + amplification on BFTA's Beehiiv newsletter + Nostr + X + LinkedIn channels | For Tier 2 outsiders who care more about audience reach than money |
| **Modest paid** | Above + $100–$250 per accepted article in BTC | For external journalists / writers / academics where this is part of how they live |

**Recommended budget for paid contributors:** $500–$1,000/quarter from the 30% Programs bucket. Funds 4–8 paid pieces per quarter without straining the budget. Optional, not required.

### Things every contributor gets, regardless of compensation

- **Public byline + author bio** at the top of the article
- **Link to their own work** prominently in the byline (their site, their Substack, their handle)
- **Permanent BFTA Substack archive credit** — their article lives forever with their name on it
- **Amplification** — BFTA shares the article on Nostr, X, and the next Beehiiv newsletter
- **FMV acknowledgment letter** for their contributed services (see `docs/art-zap-weekend/06-podcaster-acknowledgment-letter-fillable.md` — same template adapted)
- **Editorial respect** — Dion edits for clarity, but the contributor's voice and conclusions are theirs. No ghostwriting. No bait-and-switch on the angle.

## Contributor Invitation Email — Template

Use this as the first contact. Personalize the first paragraph.

**Subject:** Would you write a 600–1,200 word piece for the BFTA Substack?

Hi [Name],

[1–2 sentence personal intro — reference their work / your prior conversation / a piece they wrote that's stuck with you. Non-negotiable. Generic outreach gets ignored.]

I'm reaching out because we're growing the Bitcoin For The Arts Substack into a real publication — covering the arts-funding crisis as it happens, telling the BFTA mission story, and building an audience for our flagship event in September. The two pieces we've already published (`The Lights Are Going Out` and `Is War Good for Bitcoin?`) give a sense of the editorial voice.

I'd love to commission a contributor piece from you. Specifically:

**The angle:** [describe the specific angle you want them to write about — be concrete. Vague invitations get vague yeses that turn into no-shows. Example: "I'd love a 800-word piece from you on what it actually feels like to receive your first Bitcoin grant — the Lightning ping, the moment you realized this money has different properties than dollars, what changed about how you think about your work."]

**Length:** 600–1,200 words. Shorter is better.

**Deadline:** [Specific date — give them 3 weeks. Friday-of-publication is the post date; first draft due 10 days before.]

**Compensation:** [Pick the right tier from the doc, name it explicitly. "$200 in BTC on publication" / "FMV acknowledgment letter + amplification across all BFTA channels" / etc. Don't be vague about money or expectations.]

**What you get beyond the compensation:**
- Public byline + bio + a prominent link to your work
- Permanent placement in the BFTA Substack archive
- Amplification on our Beehiiv newsletter (~[N] subscribers), Nostr (~[N] followers), X, LinkedIn
- A written FMV acknowledgment letter for your records (services aren't tax-deductible per IRS Pub 526, but the letter is useful for your own marketing one-sheet, sponsor pitches, year-end PR)

**My commitment:**
- I edit for clarity and concision but never for conclusions. Your voice and your angle stay intact.
- I send the edited version back for your approval before publishing.
- I respect your time — if you say yes, I deliver every promise above.

Would you be in? If yes, just reply with "in" and we'll set up a 15-minute call to talk through the specific angle and the practical stuff (when to expect the edit pass, what your byline link should point to, etc.).

If this isn't a fit but you know someone who'd be perfect — send them my way.

Thanks for considering it,

Dion

## Contributor Brief — Template (sent after they say yes)

Once a contributor is in, send them this brief so they have everything they need without you babysitting.

**Subject:** Your BFTA Substack contribution — brief + deadlines

Hi [Name],

Confirming the details so we're aligned:

**Article angle:** [restate]
**Working title:** [propose 1–2 — they can change]
**Length:** 600–1,200 words
**Voice:** Direct, specific, ends with a clear point. No vague platitudes. Reference at least one concrete person, event, or number.
**Deadline for first draft:** [date — 10 days before publish date]
**Edit pass back to you:** within 48 hours of receiving your draft
**Final approval from you:** within 48 hours of receiving the edit
**Publish date:** [Friday date]
**Amplification:** I'll cross-post to Nostr + X within 4 hours of publishing, and feature it in the next Beehiiv newsletter (going out [date])
**Compensation:** [restate exactly what they get]

**What I need from you:**

1. **The article draft** by [deadline]
2. **Author bio** (50–100 words) — your name, what you do, where to find you, one link
3. **Headshot** (high-res, square crop preferred for the Substack author profile)
4. **Your Substack username** (if you have one — for cross-promotion)
5. **Any links you want included in the byline area** (your work, your site, social handles)
6. **Your Lightning address** — for compensation payment in BTC (if applicable to your tier)

**A few editorial notes that always apply:**

- BFTA's house style is "Bitcoin For The Arts" (capitalized, no italics) on first use, then "BFTA" thereafter
- Use the term "Bitcoin-funded fundraiser" not "Bitcoin-native fundraiser" when referring to the Art + Zap Weekend (per board direction on positioning — it's a fundraiser FOR BFTA, not real-time direct patronage)
- Reference our public 55/30/10/5 allocation when discussing donations
- If you mention specific dollar amounts, also mention sat amounts (BFTA convention to bridge audiences)

**Anything I should know about your writing process?** Tell me how you like to work — I adapt to your style, not the other way around.

Looking forward to it.

Dion

## Editorial standards — the bar

A contributor article passes when it has:

- A clear thesis stated in the first 3 paragraphs
- At least one specific reference (a person, event, statistic) — not generic
- At least one BFTA mission tie-in — natural, not bolted-on
- A clear next-step CTA at the end (subscribe, donate, attend Art + Zap, share)
- Reads in under 8 minutes
- The contributor's voice — not Dion's, not BFTA's institutional voice

A contributor article fails when:

- It's a generic essay that could appear on any nonprofit blog
- It contradicts BFTA's published positions without acknowledging the disagreement
- It pitches a sponsor or partner the contributor has financial interest in without disclosure
- It's just a thinly-veiled promotion of the contributor's own work

When you receive a draft that fails: send it back with specific notes. Don't try to ghostwrite the fix. The contributor has to do their own work to clear the bar.

## How many contributors to recruit total

| Cadence component | # contributors needed |
|---|---|
| Friday article every week (52/year) | 6–8 active contributors rotating |
| Backup pool for cancellations | 3–4 standby contributors |
| Pipeline for new outsider voices | 1–2 in active conversation per month |

**Initial recruitment target for the next 6 weeks:** 4 commitments from the Tier 1 list (board members + advisors + featured artists who'll write 1/month).

If you only land 2 — fine, you publish 1 contributor article every other Friday and run a Dion piece in alternating Friday slots. Cadence still works. Don't force a contributor article when there's no contributor.

## Tracking

Build a simple spreadsheet (or Google Sheet tab) with these columns:

| Contributor | Tier | Article angle | Status (invited / committed / drafting / submitted / published) | Publish date | Compensation tier | FMV letter sent? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Update weekly. Surfaces exactly who's behind, who's ready, and who needs a follow-up.
