# Bitcoin Was Never Supposed to Be an Investment

*The word "investment" is doing all the damage. Here is how to think about Bitcoin as money, why we still cannot, and why every artist should learn to.*

**Bitcoin for the Arts · Substack draft · for publication week of August 4, 2026**

## TITLE

Bitcoin Was Never Supposed to Be an Investment

## SUBTITLE

The word "investment" is doing all the damage. Here is how to think about Bitcoin as money, why we still cannot, and why every artist should learn to.

---

## BODY

Yesterday on LinkedIn, someone with the best of intentions wrote a long, careful post explaining to a beginner named Nelson how to think about Bitcoin.

The post was thoughtful. It said all the right things about not chasing candles, not investing money you cannot afford to lose, not trying to time the market, learning about security before you scale up. It ended with a line most Bitcoiners would nod at: "Your first goal shouldn't be to make money from Bitcoin. Your first goal should be to understand Bitcoin."

Read it once and you would think it was a good introduction.

Read it a second time and you would notice that every single sentence assumed the same thing. Bitcoin is an investment. Bitcoin has a price. Bitcoin is volatile. Bitcoin sits in a portfolio next to other things you allocate. Bitcoin needs a "strategy." The correction to bad Bitcoin advice is better Bitcoin investment advice.

That is the entire problem. That is why the message is not landing. And that is what every artist reading this newsletter needs to understand before we go any further.

---

### What is money supposed to do?

Open an introductory economics textbook and it will tell you that money does three things. It serves as a unit of account, so that we can price a chicken and a coat and a haircut on the same scale. It serves as a medium of exchange, so that we can trade the chicken for the coat without a barter negotiation. And it serves as a store of value, so that we can carry the chicken forward in time without having to actually keep a chicken.

That is money's job description. It is a tool. It measures. It moves value. It carries value forward.

Nothing in that job description says money should go up in price. Nothing says money should be volatile. Nothing says money should require a strategy, a portfolio, or an allocation.

A ruler does not become more or less itself when the thing being measured changes size. A ruler measures. A ruler does its job. If your ruler kept growing and shrinking every day, you would not blame the table you were measuring. You would blame the ruler.

That is what fiat currency is. A ruler that will not sit still.

---

### The pathology of a moving yardstick

Fiat currencies are not designed to hold their value. They are designed to be managed. The central authorities that issue them have specific tools they use to expand and contract the supply, keeping the currency inside a target range that serves the political and economic goals of the moment.

Those tools have names. Open market operations. Quantitative easing. Interest-rate adjustments. Reserve requirements. Credit expansion into the banking system through bonds, treasuries, and repurchase agreements. There is a whole architecture, and its purpose is to move the ruler.

When the ruler moves, everything measured against it appears to move too.

The chicken did not get more valuable. The dollar got less valuable, and now the chicken looks like it costs more. That is inflation. It is a purely monetary phenomenon, but it presents itself to the general public as a phenomenon of things. Rent went up. Milk went up. Concert tickets went up. Rarely is it explained the other way. Nothing went up. The measurement contracted.

Once the ruler is moving, every human being holding the currency is forced into a defensive posture. If you hold cash, you are losing value every year. Not because you did anything wrong, but because the ruler shrunk while you were holding it. So the culture invents a mechanism to compensate.

That mechanism is investment.

You must "invest" your money so that it grows fast enough to outrun the ruler's contraction. You must "allocate" between different asset classes so that the ruler cannot get all of you at once. You must "diversify" so that no single ruler contraction wipes you out. You must have a "strategy." You must build a "portfolio." You must "rebalance."

None of this is because it is good for you.

All of this is because the ruler is moving.

The entire investment industry, the entire personal finance industry, the entire retirement industry, the entire "here is what your money should be doing for you" industry, is downstream of one single design choice: a monetary system whose yardstick is politically managed and therefore cannot sit still.

If the ruler sat still, none of it would need to exist.

---

### Enter Bitcoin

Bitcoin is a ruler that sits still.

Its supply is fixed forever at 21 million units. Its issuance schedule is public and immutable. Its rules cannot be changed by an authority in a boardroom. Its value in the marketplace is set by the aggregate opinion of every person on Earth who chooses to hold or transact in it, and only by them, without any single actor able to expand or contract the supply.

That is not an asset class. That is a functioning ruler.

The reason the price of Bitcoin measured in dollars looks so volatile is not that Bitcoin is unstable. It is that the dollar is unstable. When a ruler that will not stop shrinking is used to measure a ruler that does not move, the not-moving ruler will look, from inside the shrinking system, like it is exploding upward.

It is not exploding upward. The system measuring it is dying.

This is the confusion at the center of every well-intentioned "how to invest in Bitcoin" LinkedIn post. The framing itself is broken. The framing assumes the dollar is the measurement, and Bitcoin is the thing being measured. But the truth is the other way around. Once you own Bitcoin and you begin to think in Bitcoin, the dollar becomes the thing being measured. Everything else in your life gets priced in sats. And every year, every fiat asset you used to hold looks smaller, because you are finally holding the ruler and watching the world through it.

---

### The Satoshi bridge

Satoshi Nakamoto knew the human mind could not make this leap unassisted.

Nothing in the Bitcoin white paper priced Bitcoin in dollars. The white paper described a peer-to-peer electronic cash system. It described how nodes could reach consensus without a trusted third party. It described a mining mechanism, a hashing algorithm, a difficulty adjustment. It did not describe an exchange rate.

But from the very first year, Bitcoin was priced in dollars. In pizzas denominated in dollars. In pennies denominated in dollars. In fractional cents denominated in dollars. That was the scaffolding, and it was necessary. Because the only reference frame the human brain of 2009 had for value was the reference frame of fiat currency, Satoshi's protocol was going to be forced to translate itself into that reference frame for as long as it took the reference frame to give way.

The pricing of Bitcoin in dollars is a temporary compromise with human psychology, not a description of what Bitcoin is.

Satoshi understood that. He priced it that way not because he had to, but because we needed him to. Two decades in, the scaffolding is still holding up the frame. But it is scaffolding. It comes down when the building can stand on its own.

---

### Beyond money, the protocol

There is one more distortion worth naming, because it comes up constantly in the Bitcoin conversation and it obscures the real value proposition of the technology.

Bitcoin is a monetary protocol. But it is also a technology. Its underlying architecture, proof-of-work, energy-backed security, immutable ledger, permissionless access, self-custody keys, enables an entire second and third order of capabilities that have nothing to do with pricing.

Bitcoin mining monetizes stranded and wasted energy that no traditional market could ever reach. Renewable installations that would otherwise be economically nonviable become viable when a Bitcoin miner is willing to buy the excess power. Grids get built. Communities in Malawi and rural Texas get infrastructure they could not have financed any other way.

Bitcoin's security model, the aggregate hash rate of the global mining network, is one of the most robust cybersecurity systems humanity has ever built. Every transaction validated by that network inherits its security. Every message signed with a Bitcoin private key inherits its cryptographic guarantee.

Bitcoin's self-custody model puts a citizen back in control of their own value. No bank. No intermediary. No permission slip. That is not a financial product. That is a civil-liberties advancement.

None of those things should be calculated as an investment. All of them should be calculated as what they are: a technological advancement of human society. Same protocol. Different framing.

---

### The gravity of the old rails

There is one more distortion worth naming, and it is happening in real time.

The same monetary architecture that trained us to think of everything as an investment is now doing its very best to pull Bitcoin back inside its own walls. Large financial institutions have begun packaging Bitcoin into the same instruments they use to control fiat: exchange-traded products, structured funds, custodial wrappers, and every other form of financialization that turns a monetary asset into a portfolio line item. On the surface, these products bring more capital into Bitcoin. Underneath, they train the next generation of Bitcoin holders to use the exact same fiat vocabulary the technology was designed to make obsolete.

If those instruments become the primary way most people ever encounter Bitcoin, the reframing this essay is arguing for will never happen. Bitcoin will remain an asset on a screen, not a ruler in a hand. The financial system will have absorbed the protocol without absorbing the point.

That is the corrective this movement has to make. And someone specific has to make it.

---

### Why this matters for artists

Bitcoin For The Arts exists because the fiat monetary system punishes exactly the kind of artist we should be building the country around.

An artist who takes fifteen years to paint one masterpiece is punished by inflation. An artist who takes three years to shoot one film is punished by inflation. An artist who spends decades building a body of work is punished by inflation. In a fiat system, every artist is forced to become an investor of their own time, chasing gigs and grants and residencies at whatever the current velocity of the market demands. The ruler will not sit still, so the artist cannot either.

Sound money changes that. When the yardstick stops moving, the artist can slow down. When the yardstick stops moving, the artist can measure success by the depth of the work rather than the speed of the churn. When the yardstick stops moving, the artist can save Bitcoin for a decade and know that, at the end of that decade, the value of that Bitcoin will represent the same or greater share of the world's productive output as it did the day the saving began.

That is what Bitcoin For The Arts is asking artists to understand. Not "how to invest in Bitcoin." Not "what allocation of Bitcoin to hold in your portfolio." Not "when to buy the dip." Those are all questions inside the fiat frame, and inside the fiat frame there is no good answer for a working artist.

The right question is: how do I begin to think in Bitcoin, so that the way I make decisions about my time, my craft, my career, and my legacy stops being distorted by a ruler that will not sit still?

---

### Take the lens off

The word "investment" is a lens that fiat put on our eyes. When we look at Bitcoin through that lens, we see something distorted. Take the lens off, and Bitcoin becomes what it always was.

Money.

A unit of account. A medium of exchange. A store of value. A tool that measures, moves, and carries value across time.

Every well-meaning post that teaches Bitcoin as an investment is a symptom of a much larger education gap. Nothing wrong with the people writing them. They are working from the only vocabulary any of us were given. But the vocabulary has to change, and Bitcoin For The Arts is going to keep changing it, because until the artists we serve can think about Bitcoin as money, they cannot yet think about their own careers, their own communities, and their own creative futures on a Bitcoin standard.

The ruler stopped moving.

Look at the world through it.

Everything else follows.
