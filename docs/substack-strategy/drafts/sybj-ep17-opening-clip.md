## BODY

Rami Korhonen has sung in the Finnish punk band Fumble since the early nineties. One of their songs opens an episode of Law & Order: Special Victims Unit. His solo project is The Higher Low. He calls the music acoustic pleb rock. The songs are about what Bitcoin can do. They never use the word.

The clip is the first ninety seconds of the episode. He says three things, in this order.

A song, when it is one of the good ones, arrives whole. He will go three weeks without touching a guitar. He is traveling. Then he picks it up in the right mood, and the song comes through. Chords, melody, lyrics. Three minutes.

Then the room. The power goes out. They keep playing.

"Even if there's two people and a dog at a coffee shop, we still play like it's a thousand people at a rock festival."

Then the book. He had been around Bitcoin since 2017, and he still calls the years through about 2021 a golden era of the culture. The morning it became the rest of his life came inside those years. Early in 2020 he read The Bitcoin Standard.

"Okay. That's it. Bitcoin is the thing."

He lost interest in everything else. He moved to Bitcoin and never looked back.

The episode after this clip is the rest of the road. The Ramones in Helsinki at sixteen. Twelve songs recorded in one four-hour sitting. The album Node To Freedom. One guitar, opening rooms that are already talking. Knut Svanholm jumping on the drums at Baltic Honeybadger in Riga and slowing Freedom Engine down for good. That song, written in El Zonte. The sats from the music, still unspent, waiting until they can pay for the next record.
