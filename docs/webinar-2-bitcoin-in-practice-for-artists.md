# Part 1: Your First Bitcoin Wallet

## What Is a Bitcoin Wallet?

A wallet is software (or hardware) that stores your private keys — the secret codes that prove you own your Bitcoin. Think of it like the key to a studio lockbox: whoever holds the key controls the funds.

**Key concept:** You are not storing Bitcoin "in" the wallet. Bitcoin lives on the blockchain. Your wallet holds the keys to access it.

## Types of Wallets

| Type | Best For | Examples |
|------|----------|---------|
| Mobile wallet (non-custodial) | Daily use, small amounts | BlueWallet, Phoenix, Muun |
| Desktop wallet | Mid-range amounts, privacy | Sparrow Wallet, Electrum |
| Hardware wallet | Long-term savings, large amounts | Coldcard, Trezor, Blockstream Jade, Bitkey, Foundation Passport |
| Lightning wallet | Instant, small transactions | Wallet of Satoshi, Zeus, Phoenix |

## Recommended Starter Setup

**Step 1:** Download a mobile wallet
- iPhone or Android: **BlueWallet** (free, open-source)
- Supports both on-chain Bitcoin and Lightning

**Step 2:** Write down your seed phrase
- 12 or 24 words that recover your wallet if your phone is lost
- Write it on paper or stamp it in metal
- NEVER store it digitally (no screenshots, no cloud, no email)
- NEVER share it with anyone

**Step 3:** Generate a receiving address
- Tap "Receive" in your wallet
- You'll see a QR code and a string starting with `bc1q...`
- This is your public address — safe to share

## Hands-On: Let's Set Up Together

1. Download BlueWallet from your app store
2. Tap "Add Wallet" → Name it "My Art Wallet" → Select Bitcoin → Create
3. Carefully write down the 12-word seed phrase on paper
4. Verify you wrote it correctly
5. Tap "Receive" — this is your Bitcoin address
6. Congratulations! You're ready to receive Bitcoin.

# Part 2: Self-Custody — Why It Matters for Artists

## What Is Self-Custody?

Self-custody means **you hold your own keys**. No bank, no exchange, no platform can freeze, seize, or block your funds.

**"Not your keys, not your coins."**

## Why Artists Should Care

| Traditional System | Self-Custody with Bitcoin |
|---|---|
| Platform can freeze your account | Only you control your funds |
| PayPal/Venmo can hold payments for 21 days | Settlement in minutes (on-chain) or seconds (Lightning) |
| Banks can close accounts without warning | No account to close — you ARE the bank |
| International transfers take days + high fees | Send/receive globally, 24/7, with minimal fees |
| Inflation erodes your savings | 21 million cap — no one can print more |

## Real-World Artist Scenarios

**Scenario 1: The Censored Photographer**
A photographer's provocative political work leads a payment processor to freeze their account. With Bitcoin self-custody, no third party can stop payments from reaching them.

**Scenario 2: The International Muralist**
An artist in Colombia is commissioned by a collector in Berlin. Traditional wire transfer: 5 days, $45 fee, exchange rate loss. Bitcoin: settled in 10 minutes, fraction of the fee.

**Scenario 3: The Patient Sculptor**
A sculptor receives a $2,000 grant in Bitcoin in 2025. They hold it in self-custody while working on a 3-year project. By the time the piece is complete, sound money principles may have preserved or grown that value — unlike fiat sitting in a savings account losing purchasing power to inflation.

# Part 3: Accepting Bitcoin for Your Art

## Three Ways to Get Paid in Bitcoin

### 1. Direct Address Sharing
- Share your Bitcoin address (QR code) on your website, at shows, or on social media
- Great for: tips, one-time payments, patron support
- Free — no middleman

### 2. Lightning Invoices
- Generate a Lightning invoice for a specific amount
- Instant settlement (seconds, not minutes)
- Tiny fees (fractions of a penny)
- Great for: merch sales, event tickets, small commissions
- Use: Zeus, Phoenix, or Wallet of Satoshi

### 3. BTCPay Server (For Serious Sellers)
- Self-hosted payment processor — the "Shopify of Bitcoin"
- Accepts on-chain + Lightning
- No third-party fees. Ever.
- Generate invoices, track payments, issue receipts
- Great for: online stores, galleries, recurring patrons

## Practical Setup: Accept Bitcoin at Your Next Show

1. **Print a QR code** of your Bitcoin address (use BlueWallet → Receive → screenshot → print)
2. **Frame it** next to your art with a small sign: "Tips in Bitcoin accepted"
3. **Add a Lightning QR** for instant small payments
4. **Include your address on your business card**
5. **Add it to your website** — a simple "Support my work" section with a QR code

## Pricing Art in Bitcoin

- Price in USD (or your local currency), accept in BTC at the current rate
- Use sites like mempool.space or your wallet's built-in converter
- Consider offering a small discount for Bitcoin payments (saves you credit card fees)
- For commissions: agree on a USD price, convert at time of payment

# Part 4: The Lightning Network — Instant Payments for Artists

## What Is the Lightning Network?

A "second layer" built on top of Bitcoin that enables:
- **Instant** transactions (seconds, not minutes)
- **Near-zero fees** (fractions of a cent)
- **Micro-payments** (even 1 sat = ~$0.001)

Think of it like this: Bitcoin is the vault. Lightning is the cash register.

## Why Lightning Matters for Artists

- Accept tips during livestreams in real-time
- Sell prints for $5 without losing $0.30 + 2.9% to Stripe
- Receive micro-patronage from fans worldwide
- No minimums — someone can send you 100 sats ($0.10) as appreciation

## Lightning in Action: The "Zap" Economy

On platforms like Nostr (decentralized social media), supporters can "zap" creators with sats instantly. Imagine:

- You post a new painting → followers zap you 1,000 sats each
- You livestream your studio → viewers tip in real-time
- You publish a poem → readers send sats as appreciation

No platform takes a cut. No algorithm decides who sees your work. You own the relationship with your audience.

## Getting Started with Lightning

1. Download **Phoenix Wallet** (auto-manages Lightning channels)
2. Or use **Wallet of Satoshi** (simplest, custodial)
3. Generate a Lightning invoice → share it or post the QR
4. Receive instant payments

# Part 5: Building a Bitcoin Patron Community

## The New Patronage Model

Traditional patronage died with the Medici. Platforms like Patreon take 5-12% + payment processing fees. Bitcoin enables a return to direct patronage — but global and permissionless.

## How to Build Your Patron Base

### Start Small: The "100 True Fans" Approach
- You don't need millions of followers
- 100 supporters sending 10,000 sats/month (~$10) = meaningful ongoing support
- Offer exclusives: studio updates, early access, behind-the-scenes

### Tools for Bitcoin Patronage
| Tool | What It Does |
|------|-------------|
| BTCPay Server | Accept recurring or one-time payments on your website |
| Nostr + Zaps | Decentralized social media with built-in Lightning tipping |
| Geyser Fund | Bitcoin-native crowdfunding platform |
| Lightning Address | Easy-to-remember payment handle (you@yourdomain.com format) |
| Podcasting 2.0 | If you have a podcast, listeners stream sats per minute |

### Content Ideas That Build Community
- Monthly studio tour (Lightning-gated or free with a tip jar)
- "Work in progress" photo series with your Bitcoin address
- Auction a small piece with Bitcoin-only bidding
- Offer commissioned naming rights for your next series
- "Stack sats with me" — document your Bitcoin journey alongside your art journey

# Part 6: Tax Basics for Artists Receiving Bitcoin

## Important Disclaimer

This is general education, not tax advice. Consult a qualified tax professional for your specific situation.

## Key Concepts

### Bitcoin Is Property (in the U.S.)
- The IRS treats Bitcoin as property, not currency
- Receiving Bitcoin as payment = income at fair market value on the date received
- Selling or spending Bitcoin = potential capital gain or loss

### For Grant Recipients
- BFTA micro-grants: received as income at the BTC value on the date of receipt
- Keep a record of: date received, BTC amount, USD value at that time, transaction ID

### Record-Keeping Best Practices
| What to Record | Example |
|----------------|---------|
| Date received | 2026-03-15 |
| BTC amount | 0.025 BTC |
| USD value at receipt | $2,500 |
| Transaction ID | abc123... (from blockchain) |
| Source | BFTA Micro-Grant |
| Purpose | Art materials + studio time |

### Tools for Tracking
- **Spreadsheet** — simple and effective for small volumes
- **Koinly** or **CoinTracker** — automated tax reporting software
- **Your wallet's transaction history** — most wallets log dates and amounts

# Part 7: How to Apply for a BFTA Micro-Grant

## Grant Overview

| Detail | Info |
|--------|------|
| Amount | $500 – $2,000 (USD equivalent in BTC) |
| Disciplines | Visual arts, theater, dance, music, writing, storytelling, film |
| Eligibility | Independent artists and small collectives |
| Review cycle | Quarterly |
| Payment | Directly to your Bitcoin wallet |
| Reporting | Not required for micro-grants |

## What We Fund

- Creation costs (materials, studio time, rehearsal space)
- Production costs (recording, staging, print, fabrication)
- Travel tied to a specific project or performance
- Artist-led community programming

## What to Include in Your Application

1. **Your name + links** (website, portfolio, socials)
2. **Project description** (what, why, and timeline)
3. **Budget + requested amount** (simple breakdown)
4. **A Bitcoin address** to receive funds (set up your wallet first!)

## Application Walkthrough

1. Visit **bitcoinforthearts.org/grants**
2. Click "Open the application form"
3. Fill in your details — keep it concise and honest
4. Include your Bitcoin wallet address (from the wallet you set up today)
5. Submit — applications are reviewed quarterly
6. If selected, BTC is sent directly to your wallet

## Tips for a Strong Application

- **Be specific:** "I need $1,500 for 40 hours of ceramic kiln time and clay materials" beats "I need money for art"
- **Show your work:** Link to a portfolio, Instagram, or even a Google Drive folder
- **Explain impact:** How will this project serve your community or advance your craft?
- **Be realistic:** Match your ask to your project scope
- **Have your wallet ready:** We pay in Bitcoin — be prepared to receive it

# Part 8: Security Best Practices

## Protecting Your Bitcoin

### The Golden Rules

1. **Never share your seed phrase** — not with BFTA, not with "support," not with anyone
2. **Write it on paper or metal** — never digitally
3. **Use a strong PIN** on your wallet app
4. **Enable 2FA** on any exchange accounts (use an authenticator app, not SMS)
5. **Verify addresses** before sending — Bitcoin transactions are irreversible

### Common Scams to Watch For

| Scam | How to Spot It |
|------|---------------|
| "Send BTC to receive more back" | No one doubles your Bitcoin. Ever. |
| Fake BFTA emails asking for your seed phrase | We will NEVER ask for your seed phrase |
| Phishing wallet apps | Only download from official sources (app store links on bitcoin.org) |
| "Investment advisors" promising guaranteed returns | Bitcoin has no guaranteed returns |

### For Larger Amounts: Hardware Wallets

Once you're holding more than you'd be comfortable losing:
- Move funds to a **hardware wallet** (Coldcard, Trezor, Jade, Bitkey)
- Store the device safely (fireproof safe, separate from seed phrase)
- Consider a multi-signature setup for very large amounts
