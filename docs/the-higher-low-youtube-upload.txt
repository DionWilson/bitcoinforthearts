=== DESCRIPTION ===

In this episode, Bitcoin For The Arts founder Dion Wilson sits down with The Higher Low, the Finnish Bitcoin musician also known as Rami Korhonen, the singer of the melodic punk rock band Fumble since the nineties, and the one-person do-it-yourself project he calls Acoustic Pleb Rock.

Rami noticed Bitcoin in 2017 and fell all the way in a few years later. On the weekends of long business trips in 2021 he started writing short, punchy songs about what Bitcoin can do in the world, with one rule: the songs never use the word Bitcoin. He recorded the first batch over Christmas 2021, released LFG in January 2022, and released the debut album Node To Freedom in February 2023.

Since then he has taken one voice and one electro-acoustic guitar onto Bitcoin stages from Amsterdam to Hodlween in Charlotte to Satoshi Rockamoto in Riga to a Thailand afterparty, finished an eleven-country solo tour in 2023 to 2024, and released the full-band second album Freedom In the Digital Age in August 2025 with a launch show at BTChel Helsinki. Freedom Engine, one of the songs, was written in El Zonte, El Salvador. He crowdfunded the vinyl in bitcoin on Geyser.

This is the full story: Fumble and what punk taught him, why the songs refuse the word, what the road taught him about plebs and campfires, why Bitcoin is punk rock, and what changed when he handed the new record to Mikko Salo, Henkka Huhtanen, and Jesperi Mommo. His stated aim is simple: a campfire fan should like the song before they know what a sat is, and a pleb should smile.

Rami Korhonen, recording as The Higher Low. Finnish singer of melodic punk rock band Fumble since the nineties. Solo project started 2021. Debut album Node To Freedom (Feb 2023). Second album Freedom In the Digital Age (Aug 2025, Walkalone Records), 10 tracks, full-band sound, produced / mixed / mastered by Jesperi Mommo, drums Mikko Salo, bass Henkka Huhtanen. Freedom Engine written in El Zonte, El Salvador. Vinyl crowdfunded on Geyser. Author of the Higher Low Substack on Bitcoin, macroeconomics, and DIY punk rock.
