# Bitcoin Arts Park Booth Schedule

**Event:** Midwest Bitcoin Summit
**Location:** Greater Columbus Convention Center, Columbus, Ohio

**Booth Footprint:** 30-foot activation in the expo hall
**Prepared for:** Julie Costello, programming lead
**Prepared by:** Dion Wilson

---

## Design principle

A working expo booth is not a theater. Attendees are moving, drifting, grazing. They stop for 3 to 10 minutes. Very few will sit through a 90-minute film. The booth is designed for two audiences at once:

1. **Drifters.** People walking past who need something visually interesting every 30 seconds to pull them in. This is what the mining printer, Shipwreck Shawn's live painting, and rotating film teasers on the monitor are for. These run continuously.
2. **Anchors.** People who deliberately come at a specific time for a specific piece of programming. This is what the 30-minute film excerpts, IndeeHub demos, book reading, and artist story times are for. These are scheduled on a printable card so an attendee can plan around them.

Both audiences are served every hour of both days.

---

## Continuous booth activities (all day, both days)

These run from booth open at 9:00 AM to booth close at 5:30 PM without interruption. No scheduling required beyond turning them on in the morning and off at the end of the day.

- **Proof of Print mining printer.** Live mining feed on the primary monitor. Prints occasional receipts, poems, images, or ledger entries generated from mining data. Draws attention continuously and gives attendees something physical to walk away with. Assign one volunteer to keep the paper stocked and hand out prints.
- **Shipwreck Shawn live painting.** Shawn is at his easel in the corner of the booth for both days, working on his in-progress piece. Attendees can watch, ask questions, or buy prints. Provide Shawn with a chair for breaks. Suggested breaks: 11:00 to 11:15 AM, 1:00 to 1:30 PM (during stage time when booth is quieter), 3:30 to 3:45 PM.
- **Film teaser loops on secondary monitor.** Three-to-five-minute cut-downs of *Dirty Coin*, *Far From Home*, and *Have Bitcoin* (verify third title with Julie) play on rotation on a secondary monitor throughout the day. This is the "grazing" film content. The 30-minute scheduled excerpts happen at the primary viewing area on a separate monitor.
- **Silent auction and artist directory station.** A tablet or laminated sheet where attendees can browse the BFTA Artist Directory and place bids on the silent auction item. Manned by volunteer throughout the day.

## Rhythm summary

The booth pulses on a predictable hour-by-hour rhythm.

- **Top of every hour**: something scheduled starts.
- **Bottom of every hour**: booth reverts to continuous activity.
- **Lunch hour (12-1 PM)**: stage takes center attention, booth goes quiet.
- **Post-stage hour**: highest booth traffic. Best content lands here.
- **Last hour of day**: wind-down, silent auction close, sponsor thanks.

An attendee walking past the booth at any point in either day encounters either scheduled programming or continuous visual activity. There is no dead hour. There is no wasted footprint.

The 30-minute film excerpts specifically solve the problem Dion named. Nobody sits through a 90-minute documentary in an expo hall. Everybody will stop for a 30-minute anchor moment during their scheduled break, or for a 3-to-5-minute teaser loop while walking past. Both audiences get served, and either can be entry points into full-length views of the films after the event via Kinema, IndeeHub, or Geyser.
