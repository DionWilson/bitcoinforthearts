Share Your Bitcoin Journey: James Kintore — Interview Questions


Format note (Dion only): Two beats per question. One short premise + one short
question. ~30–40 words total. Anything that doesn't fit the premise becomes a
follow-up after he answers. Same intellectual depth as the Benjamin Ella set;
just tighter delivery so the punch lands and James doesn't lose the thought.


HOST INTRO (spoken on camera before Q1)

Same structure as the Ethan Turer and Joe Martin episodes — constant opener,
then guest framing, then a one-line "in this conversation" tease, then the
handoff.

  Welcome to another episode of Share Your Bitcoin Journey presented by
  Bitcoin for the Arts. I am Dion Wilson, the founder and executive director
  for Bitcoin for the Arts, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit empowering artists with
  Bitcoin grants, education, and sovereign funding tools.

  Today, we have the pleasure of speaking with an Australian interior
  designer, the founder of Kintore Design, and one of the most respected
  voices in high-end residential design on the New South Wales coast. Known
  for his uncompromising materiality, his small-batch furniture work through
  his brand Mat and Kin, and his clear-eyed advocacy for sound money inside
  the design world.

  His Woollahra Residence project was published in The Local Project and
  Artedomus for the level of craft and intention in the work. He runs Mat
  and Kin out of walnut and cast-bronze pieces — small batch, made to outlast
  the buyer. He has spoken publicly about Bitcoin as a store of value
  alongside real estate, and brings the framework of "going where you're
  treated best" to how he thinks about life and work.

  In this conversation, he shares his personal journey from designer to
  Bitcoiner, how the materials he chooses for clients and the conviction he
  holds for Bitcoin point in the same direction — toward things made to last.
  We talk about what his high-net-worth design clients are actually saying
  about the Australian dollar in 2026, what a Bitcoin-native client of the
  future looks like, and what a design career on a Bitcoin standard could be.
  His story is grounded, articulate, and full of practical wisdom for any
  maker or craftsperson who has ever felt the pressure of fiat erosion on the
  work they were trying to make.

  Without further ado, welcome to the show, James Kintore.

BACKGROUND & JOURNEY

1. James, welcome to Share Your Bitcoin Journey. Before Kintore Design,
before Mat and Kin, before any of the work people know you for — there was
a kid who eventually became a designer. Where does that story actually
start for you?

2. Most designers can point to a single thing that locked it in early — a
house they grew up in, a building they couldn't stop looking at, a person
who showed them what space could actually do. What was that for you?

3. Walk us through how you actually became a designer — formal training,
who you worked under early on, what you learned about how the work really
gets made before you ever started Kintore.

4. Most working designers can name the project where they first felt like
"this is what I'm doing for the rest of my life." What was that one for
you?

5. Out of all that came Kintore Design, and then Mat and Kin. For listeners
hearing your name for the first time — what's the work today, and what's
the standard you hold yourself to?


THE BITCOIN TURN

6. How did you first find Bitcoin — was there a specific moment, a specific
person, or a specific experience that planted the seed?

7. Every Bitcoiner has a "something snapped" moment — a lived experience as
a working professional that made Bitcoin go from interesting to obvious.
As a young designer running cash flow, dealing with banks, watching the
Australian dollar — what was yours?

8. You've gone public with your Bitcoin conviction — the Jake Woodhouse
Podcast, talking real-estate-vs-Bitcoin in front of the same world your
clients move in. What did that cost you, and what did it open up?


MATERIALS, CRAFTSMANSHIP & SOUND MONEY

9. Interior design is built on long time horizons — European oak, walnut,
cast bronze, materials that gain patina and outlast the client. When did
Bitcoin start to feel like the same logic at a different layer?

10. Sound money and good design share a principle — scarcity by intention,
not by accident. As someone who builds rooms and objects meant to last
decades, does that parallel land for you?

11. Your furniture brand Mat and Kin makes small-batch pieces in walnut and
cast bronze — made to outlast the buyer. Bitcoin's 21 million cap is the
same move at a different scale. Same conviction running both?

12. Interior design rewards patience — projects take a year, materials are
meant to read in decade three. Bitcoiners call that low time preference.
Does the way Bitcoin asks you to think about time feel native to what you
already do?

13. You closed the company entity at the start of 2026 to restructure.
Without getting into anything you don't want public, what does the next
chapter of Kintore look like?


AUSTRALIA: REAL ESTATE, FIAT & THE PATRON CLASS

14. You build for high-net-worth clients in Sydney's most expensive
postcodes. What are those clients actually saying — privately — about the
Australian dollar, real estate, and Bitcoin in 2026?

15. Australian residential design lives on the back of leveraged real
estate — clients who borrowed to buy and renovate. If Bitcoin compresses
the real estate premium over the next decade, what changes for designers
like you?

16. Australia has a complicated relationship with money — banks closing
accounts, AUSTRAC scrutiny, restrictive crypto banking. What's it like
running a design business and holding Bitcoin in that environment?


THE WORLDVIEW

17. We're both creatives whose work is ultimately paid for by wealthy
patrons — your design clients, my dance audiences. Most Bitcoiners come
from tech or finance. What does our seat in that room let us see that
they miss?

18. You've spoken publicly about "going where you're treated best." That's
a serious framework, especially for an Australian. What does living that
out actually look like for you and the studio?

19. What would you say to a young Australian designer or maker today who
thinks Bitcoin is "a tech thing" that has nothing to do with their craft?


LOOKING FORWARD

20. Five years from now — Kintore Design, Mat and Kin, James personally —
where do you want to be, and how does Bitcoin sit inside that picture?


THE CLOSING QUESTION

21. Last question — we ask every guest this on Share Your Bitcoin Journey.
If a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from Bitcoin for the Arts landed in
your wallet tomorrow, what would it unlock for you as a creator?
