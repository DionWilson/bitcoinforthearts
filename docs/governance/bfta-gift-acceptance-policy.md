## Section 1. Purpose and Scope

**1.1 Purpose.** This Gift Acceptance Policy (the "Policy") sets forth the standards, procedures, and internal controls governing the acceptance, valuation, recording, and stewardship of gifts to Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. ("BFTA, Inc." or the "Corporation"). The Policy is intended to (i) protect BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt status and its ability to serve its mission; (ii) ensure that donor intent is honored consistently across every gift; (iii) safeguard BFTA, Inc. from gifts that would create legal, financial, or reputational harm; and (iv) support accurate financial reporting and Form 990 disclosure.

**1.2 Scope.** This Policy applies to BFTA, Inc. and to every gift solicited, offered to, or accepted by BFTA, Inc., regardless of the form of the gift or the identity of the donor.

**1.3 Administration.** This Policy is administered by the Executive Director of BFTA, Inc. or their designee, with review by the Gift Acceptance Committee established under Section 4 for matters within its scope.

**1.4 Compliance with Form 990.** BFTA, Inc. shall report the existence of this Policy on its annual IRS Form 990 (Part VI, Section B, Line 12a) and shall answer that Line "yes."

---

## Section 2. Governing Authority

**2.1 Adoption.** This Policy is adopted by resolution of the BFTA, Inc. board of directors.

**2.2 Amendment.** This Policy may be amended by majority resolution of the BFTA, Inc. board of directors.

**2.3 Effective Date.** This Policy takes effect on the date of adoption and remains in effect until amended or repealed.

**2.4 Relationship to Other BFTA, Inc. Policies.** This Policy operates alongside BFTA, Inc.'s other governance documents, including its Bylaws, Conflict of Interest Policy, Endowment Management Policy, and Grant Terms and Guidelines. Where any provision of this Policy conflicts with BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws, the Bylaws prevail.

## Section 3. Definitions

For purposes of this Policy:

**"Anonymous Gift"** means a gift for which the donor has requested that their identity not be publicly disclosed.

**"Bitcoin"** means BTC held on the Bitcoin base chain or the Lightning Network. Bitcoin does not include any other digital asset.

**"Board"** means the board of directors of BFTA, Inc.

**"Committee"** or **"Gift Acceptance Committee"** means the standing committee of the Board established under Section 4.

**"Executive Director"** means the Executive Director of BFTA, Inc. or, in the absence of that officer, the officer designated by the Board to perform equivalent functions.

**"Fair Market Value"** means the price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, both having reasonable knowledge of the relevant facts, as determined in accordance with applicable IRS guidance.

**"Gift"** means a voluntary transfer of money, property, or in-kind goods or services to BFTA, Inc., for which BFTA, Inc. gives no meaningful consideration in return other than donor recognition.

**"HODL Vault"** means the long-term Bitcoin reserve of BFTA, Inc., held in self-custody with multi-signature cold-storage protections, governed by BFTA, Inc.'s Endowment Management Policy.

**"In-Kind Gift"** means a gift of goods or services other than cash, cash equivalents, digital assets, real property, art, or publicly-traded securities.

**"Independent Director"** means a member of the Board who (i) is not an employee, contractor, or compensated service provider of BFTA, Inc.; (ii) is not an immediate family member of any such employee, contractor, or compensated service provider; and (iii) has no material financial interest in BFTA, Inc. beyond the reimbursement of reasonable expenses.

**"Non-Bitcoin Digital Asset"** means any digital asset other than Bitcoin, including but not limited to altcoins, stablecoins, tokenized securities, non-fungible tokens ("NFTs"), and other cryptocurrency assets.

**"Non-Cash Property"** means any gift other than cash, cash equivalents, or digital assets, including art, real property, publicly-traded securities, privately-held securities, and other tangible or intangible property.

**"Pledge"** means a written commitment by a donor to make one or more future gifts to BFTA, Inc.

**"Policy"** means this Gift Acceptance Policy.

**"Restricted Gift"** means a gift subject to a purpose restriction, a time restriction, or a designation to a specific program, project, beneficiary class, or named initiative.

**"Start-Up Phase"** means the period during which BFTA, Inc. employs fewer than ten (10) paid full-time staff and maintains an annual operating budget of less than five hundred thousand United States dollars ($500,000), measured at the close of the most recently completed fiscal year.

**"Unrestricted Gift"** means a gift not subject to any donor-imposed restriction beyond the general mission of BFTA, Inc.

## Section 4. Gift Acceptance Committee

**4.1 Establishment.** BFTA, Inc. shall maintain a standing Gift Acceptance Committee (the "Committee") to review gifts that fall within the scope of Section 4.3 below. The Committee is a standing subcommittee of the Board.

**4.2 Composition.** The Committee shall be composed of at least three (3) members, including:

(a) The Treasurer of BFTA, Inc., or an officer holding an equivalent financial oversight role;
(b) At least one Independent Director as defined in Section 3; and
(c) During the Start-Up Phase, the Executive Director of BFTA, Inc. may serve as an additional member of the Committee. Once BFTA, Inc. exits the Start-Up Phase, the Executive Director shall serve as a non-voting participant only.

The Board may appoint additional Committee members as it determines appropriate. Committee members serve at the pleasure of the Board.

**4.3 Matters Requiring Committee Review.** The following gifts and gift-related matters require Committee review prior to acceptance or, where indicated, prior to material action:

(a) Any non-cash property gift, including publicly-traded securities, privately-held securities, real property, and art;
(b) Any in-kind gift of goods or services with a fair market value at or above the threshold set forth in Section 5.7;
(c) Any restricted gift of $10,000 or greater (or USD-equivalent) that requires a Gift Agreement under Section 6;
(d) Any gift from a donor who has a material conflict of interest with BFTA, Inc.;
(e) Any gift that requires legal counsel review under Section 12;
(f) Any gift flagged for review under the anti-money-laundering and donor due-diligence procedures in Section 9;
(g) Any anonymous gift of $25,000 or greater (or USD-equivalent); and
(h) Any matter referred to the Committee by the Executive Director or by any Committee member.

**4.4 Meetings and Decisions.** The Committee meets as needed to review matters within its scope. Meetings may be held in person or by remote conference. The Committee may act by written consent when the matter permits, including via email.

A quorum for Committee action is a majority of members then serving. Committee decisions require a majority vote of members present. Decisions of the Committee are recorded in Committee minutes maintained by the Corporate Secretary or designee.

Committee decisions to accept or refuse gifts are final for gifts below thresholds requiring full Board approval under BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws. Any Committee decision may be appealed to the full Board by the donor, the Executive Director, or any Committee member.

**4.5 Reporting.** The Committee reports its activity to the full Board at each regular Board meeting, including a summary of gifts reviewed, decisions made, and any matters escalated for full Board consideration. Aggregate Committee activity is disclosed in BFTA, Inc.'s annual transparency report.

**4.6 Related-Party Board Membership.** Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. was founded and continues to be led by its founder, whose ongoing service to the organization is central to its mission and identity. In recognition of this founder relationship, and consistent with common practice for founder-led nonprofits, the Board of BFTA, Inc. may include a family member of a compensated officer, including a spouse of the Executive Director. A family member so serving is a full voting member of the Board and, where consistent with the Bylaws, may serve on standing committees.

Where a family member of a compensated officer serves on the Board or the Committee:

(a) The family relationship is disclosed in BFTA, Inc.'s annual conflicts-of-interest disclosures, in the annual transparency report, and in IRS Form 990 Schedule L (Transactions with Interested Persons) where applicable;
(b) The family-member Director shall recuse from any Board or Committee decision that (i) determines the compensation, employment terms, or performance evaluation of the related compensated officer; (ii) approves a transaction between BFTA, Inc. and the family-member Director or the related compensated officer, other than in the ordinary course of employment; or (iii) is otherwise identified by the Corporate Secretary as requiring recusal under BFTA, Inc.'s Conflict of Interest Policy;
(c) The family-member Director is not an Independent Director as defined in Section 3, and the presence of a family-member Director does not satisfy the "at least one Independent Director" requirement in Section 4.2(b); and
(d) The full Board retains authority to make employment and compensation decisions regarding the related compensated officer, with the family-member Director recused as provided in Section 4.6(b) above.

## Section 5. Types of Gifts and Handling

### 5.1 Bitcoin Gifts

Bitcoin gifts are the preferred form of gift to BFTA, Inc. Bitcoin gifts are received on-chain and over the Lightning Network in accordance with BFTA, Inc.'s Bitcoin-only mission.

(a) Acceptance Channels. BFTA, Inc. accepts Bitcoin gifts on-chain and over the Lightning Network. The current published Bitcoin donation addresses and Lightning invoice channels are maintained by the Executive Director or designee.
(b) Valuation. Each Bitcoin gift is valued at fair market value in U.S. dollars at the time of on-chain confirmation or at the time of Lightning invoice settlement. The valuation source is the public spot rate at a Bitcoin-only reference venue.
(c) On-Chain Receipt. BFTA, Inc. issues an on-chain receipt evidencing each Bitcoin gift.
(d) HODL Vault Contribution. Five percent (5%) of each Bitcoin gift is contributed to the HODL Vault as part of BFTA, Inc.'s long-term reserve discipline.
(e) Allocation. Consistent with BFTA, Inc.'s 55/30/10/5 allocation of unrestricted contributions, unrestricted Bitcoin gifts are allocated as follows: fifty-five percent (55%) to direct artist grants, thirty percent (30%) to programs, ten percent (10%) to operations, and five percent (5%) to the HODL Vault. Restricted Bitcoin gifts are honored one hundred percent (100%) to the donor's stated purpose in accordance with Section 6.
(f) Volatility Disclosure. Prior to accepting a pledged Bitcoin gift, BFTA, Inc. provides the donor with a written volatility disclosure explaining that the value of a Bitcoin gift may fluctuate materially between the date of pledge and the date of receipt. Neither the donor nor BFTA, Inc. is required to time the receipt of a Bitcoin gift to specific market conditions.

### 5.2 Fiat Currency Gifts

BFTA, Inc. accepts gifts in fiat currency by check, wire transfer, ACH, credit card, and comparable payment methods.

(a) Receiving Account. Fiat gifts are received into BFTA, Inc.'s fiat operating account at a Bitcoin-friendly banking institution.
(b) Bitcoin Conversion. BFTA, Inc. operates as a Bitcoin-native organization. Fiat gifts are converted to Bitcoin operationally, with timing determined to balance conversion costs, market conditions, and near-term fiat operating needs. The 5% HODL Vault contribution and 55/30/10/5 allocation are applied against the gross fiat amount received, prior to any conversion costs.
(c) Foreign Currency. Gifts received in currencies other than United States dollars are converted to USD upon receipt, at the spot rate at the receiving institution on the date of settlement. The resulting USD amount is treated as a fiat gift under this Section 5.2.
(d) Credit Card Fees. Credit card processing fees are treated as an operating expense under the 10% operations allocation and are not deducted from the donor's gross gift for purposes of the 55/30/10/5 allocation.
(e) Receipt. BFTA, Inc. issues a standard tax-deductible acknowledgment letter to the donor within thirty (30) days of receipt, in a form compliant with Internal Revenue Service ("IRS") requirements.

### 5.3 Non-Bitcoin Digital Asset Gifts

Gifts of non-Bitcoin digital assets, including altcoins, stablecoins, tokenized securities, non-fungible tokens ("NFTs"), and other cryptocurrency assets, are accepted at the discretion of BFTA, Inc. and are governed by the following:

(a) Immediate Conversion. Any accepted non-Bitcoin digital asset is converted to Bitcoin immediately upon recognition of receipt. BFTA, Inc. does not hold any non-Bitcoin digital asset for any period longer than is operationally necessary to complete the conversion.
(b) Approved Conversion Venues. Conversions are executed at Bitcoin-only conversion venues to the extent operationally available. Non-Bitcoin-only exchanges may be used only where no Bitcoin-only alternative is operationally available and only for the minimum period necessary to complete the conversion.
(c) NFTs and Illiquid Digital Assets. NFT gifts and gifts of illiquid or thinly-traded digital assets are subject to Committee review under Section 4.3(a) prior to acceptance. Committee review considers marketability, valuation, mission alignment, and any donor restrictions. Committee refusal is a valid and expected outcome for many such gifts.
(d) Post-Conversion Treatment. Once converted to Bitcoin, the resulting Bitcoin is treated as a Bitcoin gift under Section 5.1 for all subsequent purposes, including HODL Vault contribution, allocation, and reporting.
(e) No Long-Term Holding. Non-Bitcoin digital assets are never held in BFTA, Inc.'s HODL Vault, operational Bitcoin treasury, fiat operating account, or any other treasury layer beyond the minimum time required to complete conversion.

### 5.4 Publicly-Traded Securities

BFTA, Inc. accepts gifts of publicly-traded securities (stocks, bonds, exchange-traded funds, mutual fund shares) subject to Committee review under Section 4.3(a).

(a) Valuation. Publicly-traded securities are valued using the mean of the high and low quoted selling prices on the transfer date, consistent with IRS Publication 561 guidance. Valuation is determined at receipt of the securities into BFTA, Inc.'s brokerage account.
(b) Sale. Publicly-traded securities are sold within ten (10) business days of receipt, unless the Committee approves a longer holding period for a specific gift for good cause. BFTA, Inc. does not hold long positions in publicly-traded securities as a matter of investment strategy.
(c) Proceeds. Net sale proceeds, after brokerage costs, are converted to Bitcoin or applied to operations as determined by the Committee or the Board. The 5% HODL Vault contribution and 55/30/10/5 allocation apply against the gross valuation of the gift at receipt.
(d) IRS Form 8283. For securities gifts of $500 or more, BFTA, Inc. assists the donor with IRS Form 8283 acknowledgment as required by the IRS.
(e) Restricted Securities and Private Company Stock. Gifts of restricted securities (Rule 144 or Rule 145 stock), pre-IPO private company stock, and other illiquid securities are subject to Committee review, may require legal counsel review under Section 12, and may be refused where the securities cannot be liquidated within a reasonable period at a reasonable valuation.

### 5.5 Real Property

BFTA, Inc. accepts gifts of real property (land, buildings, mineral rights, and other real estate interests) subject to Committee review under Section 4.3(a) and legal counsel review under Section 12.

(a) Due Diligence. No real property gift is accepted without completion of the following due-diligence steps:

(i) Independent appraisal by a qualified appraiser meeting IRS Publication 561 standards, at the donor's expense unless the Committee approves otherwise;
(ii) Title review and confirmation of clean and marketable title;
(iii) Environmental review, including Phase I environmental site assessment where the property is or has been used for commercial or industrial purposes;
(iv) Confirmation that the property is free of unresolved liens, encumbrances, or judgments; and
(v) Assessment of holding costs, including property tax, insurance, and maintenance, during any expected holding period.

(b) Refusal Grounds. Real property gifts are refused where any due-diligence item cannot be resolved satisfactorily, where the property carries material environmental liability, or where holding costs would exceed reasonable expected net sale proceeds.
(c) Holding Period. Real property gifts are held for no longer than six (6) months, unless the Board approves a longer holding period by majority vote for good cause. Real property is not held as a programmatic asset absent a specific Board resolution making that determination.
(d) Sale. Sale proceeds, after transaction costs, are converted to Bitcoin or applied to operations as determined by the Committee or the Board. The 5% HODL Vault contribution and 55/30/10/5 allocation apply against the appraised value of the gift at acceptance.
(e) Gift Agreement. Real property gifts require a written Gift Agreement under Section 6, regardless of the property's appraised value.

### 5.6 Art and Non-Cash Collectibles

BFTA, Inc. accepts gifts of art and non-cash collectibles subject to Committee review under Section 4.3(a).

(a) Appraisal. Art and non-cash collectibles are valued by a qualified appraiser meeting IRS Publication 561 standards, at the donor's expense unless the Committee approves otherwise. Appraisals for gifts of $5,000 or more are documented on IRS Form 8283 as required.

(b) Two Treatment Paths. Upon acceptance, art and non-cash collectibles are treated in one of two ways, as determined by the Committee:

(i) Programmatic hold. The work is held as a programmatic asset if the Committee determines the work aligns with BFTA, Inc.'s mission and can be used for exhibition, artist support, or educational programming. Programmatic holds require Board approval by majority vote and are subject to insurance and safe storage arrangements at BFTA, Inc.'s expense.
(ii) Sale. The work is sold, with proceeds converted to Bitcoin or applied to operations. Sale is the default treatment absent a specific programmatic hold decision.

(c) IRS Related-Use Rule. For gifts of art valued at $5,000 or more where the donor claims a related-use deduction, BFTA, Inc. shall not sell the work within three (3) years of receipt absent a specific determination that continued programmatic use is impracticable. Compliance with IRS Section 170(e)(1)(B)(i) and related regulations is required.

(d) Fundraising Auction Use. Art donated specifically for use in a BFTA, Inc. fundraising auction is exempt from the programmatic-hold analysis in Section 5.6(b). Auction proceeds are treated as a Bitcoin or fiat gift under Sections 5.1 or 5.2 as applicable.

### 5.7 In-Kind Goods and Services

BFTA, Inc. accepts gifts of goods and services in kind.

(a) Fair Market Value. In-kind gifts are recorded at fair market value at the time of receipt. Fair market value is determined by the donor's own valuation for gifts below $5,000, and by independent appraisal for gifts of $5,000 or more.
(b) Committee Review Threshold. In-kind gifts with a fair market value at or above $10,000 are subject to Committee review under Section 4.3(b) prior to acceptance. Below that threshold, in-kind gifts may be accepted by the Executive Director or designee, subject to the refusal criteria in Section 10.
(c) Recording. In-kind gifts are recorded in BFTA, Inc.'s general ledger at fair market value and are disclosed in the annual transparency report, with the donor named unless the donor has elected anonymity under Section 7.
(d) No Effect on 55/30/10/5 Allocation. In-kind gifts are not subject to the 55/30/10/5 allocation rule. Unrestricted in-kind gifts do not affect BFTA, Inc.'s monetary treasury. Restricted in-kind gifts are recorded against the restricted purpose.
(e) Services from Related Parties. In-kind gifts of professional services from Board members, officers, or their firms are recorded at fair market value, are disclosed in the annual transparency report, and are treated as related-party transactions under BFTA, Inc.'s conflict-of-interest policy.

## Section 6. Gift Agreements

**6.1 Purpose of a Gift Agreement.** A Gift Agreement is a written instrument executed by BFTA, Inc. and the donor that memorializes the terms of a Gift, including the amount or nature of the Gift, any donor-imposed restrictions, timing, valuation methodology, donor recognition preferences, and any other material terms. A Gift Agreement is intended to protect donor intent, preserve BFTA, Inc.'s ability to steward the Gift consistently over time, and provide a clear record for financial reporting and Form 990 disclosure.

**6.2 When a Gift Agreement Is Required.** BFTA, Inc. shall execute a Gift Agreement for each of the following:

(a) Any Restricted Gift of ten thousand United States dollars ($10,000) or greater, or the Bitcoin-denominated equivalent measured at the time of pledge or receipt;
(b) Any gift of real property, regardless of appraised value, as provided in Section 5.5(e);
(c) Any Pledge, as defined in Section 3;
(d) Any deferred or planned Gift, including bequests, charitable remainder trusts, charitable lead trusts, life insurance designations, and beneficiary designations of retirement accounts;
(e) Any Gift that includes donor-imposed conditions on BFTA, Inc.'s use of the Gift, other than a designation to an existing named program or beneficiary class;
(f) Any Gift that contemplates any form of publicly-visible naming of a program, initiative, facility, event, or other BFTA, Inc. asset; and
(g) Any Gift referred by the Committee for a written Agreement.

**6.3 Form of Agreement.** The Executive Director maintains a standard Gift Agreement template approved by the Committee. Material deviations from the template require Committee review under Section 4.3. The Gift Agreement template is maintained separately from this Policy and updated by the Committee from time to time.

**6.4 Standard Terms.** Every Gift Agreement shall include, at minimum, the following:

(a) The identity of the donor and BFTA, Inc.;
(b) The amount or nature of the Gift and the anticipated form of delivery (Bitcoin, fiat, in-kind, real property, securities, or other);
(c) The valuation methodology to be used at the time of receipt, consistent with Section 5;
(d) Any donor-imposed restriction and the period of that restriction, if any;
(e) Any donor-imposed recognition preferences, including anonymity elections under Section 7;
(f) An impracticability release consistent with Section 8.4, permitting BFTA, Inc. to redirect the restricted purpose upon Board approval if the original purpose becomes illegal, impossible, or materially inconsistent with BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt purposes;
(g) A representation by the donor that the Gift is lawful, is not the proceeds of any unlawful activity, and does not violate any obligation the donor owes to a third party;
(h) A tax-consequence acknowledgment, stating that BFTA, Inc. is a 501(c)(3) organization but is not providing legal or tax advice to the donor;
(i) A no-quid-pro-quo provision, confirming that BFTA, Inc. has provided no goods or services in return for the Gift other than donor recognition and any de minimis benefits permitted under IRS regulations;
(j) A choice-of-law and dispute-resolution provision consistent with BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws; and
(k) The signatures of the donor and an authorized officer of BFTA, Inc.

**6.5 Publication of Sanitized Agreements.** BFTA, Inc.'s radical-transparency posture applies to Gift Agreements. Consistent with the donor's stated recognition and anonymity preferences under Section 7, BFTA, Inc. may publish sanitized summaries of executed Gift Agreements in its annual transparency report. Donor identifying information is omitted where the donor has elected anonymity under Section 7.3. Under no circumstances is a donor's personally identifiable information published against the donor's stated preferences.

**6.6 Modification of Gift Agreements.** Material modifications to an executed Gift Agreement require:

(a) Written consent of the donor (or, in the case of a deceased donor, the donor's estate or successor in interest); and
(b) Committee review under Section 4.3, or full Board approval where the modification exceeds Committee authority under BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws.

Immaterial administrative modifications (including corrections of typographical or arithmetic errors and updates to non-substantive contact information) may be made by the Executive Director and reported to the Committee at its next meeting.

**6.7 Retention.** BFTA, Inc. retains each executed Gift Agreement, and every amendment to it, for a period of not less than ten (10) years after the later of (i) full performance of the Gift by the donor and BFTA, Inc.; or (ii) expiration of any donor-imposed restriction. Gift Agreements associated with deferred or planned Gifts are retained for the life of the underlying instrument and thereafter for the retention period set forth in this Section 6.7.

## Section 7. Donor Recognition and Anonymity

**7.1 Radical Transparency as Default.** BFTA, Inc. operates under a public commitment to radical transparency. As a default, the identity of every donor to BFTA, Inc. is publicly disclosed in BFTA, Inc.'s annual transparency report, on its Sponsors page, and in relevant program materials. This default reflects BFTA, Inc.'s belief that public patronage is a public act, and that the working artists BFTA, Inc. serves are better supported when their patrons are visible.

**7.2 Forms of Recognition.** Subject to the donor's stated preferences under Section 7.3, forms of recognition offered by BFTA, Inc. include, without limitation:

(a) Listing on the BFTA, Inc. Sponsors page and in the annual transparency report;
(b) Named credit at BFTA, Inc. programs and events, including named-performance sponsorships, named-artist sponsorships, and named program tiers;
(c) Named credit in the "Pay the Artist" campaign, where a specific artist grant is publicly linked to a named underwriter;
(d) Recognition in the BFTA, Inc. newsletter, Substack, podcast credits, and social media channels;
(e) Physical acknowledgment at BFTA, Inc.-produced live cultural programming; and
(f) Naming of BFTA, Inc. programs, initiatives, facilities, or events, subject to the additional requirements in Section 7.7 (Naming Rights).

The particular form and duration of recognition applicable to a given Gift is set forth in the Gift Agreement or, for Gifts not subject to a Gift Agreement, in the acknowledgment materials issued to the donor under Section 13.

**7.3 Donor Election of Anonymity.** A donor may elect anonymity at any time, either at the time of Gift or thereafter. An anonymity election shall specify whether it applies to:

(a) All public recognition (an "Anonymous Gift" as defined in Section 3); or
(b) Public recognition of specific attributes only (for example, name may be published but Gift amount not, or Gift amount published without name).

An anonymity election is honored by BFTA, Inc. from the date of the election forward, subject to Section 7.4 below.

**7.4 Limits of Anonymity.** Notwithstanding any anonymity election, the identity of a donor may be required to be disclosed:

(a) On BFTA, Inc.'s IRS Form 990, Schedule B (Schedule of Contributors), which is filed with the IRS but not publicly disclosed as to specific donor identities for 501(c)(3) organizations;
(b) To BFTA, Inc.'s auditors and legal counsel in connection with BFTA, Inc.'s books and records;
(c) In response to a lawful subpoena, court order, or governmental request;
(d) Where required by BFTA, Inc.'s anti-money-laundering and donor due-diligence procedures under Section 9; and
(e) Where required by a change in applicable law.

BFTA, Inc. shall use commercially reasonable efforts to notify the donor before disclosing the donor's identity in response to any request under Section 7.4(c), except where notification is legally prohibited.

**7.5 Anonymous Gift Threshold Review.** Consistent with Section 4.3(g), any Anonymous Gift of twenty-five thousand United States dollars ($25,000) or greater, or the Bitcoin-denominated equivalent measured at the time of receipt, requires Committee review prior to acceptance. Committee review considers the source of funds, the donor's identity (which is disclosed to the Committee under the confidentiality obligations set forth in the Bylaws), and any indicia raised by BFTA, Inc.'s anti-money-laundering procedures under Section 9.

**7.6 Substantial Contributor Disclosure on Form 990.** BFTA, Inc. reports the names and amounts of substantial contributors on IRS Form 990, Schedule B, as required by IRS regulations. Anonymity elections do not exempt a donor from Schedule B disclosure to the IRS. The Schedule B is not publicly disclosed as to donor identity for 501(c)(3) public charities, but the requirement to prepare and file it is not waived by donor election.

**7.7 Naming Rights.** BFTA, Inc. may grant naming rights to programs, initiatives, facilities, events, and other BFTA, Inc. assets in recognition of Gifts. Naming rights shall be documented in a Gift Agreement under Section 6.

(a) Duration. Naming rights are granted for a defined period specified in the Gift Agreement, which may include the term of a specific event, a specified number of years, or the life of the named asset.
(b) Suspension or Revocation. BFTA, Inc. reserves the right to suspend or revoke a naming right where continued association with the named party would create material reputational harm to BFTA, Inc., would violate applicable law, or would materially impair BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt status. Suspension or revocation requires Board approval by majority vote and is subject to the terms of the underlying Gift Agreement.
(c) Retroactive Application. Naming rights are prospective from the date of Gift Agreement execution. Nothing in this Section 7.7 applies retroactively to naming rights granted prior to the effective date of this Policy.

**7.8 Recognition of Sovereign Circle and Small-Dollar Donors.** Recurring donors participating in BFTA, Inc.'s Sovereign Circle monthly giving program, and other small-dollar donors, are recognized in accordance with BFTA, Inc.'s Sovereign Circle Membership Policy. This Section 7 does not modify the recognition tiers set forth in that separate policy.

## Section 8. Restricted Gifts

**8.1 Acceptance of Restricted Gifts.** BFTA, Inc. accepts Restricted Gifts subject to the requirements of this Section 8. A donor-imposed restriction may take the form of:

(a) A purpose restriction (a specific programmatic use);
(b) A time restriction (a period during which the Gift must be applied);
(c) A geographic restriction (a specified region or community); or
(d) A designated-beneficiary restriction (a specific named artist, grantee class, or initiative).

**8.2 Documentation of Restrictions.** Every Restricted Gift is documented at the time of receipt, at minimum in BFTA, Inc.'s donor records, and additionally in a Gift Agreement where required under Section 6.2. Documentation identifies the specific restriction, the intended use of the Gift, and any time period during which the restriction applies.

**8.3 Effect on the 55/30/10/5 Allocation.** BFTA, Inc.'s standard 55/30/10/5 allocation of Unrestricted Gifts (fifty-five percent (55%) to direct artist grants, thirty percent (30%) to programs, ten percent (10%) to operations, and five percent (5%) to the HODL Vault) does not apply to Restricted Gifts. Restricted Gifts are honored one hundred percent (100%) to the donor's stated purpose, subject to the impracticability release in Section 8.4 and the reasonable operating-cost allocation permitted in Section 8.5.

**8.4 Impracticability Release.** If the specific purpose of a Restricted Gift becomes illegal, impossible, or materially inconsistent with BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt purposes, BFTA, Inc. may redirect the Gift to a purpose reasonably related to the donor's original intent, subject to:

(a) Written notice to the donor (or, in the case of a deceased donor, to the donor's estate or successor in interest) where reasonably practicable;
(b) Approval of the redirection by the Board by majority vote;
(c) Documentation of the redirection in the annual transparency report; and
(d) Compliance with applicable state charitable-trust or cy pres law where applicable.

The impracticability release is documented in every Gift Agreement executed under Section 6 to preserve BFTA, Inc.'s ability to serve donor intent over long time horizons without being trapped by circumstances the donor did not anticipate.

**8.5 Reasonable Operating-Cost Allocation.** BFTA, Inc. may allocate a reasonable share of overhead and administrative costs to the stewardship of a Restricted Gift, consistent with generally accepted accounting principles for nonprofit organizations. The reasonable operating-cost allocation shall not exceed fifteen percent (15%) of the Restricted Gift and shall be disclosed to the donor in the Gift Agreement executed under Section 6, if any, or in the acknowledgment materials issued under Section 13.

For Bitcoin Restricted Gifts specifically, the five percent (5%) HODL Vault contribution provided for in Section 5.1(d) is not applied to Restricted Gifts unless the donor's stated purpose is contribution to the HODL Vault.

**8.6 Reporting on Restricted Funds.** BFTA, Inc. maintains segregated tracking of Restricted Gifts in its general ledger. Restricted-fund balances, activity, and stewardship are disclosed in:

(a) BFTA, Inc.'s annual transparency report;
(b) BFTA, Inc.'s annual financial statements, subject to any independent audit process; and
(c) IRS Form 990, in the sections addressing restricted funds and program-related expenses.

Individual Restricted-Gift donors are provided with a stewardship report on the use of their Gift, at intervals specified in the Gift Agreement or, in the absence of a Gift Agreement, annually until the restriction is fully satisfied.

**8.7 Refusal of Restrictions.** BFTA, Inc. may refuse a Restricted Gift where the proposed restriction:

(a) Would violate applicable law, including the terms of BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws or Conflict of Interest Policy;
(b) Would require BFTA, Inc. to discriminate on the basis of any characteristic protected under applicable federal or state law;
(c) Would be materially inconsistent with BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt mission or would jeopardize BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt status;
(d) Would create a material administrative burden disproportionate to the size of the Gift;
(e) Would obligate BFTA, Inc. to expenditures that materially exceed the value of the Gift; or
(f) Cannot practically be honored due to the specificity or narrowness of the restriction.

Refusal of a Restricted Gift is a Committee decision under Section 4.3, and is documented consistent with Section 10 (Refusal Criteria).

**8.8 Naming and Restricted Gifts.** Where a Restricted Gift also carries naming rights, both this Section 8 and Section 7.7 (Naming Rights) apply. Nothing in this Section 8 modifies the naming-rights framework in Section 7.

## Section 9. Donor Due Diligence and Anti-Money-Laundering Procedures

**9.1 Purpose.** This Section 9 establishes BFTA, Inc.'s procedures for verifying donor identity, assessing the source of contributed funds, screening against applicable sanctions programs, and refusing Gifts that would create legal, regulatory, or reputational harm. The procedures reflect BFTA, Inc.'s commitment to lawful stewardship of donated resources and to its own long-term standing as a 501(c)(3) public charity. This Section 9 constitutes BFTA, Inc.'s complete anti-money-laundering framework and is not supplemented by a separate policy document.

**9.2 Governing Principle.** BFTA, Inc. accepts Gifts in good faith from donors who present themselves in the ordinary course. BFTA, Inc. does not treat every donor as a suspect and does not require exhaustive documentation for ordinary-course Gifts. The procedures in this Section 9 apply proportionally to Gift size, gift type, and specific risk indicators identified below.

**9.3 Donor Identification.** BFTA, Inc. collects and retains, at minimum, the following information for each Gift:

(a) For Gifts of five hundred United States dollars ($500) or greater (or Bitcoin-denominated equivalent): the donor's legal name, primary contact email address, and the donation channel used;
(b) For Gifts of one thousand United States dollars ($1,000) or greater: the donor's mailing address, in addition to the information collected under Section 9.3(a);
(c) For Gifts of ten thousand United States dollars ($10,000) or greater: the donor's mailing address, phone number, and, for corporate or entity donors, the entity's legal name, state of incorporation, and the name of the individual authorized to make the Gift on the entity's behalf; and
(d) For Anonymous Gifts of twenty-five thousand United States dollars ($25,000) or greater: full donor identification as required in Section 9.3(c), collected and reviewed by the Committee under Section 4.3(g). Donor identity remains confidential to the Committee and BFTA, Inc.'s auditors and legal counsel, and is not disclosed publicly.

**9.4 Source of Funds Inquiry.** BFTA, Inc. conducts a written source-of-funds inquiry for each of the following:

(a) Any Gift of fifty thousand United States dollars ($50,000) or greater;
(b) Any Gift, regardless of amount, that presents one or more of the high-risk indicators set forth in Section 9.6; or
(c) Any Gift referred by the Committee for source-of-funds review.

The source-of-funds inquiry consists of a written attestation from the donor addressing:

(i) The general nature of the source of the funds (for example, earned income, business proceeds, investment gains, inheritance, sale of assets, or other lawful source);
(ii) An affirmation that the funds are not the proceeds of any unlawful activity; and
(iii) An affirmation that the Gift is made from the donor's own resources or from an entity the donor is authorized to represent.

Where the donor is a corporate or other entity, the source-of-funds inquiry additionally addresses the entity's principal business activity and ownership structure to the extent reasonably necessary to identify the underlying source of funds.

**9.5 Sanctions Screening.** BFTA, Inc. screens each donor of a Gift of five thousand United States dollars ($5,000) or greater against the following sanctions programs:

(a) The U.S. Department of the Treasury Office of Foreign Assets Control ("OFAC") Specially Designated Nationals ("SDN") List;
(b) The OFAC Sectoral Sanctions Identifications List;
(c) The U.S. Department of Commerce Bureau of Industry and Security Entity List, where the donor is a corporate or other entity; and
(d) Any additional sanctions programs identified by BFTA, Inc.'s legal counsel as applicable to BFTA, Inc.'s operations.

Sanctions screening is conducted at receipt of the Gift and, for recurring donors, at least annually thereafter. Screening is documented in BFTA, Inc.'s donor records.

**9.6 High-Risk Gift Indicators.** BFTA, Inc. applies enhanced due diligence to Gifts presenting any of the following indicators:

(a) The donor is located in, or the Gift originates from, a jurisdiction identified by the U.S. Department of the Treasury Financial Crimes Enforcement Network ("FinCEN") as a jurisdiction of primary money-laundering concern, or a jurisdiction subject to comprehensive U.S. sanctions;
(b) The Gift is structured to fall immediately below a due-diligence, source-of-funds, or Committee-review threshold under this Policy;
(c) The donor has previously withdrawn a pledged Gift, refused to complete standard identification procedures, or provided information that could not be verified;
(d) The Gift is contingent on BFTA, Inc. taking, refraining from, or delaying an action inconsistent with BFTA, Inc.'s mission or tax-exempt status;
(e) The Gift is offered together with a request that BFTA, Inc. return, redirect, or refund a portion of the Gift to any party;
(f) The donor requests that the Gift be recorded at a value materially different from its actual fair market value at receipt; or
(g) Any other circumstance the Committee determines constitutes an elevated risk.

**9.7 Bitcoin-Specific Considerations.** BFTA, Inc. is a Bitcoin-native organization and does not treat privacy-preserving Bitcoin practices, on their own, as an indicator of illicit activity. Consistent with the governing principle in Section 9.2, and with recognition that financial privacy is compatible with lawful conduct, BFTA, Inc. applies the following Bitcoin-specific procedures:

(a) OFAC screening under Section 9.5 applies to Bitcoin addresses identified on the OFAC SDN List. BFTA, Inc. does not accept Bitcoin from an address appearing on the SDN List and returns any funds received from such an address to the extent operationally feasible, in consultation with legal counsel;
(b) BFTA, Inc. does not conduct blanket chain-analysis screening of every incoming Bitcoin Gift and does not treat CoinJoin, Lightning Network, or other privacy-preserving practices as inherent grounds for enhanced review;
(c) For Bitcoin Gifts of one hundred thousand United States dollars ($100,000) or greater (or Bitcoin-denominated equivalent), or for any Bitcoin Gift presenting the high-risk indicators in Section 9.6, BFTA, Inc. may engage a blockchain-analytics provider to review the transaction against the SDN List and known illicit-activity patterns. The scope of any such review is limited to sanctions and known illicit-activity screening and does not extend to general surveillance of the donor's on-chain history; and
(d) Where blockchain-analytics review returns a positive match to sanctioned addresses or known illicit-activity patterns, the Gift is refused under Section 9.8.

**9.8 Refusal for Due Diligence Reasons.** BFTA, Inc. refuses any Gift where:

(a) Sanctions screening under Section 9.5 or Section 9.7 returns a positive match;
(b) The donor refuses to complete a source-of-funds inquiry required under Section 9.4;
(c) The Committee, after review, determines that the source of funds cannot be reasonably established as lawful; or
(d) The Committee, in its judgment, determines that acceptance of the Gift would create a material risk of legal, regulatory, or reputational harm to BFTA, Inc.

Refusal under this Section 9.8 is documented in Committee minutes and in BFTA, Inc.'s donor records. Refusal is communicated to the donor in writing with a general explanation, subject to any legal restrictions on the disclosure of the grounds for refusal.

**9.9 Reporting Obligations.**

(a) IRS Form 8300. BFTA, Inc. files IRS Form 8300 for cash Gifts of more than ten thousand United States dollars ($10,000) received in a single transaction or in related transactions, as required by the Internal Revenue Code. For purposes of Form 8300, "cash" is defined by the IRS and does not include Bitcoin, other digital assets, checks, or wire transfers.
(b) Suspicious Activity. Where BFTA, Inc. becomes aware of specific information suggesting that a Gift is the proceeds of unlawful activity, BFTA, Inc. shall consult legal counsel promptly to determine whether any reporting obligation to law enforcement or FinCEN applies. BFTA, Inc. is not a financial institution and is not subject to the Bank Secrecy Act's Suspicious Activity Report ("SAR") regime as a general matter, but may nonetheless be subject to reporting obligations under other applicable law.
(c) Form 990 Disclosures. Aggregate donor due-diligence activity is not itself a required Form 990 disclosure. Specific Gifts refused for due-diligence reasons are recorded in BFTA, Inc.'s books, whether or not they trigger Form 990 disclosure.

**9.10 Recordkeeping.** Records of donor identification, sanctions screening, source-of-funds inquiries, and any refusals under this Section 9 are retained for a period of not less than seven (7) years from the date of the Gift (or attempted Gift), consistent with the general recordkeeping requirements in Section 14.

**9.11 Training.** The Executive Director and Committee members shall receive periodic training, at intervals of not less than every twenty-four (24) months, on the procedures set forth in this Section 9 and on developments in applicable law affecting nonprofit donor due diligence. Training may be delivered through counsel, industry publications, or continuing-education programs designed for nonprofit boards.

## Section 10. Refusal Criteria

**10.1 Grounds for Refusal.** BFTA, Inc. reserves the right to refuse any Gift, in whole or in part, at its sole discretion. Without limiting the foregoing, BFTA, Inc. shall refuse a Gift where one or more of the following applies:

(a) Acceptance would violate applicable law, including the terms of BFTA, Inc.'s Bylaws or the Internal Revenue Code provisions governing 501(c)(3) organizations;
(b) Acceptance would jeopardize BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt status;
(c) Acceptance would create a material conflict of interest that cannot be adequately managed under BFTA, Inc.'s Conflict of Interest Policy;
(d) The Gift, or the donor-imposed restriction attached to the Gift, is materially inconsistent with BFTA, Inc.'s mission;
(e) The Gift is subject to donor-imposed restrictions that are illegal, that would require BFTA, Inc. to discriminate on the basis of any characteristic protected under applicable law, or that cannot be reasonably honored;
(f) The Gift fails donor due diligence under Section 9;
(g) The Gift is of non-cash property that cannot be liquidated within a reasonable period at a reasonable valuation, or that carries material holding costs disproportionate to expected net proceeds;
(h) Acceptance would create a material reputational risk to BFTA, Inc.; or
(i) Any other circumstance the Committee or the Board determines to be a sufficient ground for refusal.

**10.2 Decision Authority.** The following decision authority applies to Gift refusals:

(a) The Executive Director may refuse a Gift below Committee-review thresholds under Section 4.3, subject to reporting the refusal to the Committee at its next meeting;
(b) The Committee refuses Gifts within its scope under Section 4.3; and
(c) The Board may refuse any Gift and may reverse any Executive Director or Committee refusal decision by majority vote.

**10.3 Communication with the Donor.** A refusal is communicated to the donor in writing, in a manner consistent with the dignity of the donor relationship, within thirty (30) days of the refusal decision. The written communication shall include:

(a) A statement that BFTA, Inc. has declined the Gift;
(b) A general explanation of the reason for refusal, subject to any legal restrictions on disclosure; and
(c) Contact information for follow-up questions from the donor.

Where a refused Gift has already been received by BFTA, Inc. (for example, a Bitcoin transfer or wire transfer that arrived without prior review), BFTA, Inc. shall use commercially reasonable efforts to return the Gift to the donor, subject to any legal restrictions.

**10.4 No Obligation to Accept.** Nothing in this Policy, and nothing in any communication by BFTA, Inc. concerning gift acceptance generally, creates an obligation on BFTA, Inc. to accept any specific Gift. The decision to accept or refuse a Gift rests within the discretion of BFTA, Inc. as set forth in this Section 10 and elsewhere in this Policy.

## Section 11. Pledges and Deferred Gifts

**11.1 Pledges.** A Pledge is a written commitment by a donor to make one or more future Gifts to BFTA, Inc.

(a) Documentation. A Pledge is documented in a Gift Agreement under Section 6.
(b) Enforceability. BFTA, Inc. generally treats Pledges as expressions of donor intent rather than legally enforceable obligations, except where the Gift Agreement expressly provides otherwise and BFTA, Inc. has taken specific action in reliance on the Pledge. BFTA, Inc.'s general practice is not to pursue legal enforcement of unfulfilled Pledges.
(c) Bitcoin Volatility. Pledges denominated in Bitcoin are subject to the volatility disclosure in Section 5.1(f). Pledges denominated in United States dollars but expected to be paid in Bitcoin may be settled at the Bitcoin-denominated equivalent measured at the time of settlement, at the donor's election.
(d) Failure to Fulfill. Where a Pledge is not fulfilled within the timeframe specified in the Gift Agreement, BFTA, Inc. and the donor may amend the Pledge under Section 6.6, cancel the Pledge, or renegotiate the terms.

**11.2 Bequests.** BFTA, Inc. accepts bequests through wills, revocable living trusts, and other testamentary instruments.

(a) Recommended Language. BFTA, Inc. maintains recommended bequest language for donors, provided upon request. The recommended language identifies BFTA, Inc. by legal name and EIN and clarifies the donor's intent as to purpose and use.
(b) Notification. Donors are encouraged, but not required, to notify BFTA, Inc. of an anticipated bequest.
(c) Acceptance. Bequests are accepted subject to the standard Gift-acceptance procedures in this Policy, applied at the time the bequest is received.
(d) Restricted Bequests. Where a bequest is restricted, the impracticability release in Section 8.4 applies. Donors are encouraged to consult with BFTA, Inc. during their estate-planning process to ensure that restrictions can be honored.

**11.3 Charitable Remainder Trusts and Charitable Lead Trusts.** BFTA, Inc. may accept designations as an income beneficiary or remainder beneficiary of a charitable remainder trust or charitable lead trust, subject to Committee review under Section 4.3 and legal counsel review under Section 12. BFTA, Inc. does not act as trustee of a charitable trust absent specific Board approval and adequate administrative capacity.

**11.4 Life Insurance.** BFTA, Inc. may accept designations as a beneficiary or owner of a life insurance policy, subject to Committee review under Section 4.3.

(a) Ownership-Transferred Policies. Where BFTA, Inc. is designated as the owner and beneficiary of a life insurance policy, and the donor has committed to continue paying premiums, the Gift is recorded at the cash surrender value of the policy at the time of transfer. Ongoing premium payments by the donor are recorded as additional Gifts.
(b) Beneficiary-Only Designations. Where BFTA, Inc. is designated only as a beneficiary of a life insurance policy, no Gift is recorded until proceeds are received. BFTA, Inc. is not entitled to enforce continued premium payment against the donor.
(c) Paid-Up Policies. BFTA, Inc. may accept paid-up life insurance policies subject to Committee review of the cash surrender value, the projected death benefit, and any policy-specific considerations.

**11.5 Retirement Account Beneficiary Designations.** BFTA, Inc. accepts designations as a beneficiary of an Individual Retirement Account (IRA), 401(k), 403(b), or similar qualified retirement account. Donors are encouraged to consult their own tax advisors regarding the tax treatment of retirement account beneficiary designations. No Gift is recorded until distribution is received.

**11.6 Charitable Gift Annuities.** BFTA, Inc. does not currently issue charitable gift annuities. This determination is subject to future revision by the Board in consultation with legal counsel, following the establishment of appropriate reserves and state-by-state registration where required.

## Section 12. Legal Counsel Review

**12.1 When Required.** BFTA, Inc. engages legal counsel to review a proposed Gift where:

(a) The Gift is of real property (Section 5.5);
(b) The Gift involves restricted securities, private-company stock, or other illiquid securities (Section 5.4(e));
(c) The Gift includes donor-imposed restrictions that appear novel, ambiguous, or potentially in tension with applicable law or BFTA, Inc.'s tax-exempt status (Section 8);
(d) The Gift involves a charitable remainder trust, charitable lead trust, life insurance policy transfer, or similar deferred-giving instrument (Section 11);
(e) The Gift presents due-diligence concerns identified under Section 9;
(f) The Gift is referred by the Committee or the Board for legal review; or
(g) Refusal of a Gift, or the return of a previously accepted Gift, presents legal complexity that in the judgment of the Executive Director warrants counsel review.

**12.2 Counsel Engagement.** Legal counsel for gift-acceptance matters may be BFTA, Inc.'s standing counsel or specialist counsel engaged for a specific matter. The Executive Director engages counsel within the operating budget approved by the Board. Engagements that exceed the operating budget are approved by the Committee, or by the Board where the Bylaws require Board approval.

**12.3 Cost Allocation.** Legal counsel fees for gift-acceptance review are ordinarily treated as an operating expense of BFTA, Inc. Where a Gift Agreement or applicable law provides for donor payment of counsel fees (for example, in the case of real property gifts under Section 5.5(a)(i)), the Gift Agreement shall so state.

**12.4 Independent Advice for Donors.** Nothing in this Section 12 is intended to provide legal or tax advice to the donor. BFTA, Inc.'s counsel represents BFTA, Inc. Donors are encouraged, and expected, to consult their own independent legal and tax advisors regarding the tax and legal consequences of any Gift.

## Section 13. Receipting and Acknowledgment

**13.1 Timing.** BFTA, Inc. issues a written acknowledgment for each Gift within thirty (30) days of receipt. The acknowledgment satisfies the substantiation requirements of Internal Revenue Code Section 170(f)(8) and IRS Publication 1771.

**13.2 Contents.** Every written acknowledgment shall include:

(a) The name of BFTA, Inc. and its EIN;
(b) The date of the Gift;
(c) A description of the Gift, without a stated dollar value for non-cash property (which is the donor's responsibility to determine);
(d) A statement of whether BFTA, Inc. provided any goods or services in return for the Gift, and if so, a good-faith estimate of the fair market value of those goods or services;
(e) For Bitcoin Gifts, the transaction identifier (on-chain) or payment identifier (Lightning), and the USD-equivalent value at the time of confirmation, provided as a reference and not as a valuation the donor is required to use for tax purposes; and
(f) For Gifts subject to a Gift Agreement, a reference to the executed Gift Agreement.

**13.3 Quid Pro Quo Disclosures.** For Gifts of seventy-five United States dollars ($75) or more where the donor receives goods or services in return, BFTA, Inc. provides a written quid pro quo disclosure statement consistent with IRS Publication 1771.

**13.4 Fair Market Value Determination.** BFTA, Inc. does not appraise Non-Cash Property or determine fair market value for the donor's tax purposes. Donors are responsible for determining and substantiating the fair market value of Non-Cash Property Gifts, including obtaining qualified appraisals where required by the IRS (generally for Gifts of $5,000 or more). BFTA, Inc. cooperates in signing IRS Form 8283 where the donor's appraisal and Gift meet the applicable requirements.

**13.5 Electronic Delivery.** Acknowledgments may be delivered electronically. Electronic acknowledgments satisfy the requirements of Section 13.1 and Section 13.2 provided that they are reasonably calculated to reach the donor and are retained in BFTA, Inc.'s records.

## Section 14. Recordkeeping and Reporting

**14.1 Records Maintained.** BFTA, Inc. maintains, for each Gift, at minimum:

(a) The donor identification information collected under Section 9.3;
(b) The type and amount of the Gift;
(c) Any Gift Agreement, restriction, or donor recognition preference;
(d) The receipt or acknowledgment issued under Section 13;
(e) Any due-diligence records generated under Section 9; and
(f) The recorded allocation of the Gift under the 55/30/10/5 rule (for Unrestricted Gifts) or to the restricted purpose (for Restricted Gifts).

**14.2 Retention Periods.** Records are retained for the following minimum periods:

(a) Gift records generally: seven (7) years from the date of the Gift;
(b) Gift Agreements and related instruments: as provided in Section 6.7;
(c) Due-diligence records under Section 9: seven (7) years from the date of the Gift;
(d) Records related to real property Gifts, deferred Gifts, and life insurance Gifts: the life of the underlying asset plus seven (7) years thereafter; and
(e) Any records required to be retained for a longer period by applicable law: for the period so required.

**14.3 Annual Transparency Report.** BFTA, Inc. publishes an annual transparency report disclosing, at minimum:

(a) Aggregate Gifts received during the fiscal year, categorized by Gift type;
(b) The 55/30/10/5 allocation of Unrestricted Gifts, with actual expenditure against each allocation category;
(c) Restricted-fund activity, including balances and stewardship progress;
(d) Named grantees, named sponsors, and named artist-support recipients, subject to donor and grantee anonymity elections;
(e) HODL Vault balance and activity, consistent with BFTA, Inc.'s Endowment Management Policy;
(f) Aggregate Gift refusals during the fiscal year, without identifying the specific donors; and
(g) Related-party transactions, consistent with Section 4.6 of this Policy and BFTA, Inc.'s Conflict of Interest Policy.

**14.4 Form 990 Disclosures.** BFTA, Inc. files IRS Form 990 annually and discloses, at minimum:

(a) The existence of this Policy on Form 990, Part VI, Section B, Line 12a (see Section 1.4);
(b) Related-party transactions on Form 990, Schedule L, as applicable;
(c) Substantial contributors on Form 990, Schedule B (subject to public-disclosure limits for 501(c)(3) public charities);
(d) Non-cash contributions and their reporting details; and
(e) Any other disclosures required by the Form 990 instructions.

**14.5 State Filings.** BFTA, Inc. complies with state charitable-solicitation registration and reporting requirements applicable to the states in which it solicits Gifts, including registration and annual reporting with the New York State Office of the Attorney General Charities Bureau.
