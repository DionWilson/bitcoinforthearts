# Podcaster Acknowledgment Letter — Template

⚠️ **Important:** Have your CPA review and approve this template before sending the first one. The language below is consistent with IRS Publication 526 but should be blessed by your tax preparer for your specific 501(c)(3) situation.

---

## Why this is an "acknowledgment letter," NOT a "tax receipt"

IRS Publication 526 explicitly states: **the value of contributed services is not tax-deductible to the donor.** Even when the service has a clear fair market value (e.g., "my standard hosting rate is $5,000"), the donor cannot deduct it.

What you **can** legitimately do is issue a written acknowledgment that:

1. **Documents** the service contribution and its fair market value
2. **Recognizes** the contribution publicly and formally
3. **Distinguishes** the contributed service (not deductible) from any out-of-pocket expenses (which ARE deductible)

This letter is gold for the podcaster's own records, marketing one-sheet, sponsor pitches for their own show, and year-end PR. Many podcasters value this nearly as much as a tax receipt would have been.

## Worked example with real numbers

If a podcaster tells you their normal rate is $5,000 to host a 4-hour event, and they donated 2 hours of on-air time + prep + 1 social post, the letter would say:

- Hours contributed: **2 hours on-air + 30 minutes prep**
- Fair market value: **~$2,500** *(using their pro-rated rate of $5,000 ÷ 4hr = $1,250/hr × 2hr = $2,500)*

Always use the podcaster's **own quoted rate** as the basis. If they say "I usually charge $X for this," that's the FMV. Don't inflate or guess.

---

## What to file alongside this letter

For each acknowledgment letter sent, keep in BFTA's records:

1. **The signed/sent copy** (PDF in your nonprofit records folder)
2. **Documentation of the FMV basis** — a screenshot of the podcaster's rate sheet, an email where they quoted the rate, or your written notes from a verbal quote (date, who said it, what was said)
3. **Tracking entry in the spreadsheet** — `tabs/podcaster-hosts.csv` has an "Acknowledgment Letter Sent?" column. Fill it in.

This documentation matters in two scenarios: **annual transparency reporting** (you'll publicly report the value of in-kind services received) and **IRS audit** (rare for a 501(c)(3) at your scale, but the documentation must exist).

---

## What NOT to do

- ❌ **Do not** issue a "Donation Receipt" or "Tax Receipt" for the value of contributed services. That is misleading and not legally permitted.
- ❌ **Do not** put a dollar amount on the letter and call it deductible.
- ❌ **Do not** record the contributed service value as cash revenue on BFTA's books. (Donated services may be recorded as in-kind contributions on the financial statements only if they meet specific GAAP criteria, which podcaster hosting generally does not. Your accountant will know.)

---

## What you CAN issue a real tax receipt for

If a podcaster (or any donor) gives you any of the following, those **ARE** tax-deductible and you should issue a proper donation receipt:

- A cash donation of any amount
- A Bitcoin donation of any amount
- Tangible goods (e.g., they donate their hardware wallet inventory for the silent auction → fair market value of the goods is deductible)
- Travel and out-of-pocket expenses they paid in service to BFTA (mileage, hotel, flights — itemized)
