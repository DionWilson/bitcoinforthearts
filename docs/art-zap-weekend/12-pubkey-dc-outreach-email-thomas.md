## Why the email is structured this way

- **Personal acknowledgment first** — "we've crossed paths a few times" before any business — Bitcoin venue partners care about the relationship, not the transaction
- **Katie acknowledgment prevents political miss** — letting her know upfront that you've looped her in shows good faith and prevents an awkward "I thought I was handling this" moment internally at Pubkey
- **AEA / DTH / Ailey credentials in second paragraph** — gives you instant arts-world credibility that distinguishes you from generic "crypto founder" outreach Thomas probably gets every week
- **"What we're planning" before "what we're asking"** — they need to understand the event before they evaluate the ask. Reverse that order and the ask feels presumptuous.
- **"Where Pubkey comes in" as a header** — telegraphs the ask is coming, makes the email scannable
- **The ask is a partnership, not a rental** — frame it as something you're building together. Venues are far more receptive to partnership language than rental language because partnerships have upside for them.
- **The "what you'd get" section is concrete and longer than the ask** — quantifies the value flowing back to Pubkey so the partnership is obviously two-sided
- **Vegas urgency** — gives Thomas a real reason to respond quickly without manufacturing pressure
- **Two paths to respond (call OR email)** — lowers friction. Many venue people prefer email; calling them creates an obstacle.
- **Closing is warm but professional** — "Looking forward to building something together" beats both "Looking forward to your reply" (cold) and "I really hope you can help us!" (needy)
