## Why this email is structured this way

- **"Yes" comes first.** Julie put real thought into her email. Burying acceptance behind throat-clearing reads as defensive. Lead with adoption, then the asks.
- **"This is a meaningful improvement on what I had"** — explicitly naming that she made the plan better is more powerful than generic thanks. It tells her that her input changed something real.
- **The cost ask is specific.** "$1K, $5K, or $10K" gives her concrete anchors to react to instead of an open-ended "what should we budget?" — much easier for her to respond. Asking for a range is also lower stakes than asking for a quote.
- **The names ask is framed around her judgment.** "Even one or two names from someone with your judgment is worth a hundred Craigslist responses" treats her as the expert she is. People respond to being treated as experts.
- **The Pubkey intel paragraph reframes her warning into a strategic advantage.** She gave you something the venue itself couldn't have told you about their own limitations. The reply uses that to make the Pubkey conversation cleaner.
- **The Val question is honest** — better to ask "remind me who you mean" than to nod and pretend, then have a confused conversation later
- **Closing acknowledges her time**, not just her advice. Board members at small nonprofits give time, not money. Recognize that explicitly.
