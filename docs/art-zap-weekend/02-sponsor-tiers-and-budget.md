## Why $5K – $25K Is the Right Range for *This* Event

### Why $25K (not $50K) for Title

- You are a first-time event with no precedent metrics. Sponsors at $50K+ want case studies, prior-year numbers, and audited audience data — none of which you have yet.
- $25K is the sweet spot where a Bitcoin-native company can write the check without a board meeting (CMO discretion at most BTC companies) but still feel meaningful enough to warrant naming rights.
- After the pilot, the Title tier can scale to $50K for a 2027 event with proven numbers.

### Why $10K for Supporting

- $10K is the standard "real but not bet-the-farm" sponsor check in the Bitcoin and arts spaces. Same range as a Bitcoin Park sponsorship slot or a small NYC arts gala table.
- Three slots gives flexibility: if Title doesn't close, three Supporting checks ($30K) actually *exceeds* one Title.

### Why $5K for Friend

- $5K is the entry point that makes the difference between "we can pay artists" and "we can also pay producers, fund the venue, and feed people." It's the workhorse tier.
- Five slots — many small Bitcoin businesses (wallets, exchanges, podcasts, hardware vendors) will sponsor at $5K who would never sponsor at $10K.
- Five mentions per day across the show is sustainable; ten mentions would feel spammy.

### Why no $1K or $500 tier

- Below $5K, sponsor management cost (graphics, scheduling reads, social posts, invoicing, follow-up) exceeds the revenue. Push smaller donors toward the **Sovereign Circle** memberships ($5–$101/month) and direct one-time donations during the stream.

## How Event Costs Reconcile With the 55/30/10/5 Model — Important

You raised a real concern: how can BFTA fund a major event without breaking the published transparent allocation? **Three legal and ethical paths.** We recommend Option A and use B and C only as needed.

### ✅ Option A — Treat the event as part of the 30% Programs bucket (recommended)

The 30% Programs bucket in BFTA's published policy is *defined* as workshops, residencies, co-productions, and live productions. **The Art + Zap Weekend IS a program.** Event hard costs (production, A/V, performer honoraria, venue, finale) come naturally out of the 30% bucket. The 55% to direct artist grants includes the **21 featured-artist honoraria** as the first tranche of those grants.

Under this framing, no policy needs to change. The model is honored exactly as published, and sponsors can be told: **"55% of every sponsor dollar goes to direct artist grants — 21 artists this event get their share. 30% goes to programs — including the production of this event. 10% to operations. 5% to the HODL Vault endowment."**

This is the cleanest, most defensible position with sponsors, donors, board, and regulators.

### ⚙️ Option B — Restricted gifts (use selectively)

Sponsors can legally restrict their gift to a specific use ("This $10,000 is restricted to event production costs"). Restricted gifts don't flow through the standard allocation — they go straight to the restricted purpose. This is normal nonprofit practice. Use sparingly because too many restricted gifts breaks the unrestricted operating model and signals dependency on individual sponsor preferences.

**When to use:** if a sponsor specifically asks for their dollars to fund one specific thing (e.g., the venue, the Broadway performer's honorarium, hardware wallet gifts for artists). Document the restriction in the sponsor agreement. Don't volunteer it.

### ⚖️ Option C — Board resolution carving out event costs (only if A+B don't cover)

The board can pass a one-time resolution: *"Sponsorship dollars from Art + Zap Weekend will first fund documented event hard costs, with remainder allocated per the standard 55/30/10/5 model."* Must be:

- Disclosed to sponsors at the time of solicitation (in writing in the sponsor agreement)
- Reflected in board minutes
- Reported in the 2026 annual transparency report

Legal but a credibility cost. Only use if Options A + B don't cover hard costs, which they should — see the budget scenarios below.

### Recommended language for sponsor conversations

> "Bitcoin For The Arts allocates every dollar through our public 55/30/10/5 model: 55% to direct artist grants, 30% to programs, 10% to operations, 5% to the HODL Vault. Art + Zap Weekend lives inside that 30% Programs allocation — production costs, venue, A/V, and event-related staffing. Your sponsorship feeds the same model as every other dollar BFTA receives, including direct payments to the 21 artists you'll see on the broadcast."

## Artist Participation Policy

**Featured artists participate as mission-supporters of BFTA's 501(c)(3) cause — no honorarium, no fee, no quid pro quo.**

This is by board direction. The reasoning:

- **Removes any quid pro quo.** Artists are *not* being compensated for their participation. They are donating their time and creative work in support of a 501(c)(3) cause, exactly as a volunteer would. This eliminates legal ambiguity around tax-exempt org compensation rules.
- **Reinforces grassroots ethos.** "Artists giving their time so other artists can be funded" is a stronger founding-event story than "artists paid to perform."
- **Frees the entire 55% bucket to fund the Q3 nationwide micro-grant cycle.** Every dollar in the artist-grants bucket from this event seeds grants for *more* artists nationwide than just the 21 on the broadcast.
- **Audience tips remain a real upside for artists.** Each artist's personal Lightning address is shown during their segment as a tip jar — direct tips are 100% theirs, outside BFTA's books.

### What artists DO get

- A clipped highlight reel of their segment (theirs to use anywhere)
- Permanent archive on YouTube and Nostr with full credit and links
- Personal LN address shown on-screen during their segment as an audience tip jar (100% of direct tips are theirs)
- Social posts from BFTA's channels and partner podcaster networks
- Invitation to the Monday in-person finale at Pubkey
- A written acknowledgment of their contribution from BFTA (NOT a tax receipt — services aren't deductible per IRS Pub 526; same template as the podcaster acknowledgment letter)
- Optional inclusion in the silent art auction (artist credited as donor)

### Surprise post-event gratitude payment (board-discretionary)

If the event is exceptionally successful, the board may — at its sole discretion, **after** the event closes — approve a one-time gratitude payment to participating artists as a thank-you. **This must remain a genuine surprise to artists in advance, or it stops being a surprise and becomes implicit compensation.**

**Codified threshold formula** (board may revisit per event):

> *If cash sponsorship revenue exceeds $50,000 AND the 55% Direct Artist Grants bucket has at least $15,000 of surplus after Q3 nationwide micro-grant commitments are funded, the board MAY (not shall) approve a one-time gratitude payment of up to $250 per participating artist, distributed within 30 days of the event.*

**If approved:**
- Announced live at the Monday finale as a surprise reveal (dramatic + photographable + builds artist loyalty)
- Paid in BTC to each artist's confirmed Lightning address within 30 days
- Documented as a post-event recognition expense, not a contracted honorarium
- Artist acknowledgment letter updated to note any gratitude payment received

**If NOT approved:**
- No payment is made; no commitment was ever in place
- Artists never knew there was a possibility, so no expectation is broken

### Broadway headline performer — the one exception

The Monday-night Broadway-caliber headliner receives an **honorarium of approximately $2,500 paid from the 30% Programs bucket.** This is the only paid performer.

Why this exception:

- A Broadway-caliber live in-person performance with potential travel is a fundamentally different ask than a remote 25-min broadcast slot
- Asking a Broadway performer to do an in-person Monday-night show entirely for free would block the strongest finale draw
- This is a **production cost** for the in-person finale event, not a participation fee — paid from Programs bucket, not Artist Grants bucket
- Disclosed to all parties (sponsors, board, artists if asked) as the one paid performance role at the event

If a Broadway-caliber performer in your network IS willing to do it for free as a mission contribution, the saved $2,500 flows back to the Programs bucket (or to the post-event gratitude fund if approved).

## Audience Donation / Zap Policy

**The on-screen donation QR is BFTA's Lightning address (`donate@bitcoinforthearts.org`) for the full 8 hours of broadcast.** This is a fundraiser FOR Bitcoin For The Arts.

During each artist's ~25-minute segment, that artist's personal Lightning address is shown in a secondary on-screen lower-third labeled **"Tip [Artist] Directly"** as a tip jar — given that artists are not being paid an honorarium, the tip jar is shown more prominently and for the full duration of their segment. The main BFTA donation QR remains visible throughout. Direct tips to the artist's personal address are 100% theirs and not counted against BFTA's revenue.

This policy is honest with all three audiences:

- **Donors** see one clear default ("support BFTA") with a clear secondary path ("tip the artist") — no confusion about where their sats go
- **Artists** know they're contributing as mission supporters and that any direct audience tips are their upside (no honorarium expectation)
- **Sponsors** know their event is a real fundraiser — donor flow defaults to BFTA, and the 55% bucket is freed to fund Q3 nationwide micro-grants since participating artists are uncompensated

## Producer Staffing — Solo vs Hired Professional

You raised this as a real concern. Solo producers are doable but high-risk. Recommended thresholds:

| Funding level | Producer setup | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Floor ($25K) | Solo producer (Dion or 1 trained volunteer) | $0 (volunteer) |
| Target ($45K) | **Hired professional NYC producer** + 1 volunteer second seat | ~$1,500–$3,000 |
| Stretch ($80K) | Hired producer team (lead + technical director) + 1 volunteer | ~$3,000–$6,000 |

NYC freelance live producers run $500–$1,500/day. For 2 broadcast days that's $1,000–$3,000 (plus optional rehearsal day). At the target funding level this is a no-brainer upgrade — significantly reduces single-point-of-failure risk and gives you a backup if anything goes wrong on day-of.
