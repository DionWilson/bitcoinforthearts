## Why the forwarding template is structured the way it is

- **Third-party voice from Trey, not BFTA voice.** Sponsors are far more receptive to "I've been working with X, you should look at this" than to a BFTA-direct pitch arriving via Trey. Preserves the warm-intro dynamic.
- **AEA / Dance Theatre of Harlem / Lion King in paragraph 2.** Instant arts-world credibility for any sponsor who doesn't already know BFTA. Distinguishes from generic "crypto founder asking for sponsorship" outreach.
- **"September 2026 (date locking imminent)" framing.** Honest about the date being not-yet-locked while still being concrete enough for a sponsor to evaluate against their calendar. Doesn't commit BFTA to a date Pubkey hasn't confirmed.
- **Tiers with one-line value summaries, not full leave-behind detail.** The forwarding email shouldn't be a wall of text. Sponsor sees the tiers, gets curious, opens the attached PDF for full detail.
- **Single CTA: `sponsor@bitcoinforthearts.org` OR reply to Trey.** Two paths, both clean. Lets the sponsor pick whichever feels lighter — both end in the same place (a 30-min call with Dion).
- **The "[one personalized sentence Trey writes per contact]" placeholder.** Forces Trey to add one personal touch per send. A generic forward gets ignored; one personalized line is the difference between a yes and a delete.
- **Closes with "Thanks for taking a moment with this."** Warm, not needy. Doesn't oversell. Treats the sponsor as a peer.
