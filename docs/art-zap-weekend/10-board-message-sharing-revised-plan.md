Board,

Following up on the concerns raised in our last conversation about the original 7-day Zap-a-Thon — particularly around producer burnout, audience attrition, host coordination, and how the event would be funded against our published 55/30/10/5 model.

I've taken those concerns seriously and revised the plan top to bottom. The revised plan still delivers our core goal — 21 featured artists, a global broadcast, and a meaningful in-person culminating event — but does so in a format that is materially more manageable for our small team and stronger from a sponsor and donor perspective.

**Linked here for your review:** [Google Slides deck — Revised Art + Zap Weekend Plan]

The deck is structured for a board context — every concern you raised gets a direct answer, and every meaningful change has a one-line rationale. It is 13 slides and should take 10–15 minutes to read carefully.

The biggest changes from the original plan, summarized:

- **Format:** moved from 7 consecutive days × 3 hrs each (21 hours) to **2 days × 4 hours of broadcast (Saturday + Sunday)** plus a **separate Monday-night in-person finale at Pubkey** with a Broadway-caliber headliner (Broadway is dark on Mondays, which makes top NYC talent uniquely available).
- **Podcaster hosts:** moved from 7 daily hosts × full days to **4 podcasters per broadcast day at 1–2 hours each**, with optional 25-minute "drop-in" slots for the biggest names. This is a far lighter ask, which means more yeses from podcasters whose audiences we actually want.
- **Producer staffing:** scales with funding. Solo at floor, hired professional NYC producer at target funding, full producer team at stretch. Removes the single-point-of-failure risk you flagged.
- **Sponsorship:** structured as 3 tiers — $25K Title (1 slot), $10K Supporting (3 slots), $5K Friend (5 slots). 9 slots total, $80K stretch / $45K realistic target. In-kind credited dollar-for-dollar.
- **Funding the event without breaking 55/30/10/5:** event production costs flow naturally through the 30% Programs bucket (which is what 30% is for in our published policy). No policy change required. Sponsor-facing language is in the deck.
- **Artist participation (per the board's later direction):** featured artists participate as **mission supporters of our 501(c)(3) cause** — no honoraria, no quid pro quo. The entire 55% Direct Artist Grants bucket from this event flows to Q3 nationwide micro-grants. At the target funding scenario, sponsor dollars from this single event seed roughly **49 micro-grants** for artists nationwide. That's a stronger sponsor story than the prior honorarium model.
- **Audience donation flow:** the on-screen donation QR is BFTA's Lightning address for the entire 8 hours of broadcast. Each artist's personal Lightning address is shown during their segment as a tip jar — direct tips are 100% theirs. This is a fundraiser FOR BFTA, not a tip-jar event.
- **Surprise post-event gratitude payment** (board-discretionary, internal-only): if cash sponsorship exceeds $50K AND the 55% bucket has at least $15K of surplus after Q3 commitments, the board may approve a one-time gratitude payment up to $250 per participating artist as a surprise reveal at the Monday finale. This is INTERNAL ONLY — never disclosed to artists or sponsors in advance, or it stops being a surprise and recreates the quid pro quo we're avoiding. Documented in the deck.
- **Venue:** Pubkey DC as primary, Pubkey NYC or another in-kind NYC venue as backup. An NYC venue contributed in-kind is one of the strongest sponsor opportunities on our menu.

**What I'm asking from each of you:**

1. **Read the deck carefully** before our next conversation. I've put effort into making it skimmable but the trade-offs are in the details.
2. **Approve or push back** on the 5 specific items in Slide 9 (revised format, sponsor structure, 30%-Programs reconciliation, producer staffing scale, per-board-member deliverables).
3. **Take on one specific deliverable** as outlined on Slide 10. Each ask is one-and-done and uses your existing network.
4. **Avi and Kyle** — separately I'll send you the conference pitch runner so you have material to make sponsor asks at the conference next week. That's `09-board-conference-pitch-runner.md` in our shared docs folder.
5. **Reply with thoughts** before [date — suggest 3–5 days out]. Even a quick "approved" or "I have questions" helps me know where to focus.

A standing offer: if any slide raises a real question or concern, I'd rather hear it now in writing than discover it on the day of the event. Better to redesign the format one more time than to launch a flawed version of it.

Thank you for the work you've put into pressure-testing this so far. The revisions made the plan significantly stronger.

## Tone notes

- Both versions lead with **"you raised these concerns, and here's how I addressed them."** That framing is non-negotiable for board comms — it signals you actually listened, which builds trust for the next round of pushback you'll inevitably need.
- The "push back is welcome — better to redesign now than launch a flawed version" close is genuine, not throat-clearing. It actually invites scrutiny, which is what good boards do best.
- The deliverable ask (Slide 10's per-board-member deliverable) is **one specific thing per person** — that's how you actually get board members to do work. Open-ended asks die in inboxes.
