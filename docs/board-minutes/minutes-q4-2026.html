5.Grant awards

The board reviewed three funded projects, totaling $3,900. Of that, $2,900 has been paid.

  • Ainsley Costello. $2,500. Paid. A Nashville musician who opened Bitcoin Arts Park at the Midwest Bitcoin Summit, performed with her band, and sat on the culture panel. The grant covered the cost of appearing: the band, food, and lodging. Funded because she is building Bitcoin into a working music career, in public.
  • Aksana Zasinets (5Ksana). $400. Paid. Embroidery for Freedom. She has made Bitcoin-themed physical art by hand since 2017. The grant supports that work, and a finished embroidered piece comes to Bitcoin For The Arts. Funded because the craft is real, it takes time, and it reaches a U.S. audience.
  • Christopher Arcella. $1,000. Approved, not yet paid. The Bitcoin Executor, a narrative feature film in late post-production. The award supports the theatrical plan, marketing, and ticketing that can settle in bitcoin. Bitcoin For The Arts will be credited in the end titles. Payment follows the signed grant agreement.

6.Programming that generates funding

The Chair framed the discussion: Midwest showed what the money does, and the grants are the mission. The question for the board is what BFTA runs next that a donor can see and support. Three programs were presented:

  • Grants. Three named projects, a reason for each, and a public record of what the money did.
  • Artist education webinars. The lessons are on the website with a signup. A live date has not been set.
  • An event in Nashville. A program in the same week as the Bitcoin Conference, with a place and a date for donors to fund.

Questions put to the board: which single program to ask for between now and January; who can introduce a donor, foundation, or partner before the next meeting; and whether the public ask should be tied to the grants, the webinars, or Nashville.
