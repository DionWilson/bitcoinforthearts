# Volunteer Onboarding

Reusable playbook for getting a new BFTA volunteer from "yes, I'm in" to "actually contributing in a defined way." Designed to be 10 minutes of fill-in-the-blanks per new volunteer instead of starting from scratch each time.

## When to use this

Use this when:

- A new volunteer has said yes to working with BFTA (post-interview, post-handshake)
- You need to send them a welcome / kickoff email within 24-48 hours
- You want a defined weekly rhythm (meeting + tasks + Bitcoin education) instead of ad-hoc check-ins

Don't use this for:

- Board prospects (different process — that's cultivation, not onboarding)
- Major donors (cultivation, not volunteer work)
- One-off contractors with a specific deliverable and end date
- Featured artists (different relationship — they're not volunteering, they're being featured)

## The files in this folder

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `PLAYBOOK.md` | The complete playbook — welcome email template, weekly meeting agenda format, 8-week Bitcoin education ladder, common task sets by volunteer type, pitfalls, how to scale down or graduate. |
| `README.md` | This file. Orientation only. |

## The 10-minute fill-in-the-blanks flow

1. Open `PLAYBOOK.md`.
2. Find the **Welcome Email Template** section.
3. Copy the email into your email composer.
4. Fill in the bracketed placeholders:
   - `{{VOLUNTEER_FIRST_NAME}}`
   - `{{MEETING_DAY}}` + `{{MEETING_TIME}}`
   - `{{ROLE_FOCUS}}` (pick from the role types in the playbook — e.g. "artist outreach," "sponsor outreach")
   - `{{TASK_1}}`, `{{TASK_2}}`, `{{TASK_3}}` (pick from the role-specific task sets in the playbook, or write your own)
5. Read through once for voice consistency.
6. Send.

That's it. Everything else (the weekly meeting structure, the Bitcoin education ladder, the materials inventory) is identical across volunteers and is referenced in the email by link to the website + the Signal group.

## What the playbook captures that you'd lose otherwise

Things that took a meeting or two with Anna and Ben to figure out, now codified:

- **Three tasks max for week 1.** Not five. Not "go set up everything." Three.
- **Bitcoin literacy is explicitly NOT a prerequisite.** Removes the "I'm not qualified" anxiety underneath enthusiasm.
- **Signal is the channel.** Permission to ask "small" questions any time.
- **Weekly meeting structure is the same every week:** 5 min recap → 10 min Q&A → 10 min Bitcoin lesson → 5 min next tasks. Predictability lowers the cognitive cost of showing up.
- **The 8-week Bitcoin ladder.** One concept per week. Cumulative, not flooding.
- **Telling a volunteer "don't do MORE than the assigned tasks"** is more important than telling them what the tasks are. New volunteers over-do week 1 and burn out by week 3.
- **The volunteer's "I'm new" perspective is an asset.** Have them flag where messaging confuses them — that's data, not noise.

## Maintenance

Update the playbook when:

- A volunteer onboarding teaches us something the playbook should know. The next person benefits.
- The Bitcoin education ladder needs updating (new tools emerge, BTCPay or Alby UIs change, etc.)
- A new volunteer role type emerges (e.g. social media manager, research assistant). Add the role and its task set.

Don't update the playbook just because:

- This particular volunteer is unusual. Specific instructions to that person go in their personalized version of the email; the playbook stays generic.

## Privacy note

This folder is suitable for committing to the repo — it's a process artifact, not personal correspondence. The actual emails sent to specific named volunteers don't get committed here; only the template does. Same principle as the partnership pitch docs (which describe a process and pitch architecture, not personal exchanges).
