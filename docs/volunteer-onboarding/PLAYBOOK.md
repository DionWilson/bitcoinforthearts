## Weekly meeting agenda format

Same 30-minute structure every week. The predictability lowers the cognitive cost of showing up and prevents the meeting from drifting into vague check-in.

| Block | Time | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Recap** | 5 min | Volunteer reports what they did last week — what worked, what didn't, anything that surprised them. Dion listens, doesn't redirect yet. |
| **Q&A** | 10 min | Volunteer's open questions from the week. Bitcoin, BFTA, outreach phrasing, anything. Dion answers; if a question is too big for the meeting, captures it and answers later via Signal. |
| **Bitcoin lesson** | 10 min | One concept from the 8-week ladder. Dion teaches, volunteer asks clarifying questions. Goal: by week 8, volunteer can hold a basic Bitcoin conversation without panicking. |
| **Next tasks** | 5 min | Three tasks for the coming week, named explicitly. Volunteer repeats them back so both parties confirm the same expectations. |

Don't extend past 30 minutes by default. If something big needs more time, schedule a separate follow-up rather than blowing through the cap.

## 8-week Bitcoin education ladder

One concept per week. Cumulative, not flooding. Each lesson is 10 minutes inside the weekly meeting; supplemental reading is optional, not required.

### Week 1 — Why Bitcoin matters for the arts

The *story*, not the tech. Federal arts funding is collapsing. Fiat is debasing. Working artists are exposed. Bitcoin is the foundation underneath an arts funding model that doesn't depend on the institutional rails that are failing. The 55/30/10/5 allocation. The HODL Vault as endowment.

Optional reading: BFTA's About page + the ARPA Cliff Substack piece.

### Week 2 — How donors give in Bitcoin

The mechanics. Wallets (custodial vs. self-custody at a high level). Lightning vs on-chain (instant + cheap vs slow + final). BTCPay Server is the merchant-side processor BFTA uses. When someone donates, here's the chain of events.

Optional: have them walk through the BFTA donate page and describe what they see.

### Week 3 — What Nostr is and why BFTA uses it

Nostr in one sentence: a decentralized social protocol with built-in money via Lightning ("zaps"). BFTA has a Nostr presence because that's where Bitcoin-native creators live. They've already set up their Primal profile in week 1; now they understand why.

### Week 4 — Lightning Network in plain English

Lightning lets payments below ~$50 work without insane fees. It's why BFTA can give $5 zaps to artists and have it make sense. The micro-grant model only exists because Lightning exists.

### Week 5 — Bitcoin culture

Who's who. Big events (Bitcoin 2026 Vegas, Bitcoin Is For Everyone Portland, Camp Nakamoto, BIFE, the BPI policy day in DC). Conferences are where BFTA finds artists and donors. Bitcoiners hate fiat for specific philosophical reasons; understanding that helps the volunteer pitch better.

### Week 6 — Wallet basics

How to receive a small amount of BTC. The volunteer should set up at least a Lightning wallet (Wallet of Satoshi or similar custodial is fine for a beginner). Send them 100 sats from BFTA's hot wallet so they've actually received Bitcoin. The experience demystifies it.

### Week 7 — The 55/30/10/5 allocation in detail

Why BFTA uses this specific split. What goes in each bucket. Why the HODL Vault is 5% — it's the long-term endowment that compounds. Show them the year-end-2026-goal PDF so they understand the math.

### Week 8 — Where Bitcoin meets the arts globally

Case studies. Artists in Argentina, Nigeria, Lebanon — places where local currency debases visibly and Bitcoin solves a real problem. Beth Fletcher / Lady Nakamoto's work. The bigger picture of Bitcoin as cultural infrastructure, not just finance.

### Beyond week 8

By week 8 the volunteer should be conversant enough to onboard the NEXT volunteer at a basic level. Move toward more autonomy and project ownership. They might co-host the weekly meeting with you, or take over outreach for a specific artist segment, or start drafting a section of the next newsletter.

## Common pitfalls + how to spot them

Things that have made earlier onboardings harder than they needed to be, with the fix in each case:

### Pitfall: volunteer over-does week 1

**Symptom:** They reply within hours with all three tasks completed plus extra work they invented themselves.

**Why it matters:** New-volunteer enthusiasm + your enthusiasm for what they're doing creates a pace neither of you can sustain. Burnout by week 3.

**Fix:** Slow the cadence deliberately. Reply praising the work, then assign just three more tasks for the next week — same size. Don't reward over-delivery by giving them more.

### Pitfall: volunteer goes silent after week 2

**Symptom:** Two missed Signal pings, no response on what they did this week.

**Why it matters:** Silence usually means they got stuck, felt embarrassed, or got overwhelmed — not that they lost interest. If you wait, the silence becomes the relationship.

**Fix:** Within 3-4 days of silence, send a low-pressure note: *"Hey, just checking in. Anything stuck? Even a one-line response is fine."* Most volunteers will re-engage if the reach-out is gentle. If silence persists past a second note, accept they've drifted and don't push further.

### Pitfall: volunteer asks no questions

**Symptom:** Every Q&A block is "no questions, just want to listen."

**Why it matters:** This is usually a confidence issue, not an actual lack of questions. They're worried about looking dumb.

**Fix:** Tell them you want them asking *more* questions, not fewer. Make a point of saying "great question" when they finally ask one. Demonstrate that you don't know all the answers either — when something is at the edge of your knowledge, say so. Modeling intellectual humility makes it safe for them to do the same.

### Pitfall: volunteer wants to skip the Bitcoin lessons

**Symptom:** "I get the idea — can we just talk about the outreach?"

**Why it matters:** They will hit a wall during outreach when someone asks them a Bitcoin question and they don't have an answer. The lessons aren't filler; they're scaffolding.

**Fix:** Keep the 10-minute Bitcoin lesson. Frame it as "I want you to be able to answer the questions sponsors and artists will ask you — that's what this 10 minutes is for, not Bitcoin trivia." Tying it to their goals makes it stick.

### Pitfall: volunteer drifts into being a "discussion partner" instead of a contributor

**Symptom:** Weekly meetings run long with brainstorming, vibing, philosophical Bitcoin chat. No real tasks getting done.

**Why it matters:** Volunteers who become "talking buddies" feel valuable but don't move BFTA forward, and eventually they get bored when the conversations don't translate into impact.

**Fix:** Keep the meeting agenda strict — recap, Q&A, lesson, next tasks. If they want a longer Bitcoin conversation, schedule it separately. Their tasks need to be the focus of every meeting.

## How to scale down or graduate a volunteer

Not every relationship needs the full 8-week cadence forever. Recognize when to adjust.

### Scale down — if they're capable but life is busy

Some volunteers can deliver well but only have 2-3 hours a month. That's a real contribution; don't ask for more.

- Move from weekly to **monthly** meetings.
- Reduce tasks to one per month.
- Keep them in the Signal thread but don't expect daily engagement.
- They're now a "monthly contributor" rather than an "active volunteer." Communicate the shift explicitly — *"Sounds like monthly works better for your bandwidth right now. Let's switch to that and revisit in 3 months."*

### Graduate — if they've maxed out the 8-week program

By week 8, a strong volunteer has the context to take real ownership of something. Possible next moves:

- **Project lead.** Hand them ownership of one specific BFTA workstream (the September event sponsor outreach, the next newsletter, an artist segment of outreach). Define the scope, set their authority level, then step back.
- **Co-host the weekly meeting.** They run the agenda; you become the participant. Develops their ownership muscle and frees you up.
- **Onboard the next volunteer.** They're now the experienced one. Have them deliver the week-1 talk-through for the next person coming in. Reinforces their learning and reduces your load.
- **Move to advisor status.** If they're consistent, capable, and aligned, formal advisor / committee-member roles open up. That's the path from "volunteer" to "trusted leader."

### Quietly drop — if they're not delivering and not responding

Volunteers who have ghosted twice and not responded to soft re-engagement are gone. Don't carry them as "active." Mark them as inactive in your tracker, stop sending materials, and free up the energy you were spending. If they re-emerge, welcome them back with a fresh week-1 framing — don't expect them to pick up where they left off.

## Materials inventory volunteers should have access to

What every volunteer should be able to point at within their first two weeks:

| Material | Where | Use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **BFTA website** | bitcoinforthearts.org | Public-facing reference for any conversation |
| **Sponsor sheet PDF** | bitcoinforthearts.org/art-zap-weekend (linked) | Attach to outreach emails; reference during sponsor pitches |
| **Year-end 2026 Goal one-pager** | `docs/year-end-2026-goal/` in this repo | Hand to donors who ask "what does success look like?" |
| **Donor / artist tracking sheet** | Google Sheets (volunteer gets edit access) | Log who's been contacted, when, response status |
| **Signal team thread** | The shared BFTA team chat | Day-to-day questions, quick announcements |
| **BFTA Nostr profile** | npub15rnn220qfdyhpgv9apjt38nadc0dzj8a7zpcrd2q4spq5apvvt2suswnaw | The org's Nostr identity; volunteer should follow + understand it |
| **Latest newsletter issue** | docs/newsletter/ (this repo) or the Beehiiv archive | Stylistic reference + content lookup for past announcements |
| **Brand kit** | bitcoinforthearts.org/brand-kit (public) + `public/brand-kit/` in repo | Visual assets if volunteer is doing any external-facing creation |

When granting access to the Google Sheets, do it **read-only** for the first two weeks. Promote to edit access once the volunteer has shown they understand the data and won't accidentally break a formula or overwrite a column. This isn't trust-test gating; it's just basic protection against well-intentioned beginner mistakes in a shared system.
