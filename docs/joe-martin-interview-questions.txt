Share Your Bitcoin Journey: Joe Martin — Interview Questions

For BFTA Podcast Series · YouTube · Recording date TBD (targeting late May 2026)
Show host: Dion Wilson · Founder, Executive Director, Interim Chair, Bitcoin for the Arts

Source material reviewed: joemartinmusic.com · his Nostr profile
(npub19r9qrxmckj2vyk5a5ttyt966s5qu06vmzyczuh97wj8wtyluktxqymeukr) · his
Shopstr marketplace listing · "Three Chords And The Truth" review of his
new album "Alone In..." (April 2026) · his publicly posted "no AI" album-
production statement · the gig + community engagement we can see in his
recent Nostr feed.

Note for the host: Joe is a singer-songwriter — a self-described
"21st century troubadour" based on the Isle of Man. His artistic identity
right now is built around a recent album ("Alone In...") that he
intentionally made with zero AI involvement, traveling half-way around
the world and spending most of his savings to do it. His public quote
about the project frames AI and inflation as twin forces "eating the
world." That parallel — between AI slop debasing art the way fiat
debases money — is the editorial spine the BFTA episode should hold
onto. The closing question (Q21) is sacred — every episode of Share
Your Bitcoin Journey ends with the BFTA micro-grant prompt.


BACKGROUND & JOURNEY

1. Joe, welcome to Share Your Bitcoin Journey. For anyone meeting you
   here for the first time — you're a singer-songwriter based on the
   Isle of Man, you call yourself a 21st century troubadour, you just
   released an album called "Alone In..." that's getting some genuinely
   warm reviews, and you sell merch for sats. That's already a
   distinctive picture. Where does the story actually start for you?
   When did you first know you were going to spend your life writing
   songs?

2. The phrase "21st century troubadour" is doing a lot of work on
   your profile. Troubadours were medieval poet-musicians traveling
   court to court — independent operators, beholden to no label, paid
   by patrons who valued the song itself. You've put yourself in that
   lineage on purpose. Tell us what that identity means to you and
   why you chose those exact words.

3. Your bio says simply, "It's all about the song." In a music
   industry built around branding, image, social-platform optimization,
   and algorithmic exposure — that's almost a counter-cultural
   statement. What does "it's all about the song" actually mean in
   practice when you sit down to write?

4. The Isle of Man is a fascinating place to be based as a songwriter.
   Small island in the Irish Sea, ~85,000 people, its own folk-music
   tradition, geographically and culturally distinct from both Britain
   and Ireland. How did you end up there, and what does being on the
   island do to your work that being on the mainland couldn't?

5. There's a long tradition of musicians who do their most serious
   work in places that are away from the industry — far from London,
   far from Nashville, far from LA. You're as far from the music
   industry as a British-isles songwriter can get without leaving the
   British Isles entirely. Is that deliberate, or just where life
   took you?


THE BITCOIN TURN

6. You sell merch for sats. You're active on Nostr. You're part of
   the value-for-value music movement. How did you first encounter
   Bitcoin, and when did it cross from "interesting tech idea" to
   "this is going to be how I do my work as an artist"?

7. Most working musicians today operate inside platforms — Spotify,
   Apple Music, the label model, the streaming-revenue treadmill.
   You've stepped substantially outside that. What did you give up
   when you made that move, and what did you gain that the platform
   musicians don't have?

8. The value-for-value model — for a listener who hasn't encountered
   it — describe what it actually means for an independent musician
   day to day. How does it change how you decide what songs to
   release, what gigs to play, what your relationship with your
   audience is?


"ALONE IN..." — THE ALBUM AND THE ARGUMENT

9. Let's talk about the new album, "Alone In...". You published a
   statement that I think is one of the most striking pieces of
   artistic positioning I've read this year. You wrote:
   "I intentionally made my album without any AI. I wanted to make
   one last thing that was truly pure and human before AI slop and
   inflation ate the world and will make it impractical / impossible.
   That meant me traveling half-way around the world and spending
   most of my savings on making something I could truly say I was
   proud of. It was worth it."
   Walk us through that decision. When did you realize the album had
   to be made this way, and what was the moment you committed to it
   even knowing the cost?

10. The parallel you draw in that statement — between AI slop and
    inflation, both "eating the world" — is exactly the framing
    Bitcoin for the Arts is trying to surface in our own research:
    that fiat debasement and AI-generated cultural saturation are
    parallel debasements of value. One eats your money, one eats
    your art. Was that parallel something you arrived at slowly, or
    did it click for you all at once?

11. You said you traveled half-way around the world and spent most
    of your savings on the album. The traveling and the savings part
    — that's low time preference made literal. The most "Bitcoin"
    thing about the album may not be how you sell it; it may be the
    900-day commitment you made to creating it without shortcuts.
    Talk to us about what that travel and that spending actually
    looked like.

12. The Three Chords And The Truth review of the album wrote that
    you've "sharpened your writing tools, expanded the horizon,
    [and] revel in fertile pastures where good music grows." That's
    a critic describing artistic maturation. Whatever happened during
    the making of "Alone In..." — does it feel, to you, like a
    permanent change in your work? Or just one record where you
    pushed in a particular direction?

13. There's a deeper question underneath all of this. If your
    forecast about AI is right — that authentic, fully-human albums
    will become impractical or impossible within a few years — then
    "Alone In..." is something close to a final document. A
    deliberate artifact made for a moment that's about to end. Did
    you feel that weight while you were making it, and what do you
    hope this album becomes ten years from now, when its rarity is
    obvious?


THE ISLE OF MAN AS CREATIVE LOCUS

14. The Isle of Man has a particular history — long Celtic folk
    tradition, deep ties to TT racing, a status as a self-governing
    Crown Dependency that's economically more nimble than mainland
    UK on things like financial regulation. There's a small but
    serious Bitcoin community there. What's it actually like being a
    songwriter inside that culture?

15. You play gigs locally — Douglas, the harbour scene, the venues
    on the island. You've also said "no gig too big or too small, just
    happy to play my songs to anyone wanting to listen." For a
    musician with a serious album out and Nostr-side recognition from
    artists across the world, that's a pretty unusual posture. Where
    does that come from?

16. When you imagine your audience — the people who are actually
    going to listen to "Alone In..." over the next decade — who do
    you see? Who is this album for?


THE WORLDVIEW: INFLATION, AI, AUTHENTICITY

17. You're one of a small but growing community of musicians who see
    Bitcoin not as a side investment but as the foundation underneath
    a different kind of artistic career — one that doesn't depend on
    the platforms, the labels, the streaming economics, or the AI
    flooding. If a young songwriter came to you this year and said,
    "I want to build my career on those new rules, what should I
    actually do" — what would you tell them?

18. The AI-and-inflation parallel keeps coming back. Both are forms
    of debasement: one of currency, one of culture. Both quietly
    erode the value of work that was honest and hard-earned. Bitcoin
    answers the first. What — if anything — answers the second?
    Where does the cultural defense come from?

19. There's a real question whether AI-made music can ever carry the
    same weight as music made by a person who lived something and
    wrote it down. Some people would say no, never; some would say
    yes, eventually. You've staked a career-shaping decision on the
    "never" side. Make the case for that, plainly, for any listener
    who hasn't made up their mind.


LOOKING FORWARD

20. What's next for you in the back half of 2026 and into 2027 —
    touring "Alone In...," writing a follow-up, more gigs around the
    Bitcoin and Nostr conference circuit? Where are you pointed?


THE CLOSING QUESTION

21. Last question — we ask every guest this on Share Your Bitcoin
    Journey: if a $500 to $2,000 Bitcoin micro-grant from Bitcoin for
    the Arts landed in your wallet tomorrow, what would it unlock
    for you as a creator?

NOTES ON ORDER

  - Q1-5 are biographical warmup. The Isle of Man and troubadour
    questions are fun and humanizing — set the personable tone
    before the conversation gets philosophical. Spend ~10 minutes
    max on this section.

  - Q6-8 are the Bitcoin turn. Q8 is where Joe should describe value-
    for-value in his own words — his articulation as a working
    musician will land cleaner than a host-side explanation.

  - Q9-13 are the heart of the episode. The "no AI" statement is the
    quotable, sharable, share-worthy core of why this conversation
    matters. Don't rush. Let him reflect on Q13 especially — the
    "ten years from now" framing often produces the most resonant
    moments because it pulls the artist out of promotional mode and
    into legacy mode.

  - Q14-16 are the geography section. Lighter, but Q16 (who is the
    album for) often opens up emotionally — listen for the audience
    he sees in his mind, that's where his real creative compass is.

  - Q17-19 are the philosophical pivot — twin debasements of money
    and culture. This section is where the episode earns its place
    in the BFTA series specifically. Don't rush.

  - Q20-21 are the wrap. Q21 is sacred — every episode ends with it.

If running over 60 minutes, the questions to consider cutting are Q5
(deliberate-distance-from-industry — may emerge organically in Q4),
Q15 (no-gig-too-big-or-small — color, not central), and Q19 (the AI-
or-never debate — interesting but standalone-able). Q9-13 and Q21
should never be cut.
