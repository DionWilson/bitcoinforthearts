## Slide 14 — Every Era Has a Monetary Culture

Money is not neutral wallpaper.
It shapes:
- Who gets to create full-time
- Who owns the work
- Who distributes it
- What lasts

When the monetary operating system changes, culture's power structure changes with it.

---

## Slide 15 — Arts Under the Industrial Age

Industrial-age arts inherited:
- Mass production and mass media
- Central publishers, labels, galleries, studios
- Gatekept discovery
- Wage-and-salary logic applied to irregular creative labor
- Fiat money: expandable supply, institutional middle layers

Breakthroughs were real (recording, film, touring circuits).
So was dependence on institutions that could inflate, censor, or cut.

---

## Slide 16 — The Artist as Industrial Input

In the industrial frame, the artist is often:
- Talent to be contracted
- Content to be scheduled
- IP to be owned upstream
- A cost center when recession hits

Patronage existed, but it was scarce and relationship-gated.
Most creators were squeezed between passion and payroll.

---

## Slide 17 — The Hinge We Are Living Through

You are of age at a hinge:
- End of a late-industrial media order
- Rise of networked creation, AI tools, global audiences
- Collapse of trust in legacy institutions and currencies
- Search for meaning, ownership, and durable value

This is not just "new apps."
It is a civilizational software update.

---

## Slide 18 — Arts Under the Information Age

Information-age arts look like:
- Creation and distribution that can be global on day one
- Direct audience relationships (and direct payment rails)
- Digital archives and infinite copies
- New scarcity questions: attention, authenticity, provenance
- A chance to pair **digitized culture** with **digitized sound money**

Preservation alone is not enough.
We also need settlement that cannot quietly melt.

---

## Slide 19 — Digitization Without Sound Money

Recent signal: major institutions pouring millions into digitizing performing-arts archives.

That work matters.
It moves culture into the information age.

But digitized art on melting money still leaves creators fragile.
**Access without sovereignty is incomplete.**

The invitation of this decade:
digital preservation **and** digital sound money.

---

## Slide 20 — Sound Money, Said Simply

Sound money (working definition for tonight):
- Hard to create endlessly
- Easy to verify
- Able to travel without permission theater
- Able to store creative time across years

Bitcoin is the leading open-network candidate for that role in the digital era.

You do not need to agree with every Bitcoiner take.
You only need the economic intuition: **artists need money that keeps promises.**

## Slide 23 — Sovereignty for Artists (A Working Definition)

Sovereignty is not isolation.
It is **agency**:

- Custody of value
- Say over rights and releases
- Ability to accept support without a gatekeeper's permission
- Continuity when platforms change the rules

Bitcoin does not replace craft.
It changes the settlement layer under craft.

---

## Slide 24 — The Tool Stack of the Next Frontier

What unlocks a new level of creativity and ownership:
1. **Sound money** (Bitcoin / Lightning) for pay, grants, savings
2. **Direct channels** (email, Nostr, podcasts, community rooms)
3. **Portable identity and reputation** across platforms
4. **Digital archives** with clear provenance
5. **Communities** that fund what they want to exist (patronage, not only ads)

Gen Z and millennials can be the first native generation of this stack.

---

## Slide 25 — Creativity That Preserves Value Differently

When artists have more control:
- Work can be priced in time that does not evaporate as fast
- Small patrons worldwide can matter more than one grant officer
- Experiments can ship without waiting for institutional permission
- Archives can be funded as living culture, not only as museums

Value preservation becomes cultural **and** monetary.

---

## Slide 26 — Leadership Is Not Waiting for Permission

The old path: wait to be chosen.
The frontier path: build the room, then invite peers in.

You do not need to become a "crypto person."
You need to become literate in the money that will sit under the next fifty years of culture.

Leaders of the next frontier will be:
curious · sovereign · collaborative · hard to capture
