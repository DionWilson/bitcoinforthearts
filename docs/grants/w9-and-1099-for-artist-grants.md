# W-9 / 1099 for BFTA artist micro-grants

**Org:** Bitcoin for the Arts, Inc. · EIN 41-2642260  
**Not legal or tax advice.** Confirm classification with BFTA’s CPA.

## Short answer

For individual artist micro-grants at **$2,000 or more** in a calendar year (2026 threshold), **collect Form W-9 before disbursement**. It is not optional for Ainsley’s **$2,500** award.

## Why

IRS Instructions for Forms 1099-MISC and 1099-NEC (2026) say nonprofits file Form **1099-MISC** for payments of at least **$2,000** that include:

- prizes and awards, including certain non-government grants (Box 3); and  
- payments to individuals to achieve a specific objective, produce a report/product, or improve artistic/musical skill or talent (Box 3).

BFTA micro-grants with project deliverables and post-award reporting fit that description. W-9 is how BFTA gets the recipient’s legal name and TIN for the 1099.

Without a TIN on a reportable payment, backup withholding rules can apply.

## Grant vs contractor (do not mix)

| Relationship | Typical form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Grant** (award for charitable/educational project; not hire for services) | **1099-MISC** Box 3 | Ainsley Costello micro-grant |
| **Independent contractor** (paid for services) | **1099-NEC** | Steven / sound production fee |

Both paths still need a **W-9** when the annual amount meets the reporting threshold ($2,000 for 2026 for these categories).

## Ops checklist (grantee)

1. Signed grant award agreement  
2. Completed Form **W-9** on file  
3. Bitcoin / Lightning receiving address in writing  
4. Disburse BTC; record USD FMV + txid on transfer date  
5. Year-end: CPA files **1099-MISC** (unless they reclassify)
